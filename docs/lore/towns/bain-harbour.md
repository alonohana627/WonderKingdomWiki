# Bain Harbour

*The Coastal Town*

Bain Harbour is a rich and sunlit port on the east coast of Veligor, a proud merchant town of the sea that governs itself through its harbour council. Canals run in from the harbour between houses of pale stone and terracotta, merchants trade under the arcades of Merchant Street, and on the square before the Harbour Hall it is never quiet. It still feels a little like Selsior and a little like Kaldemor, with the capital's fine stone and the countryside's warmth, but its pride is its own: ships, trade and the sea.

## History

### The Fishermen's Cove

Bain Harbour began as a cove of Commons fishing families, who found that the deep, sheltered bay was the one place on that coast where boats could ride out a storm. Through the Age of Division, the cove stayed small and kept its head down, selling fish to whichever side came to buy and trying hard to stay out of the wars.

### The Shipwrights' Century

After the founding, peace changed everything. With the island united, goods needed to move from one end of Veligor to the other, and the sea was often the fastest road. The fishing families took up shipbuilding, and the cove's sheltered water became the island's busiest shipyard. Merchants followed the ships, craftsmen followed the merchants, and the cove grew into a town.

The shipwrights dug as well as built. To float timber and cargo to the yards and warehouses they cut canals in from the harbour, and the town grew up along them, its streets following the water. Their greatest work was **the Old Cut**, a canal across the neck of the headland to the open sea beyond it, so that ships could leave for the north without rounding the point in bad weather. By the century's end the fishermen's cove had become a town of trading houses, and the money that came in by sea was spent on stone.

The town takes its name from **Old Bain**, a fisherwoman remembered as the first to build a ship big enough to sail around the island. Her boat-hook still hangs above the door of the harbour hall.

### A Town of Its Own

As it grew, both Selsior and Kaldemor did a great deal of business through Bain Harbour, and more than once someone in the capital suggested that the port ought to answer to the crown's own officials. The people of Bain Harbour politely refused every time. The harbour is run by its own **harbour council**, made up of its captains, shipwrights and merchants, and the town is proud that no one else has ever had a say in its affairs.

## Environment and Identity

Bain Harbour is a town built on its water. Canals run in from the harbour through every quarter, crossed by humped stone bridges and lined with water doors where boats unload straight into the warehouses. Its houses are of pale stone under terracotta roofs, washed in the soft colours of sand, shell and sunset, with flowers in their window boxes and washing strung between them over the water. The sun is almost always on it.

Its heart is the square before the **Harbour Hall**, the grandest building on the coast, with a colonnade along its front, a bell tower over it and broad steps down to the square, where the council's business spills out among the market awnings. From the square, **Merchant Street** runs along the town's main canal under shaded arcades, lined with trading houses, counting rooms, spice and cloth merchants, and the halls of the trades. Below them lie the **Docks** and their warehouses, the **Shipyard** with its slipways and timber stacks, and the **Waterfront** of quays, inns and sailors' houses. The **Fish Market** stands where the old cove was, by the fishermen's steps, the oldest and plainest corner of the town, and Bain Harbour never forgets that it began there. On the headland to the north the **Lighthouse** watches over it all.

It is a town that loves to trade, to bargain, to show off a little and to make visitors welcome. Its merchants are proud of their ledgers and its captains of their ships, and it smells of tar, salt, spice and fresh bread.

Since the Age of Disturbance began, the sea around Veligor has grown rougher and stranger, and Bain Harbour's sailors tell of creatures in the deep water that were never there before. The harbour's ships still sail, but its captains now hire adventurers to sail with them.

## Independence

Bain Harbour is **not Selsior's port, nor Kaldemor's**. It is a town in its own right, with its own history, people and identity. What it shares with those towns is a feeling, not a chain of command, and it belongs to Wonder Kingdom on equal terms with every other settlement.

It lies on the east coast, on the opposite side of the island from [Goa Harbour](goa-harbour.md) on the west. The two ports are very different, but they are friends, not rivals, and they hold a friendly boat race between them every summer that goes all the way around Veligor.

## The Lands Around Bain Harbour

Two roads come down to Bain Harbour and one more comes in beside them: the royal road from Selsior over the eastern downs, the Southern Coastal Road along the island's south shore from Goa Harbour, and the Verdant Coast Road, which comes down from Kaldemor's countryside and reaches the town by the Strand, the long beach at its south end. None of them is as safe as it was, but all are travelled every day, and the real change is not on the roads at all. It is in the water: along the Old Cut, where the open sea now comes in, and under the headland, where the sea has turned strange in recent years.

The headland closes the bay on the north side, with the lighthouse on its point. Below the lighthouse lies **the Clatterstones**, a beach of great round boulders where harbour children have always caught crabs in the rock pools. Beyond the harbour wall, running out from the headland's foot, is **the Long Reef**, the reef that the pearl diver [Nerissa](../people/bain-harbour/nerissa.md) and her grandmother before her have dived all their lives. Below and beyond the reef a wide underwater shelf runs north under the headland, and at its edge, **the Drop**, the sea floor falls away into water that no diver has ever seen the bottom of.

### The royal road from Selsior

From the capital's great gate the road to Bain Harbour soon parts from the Royal Road and bears east, onto **the Chalk Downs**, a long country of short grass and white tracks where the wind first begins to smell of salt. On the downs' eastern edge the road runs through copses of holm oak and cork, and scrub wolves, lean and grey, have come out of the thickets to follow travellers and snatch at anything left on a cart. Drovers and carters still use the road every day, but they keep together.

The last stretch is **Baysight Hill**, where the road comes out of the trees onto a sunny bluff and the whole bay of Bain Harbour opens below: the lighthouse on its headland, the masts in the harbour, the terracotta roofs and the shining lines of the canals. Travellers stop there to look. The bluff's wildcats, which have always raided the gulls' nests on its ledges, have grown big and bold enough to raid travellers instead, and they will leap on anyone who stops long enough to unwrap a meal. From the foot of the hill the road runs down past the shipyard into town, and a lane runs north along the back of the town to the first lock of the Old Cut.

### The Southern Coastal Road

Travellers for Goa Harbour follow the Strand south to **Shellbrook Mouth**, where the Verdant Coast Road comes down over the dunes from Kaldemor, and there the Southern Coastal Road begins. It carries on south and then west along the island's south shore, all the way to Goa Harbour without passing the capital. It is the longest road out of Bain Harbour and the emptiest, used by drovers, pedlars, fishing families going between the coves, and every summer by people walking out to watch the race boats go by.

Its first stretch is **the Dune Reach**, low sunny dunes and marram grass along the shore, where the dune cats that once lived on the fishermen's leavings have grown bold and quarrelsome and follow walkers for miles. Where the coast turns west, the road comes down to **Marram Cove**, a fishing hamlet of a dozen houses in a sheltered bend of the shore, where travellers rest, fill their water bottles and hear the news from both ends of the road. The cove has no monsters and wants none. Beyond it the road comes out onto **the Long Sands**, mile after mile of pale beach and dunes, where big grey tomcats hunt the tide line and do not care whether what they are hunting is a crab or a traveller's boot. At the western end of the Long Sands the cliffs rise again, and the road reaches the foot of the Gullwalk, the clifftop path that carries it the last way into Goa Harbour.

### The Old Cut

The Old Cut leaves the harbour's north quays and runs between stone banks and towpaths across the neck of the headland to the open sea. In the shipwrights' century it was the busiest water in the town, lined with timber ponds, lock basins and boathouses, but the great ships of today are too broad for its locks, and for a generation it has carried little more than barges and children's boats. A few winters ago a storm broke its sea gates, and since then the tide has run in and out of the Cut, and with it, from the deep water, has come the sea.

**The Inner Cut** is the harbour end, a long reach of locks, towpaths and quiet warehouses with their water doors shut. In recent years crabs have come up it from the shore and grown. The largest are big enough to take a boot off, and the dockworkers call them bootcrabs. Iron-backed crabs as wide as a cartwheel sit on the lock steps, glass jellies drift in the basins with stings no healer in the harbour can name, and pale-snouted snapjaws haul themselves out onto the towpaths. Where the Inner Cut ends lies **the Pinch**, the narrowest and deepest lock on the canal, whose gates rotted away long ago, so that it fills and empties with every tide.

**The crab hunt.** It begins with [Bree](../people/bain-harbour/bree.md) of the Fish Market, who has found a crab in her rock pools that is not like any crab she has ever named. [Keziah](../people/bain-harbour/keziah.md) and [Tristram](../people/bain-harbour/tristram.md), who cleared the rocks below the headland in Keziah's first week in the harbour, have found the bootcrabs back, and bigger, in the locks of the Inner Cut, and they want to know where they keep coming from. [Clemence](../people/bain-harbour/clemence.md) the healer wants the glass jellies brought to her, so that she can make a salve for stings she has so far only been able to write down. Then [Kit](../people/bain-harbour/kit.md), the shipwright's son, goes missing along the Cut, because he wanted to see one of the sailors' sea creatures for himself. He is found on a lock gate, cut off when the tide came in and filled the lock, surrounded by crabs, and once he is home he will talk about nothing but the enormous shell he saw moving in the Pinch.

**The Bootbreaker.** In the Pinch lives the crab the others come from: a Disturbance-touched shore crab the size of a fishing boat, its shell scarred and crusted with barnacles, which has been casting off its old shells in the lock and growing larger with each one. Keziah named it the Bootbreaker. Bree has her own name for it and will tell anyone who asks.

**The Outer Cut** runs on beyond the Pinch to the sea end, where the canal comes out at the foot of the headland, between the last boulders of the Clatterstones and the root of the Long Reef. Here the water is salt and deep, and a new kind of fish has come in from the deep water: the **sawbill**, long and narrow, with a flat bill edged on both sides with teeth like a saw. Sawbills hunt along the Cut in shoals, and they are bold enough now to haul themselves out onto the towpaths and the lock steps after anything that moves.

**The torn nets.** Twice this year [Cobb](../people/bain-harbour/cobb.md)'s nets came up torn clean through in the deeper water just inside the headland, in long straight rents whose edges were pulled rather than cut. His mother [Wenna](../people/bain-harbour/wenna.md) kept a strip of the torn net, and the story begins when she lets someone carry it round the harbour. It matches the long straight tears that [Tegan](../people/bain-harbour/tegan.md) the sailmaker has been patching in sails that came back from deep water, and the grooved driftwood on [Maren](../people/bain-harbour/maren.md)'s shelf of beach finds. Cobb, who has not been past the middle of the bay since, goes out along the Outer Cut to see what tears his nets. Nerissa, whose reef lies just outside the sea gates, teaches the water of the Cut's sea end to anyone willing to learn it properly. On a night voyage of [Seraphine](../people/bain-harbour/captain_seraphine.md)'s Bright Morrow, sawbills come leaping out of the dark across the deck, and that night it is plain how a sail gets a long straight tear in it. Finally [Alwyn](../people/bain-harbour/alwyn.md) the shipwright wants a sawbill's bill and a scored plank laid on [Dunstan](../people/bain-harbour/dunstan.md)'s workbench, as proof the old man cannot argue with that the deep water calls for stronger hulls.

**The Ripsaw.** The shoal that works the Cut follows one fish: a deeply touched sawbill longer than a fishing boat, its saw scarred from end to end, which lies up in **the Gullet**, the narrow throat of the canal under the broken sea gates, where the tide runs hardest. When it moves, the whole shoal moves with it.

### The Holm Wood

From the Outer Cut's sea end the old timber path climbs onto the broad back of the headland, behind the lighthouse, where **the Holm Wood** grows: holm oaks and sea pines, dark and evergreen, where the shipwrights felled the timber they floated down the Cut. Nobody has felled it for a long time. Dusk wolves hunt in it now, and since the deep water turned strange, something stranger walks there after dark: tall shapes like hooded figures, which are not people at all, only shadow and salt mist with a pale light where a face should be. The harbour calls them lanternshades. Some carry long lashes of shadow, some hooked blades, and some a pale glim that draws the others to it, and they fade at dawn.

At the heart of the wood the roof of a great sea cave fell in long ago and left **the Lantern Deep**, a round, sheer-sided pool of sea water open to the sky, joined under the cliffs to the Drop itself. Its water rises and falls with the tide and never shows a bottom.

**The lights in the deep water.** Almost everyone in the harbour has seen one piece of this, and almost nobody has told anyone. The story begins with [Halloran](../people/bain-harbour/halloran.md), the captain of the Watch, who has stood a watch on the outer pier every night since he found the Watch's launch hanging from a mooring line pulled straight down into the water, and long fresh grooves cut into the pilings below the waterline. Standing a night on the pier with him, and climbing down among the pilings at the lowest tide to look at them, shows the grooves for what they are: rows of round marks, each ringed with fine scratches. Halloran then does what he knows he should have done long ago, and brings together the people who have been writing down what the sea is doing: [Brisa](../people/bain-harbour/brisa.md) with her list, [Delphine](../people/bain-harbour/delphine.md) with her ledger of sailors' tales, Clemence with her casebook and its row of coin-sized round welts on a deckhand's forearm, and [Ottilie](../people/bain-harbour/ottilie.md) with her tally of the small boats that no longer go out past the headland.

[Ysolde](../people/bain-harbour/ysolde.md), the Keeper of the Light, needs someone to climb the hundred and seventeen stairs and keep the lamp on a night her knees will not manage it. That is the night the pale lights come back, far out past the headland, and her grandson [Ossie](../people/bain-harbour/ossie.md) turns out to have logged the same lights in his blue logbook. Ysolde shows the private signs in the margin of the lighthouse log at last. With the Harbour Master's leave, [Ambrose](../people/bain-harbour/ambrose_chartmaker.md) unlocks the chart he keeps in his drawer, with his son [Jowan](../people/bain-harbour/jowan.md) watching, and its marks are checked one by one from the cliffs at the Holm Wood's seaward edge, over the shelf where [Annis](../people/bain-harbour/captain_annis.md)'s Saltlark felt something brush her keel and out over the Drop. The marks have been creeping towards the headland for years, and laid beside Ysolde's lights they show the same thing coming closer, until the newest lie under the headland itself. Nerissa, given back a pearl from the ledge she was reaching for, finally tells [Tristram](../people/bain-harbour/tristram.md) what she saw below the shelf, and Tristram takes his father's harpoon to the edge of the Lantern Deep at night, not to kill what is there but to see it clearly, once.

**The Deep Lantern.** What the harbour has been seeing in pieces is one creature and the shoals that hunt around it: a deeply touched squid as long as a ship, with eyes as large as cartwheels, arms whose rows of hooked suckers score wood like a chisel, and a body lit along its length with the same pale lights Ysolde has watched for three summers. It came up out of water too deep for anyone to know, and each summer it has followed its shoals a little closer to the coast, until it found the way in under the headland. Now it rises at night in the Lantern Deep, its light comes up through the trees, and the lanternshades walk wherever that light falls. The harbour, which named it after the lighthouse that saw it first, calls it the Deep Lantern.

### The Wreck of the Saltgrace

Under the harbour's south cliff, below the Waterfront, the sea has hollowed out a run of caves that boom like a bell at every high tide. Harbour children have always dared each other into the mouth of **the Bellcaves**, and salvagers have always known what lies further in: the wreck of the **Saltgrace**, a great trading ship of the harbour's early days, driven into the caves in a storm long ago and wedged there ever since, her hull broken open on the rocks and her decks tilted into the dark water.

For years old [Kenwyn](../people/bain-harbour/kenwyn.md) worked the wreck for iron and seasoned timber, going in at low tide by the salvagers' steps from the beach. This spring he found it full: crabs crusted with barnacles nesting in the holds, eels in the bilges, things moving behind the portholes. He stopped going in, and now he sits at the top of the steps telling anyone who will listen that he knows every plank of that ship and will draw them a map of her for nothing.

The Saltgrace has other ties to the harbour. Burned into her timbers is the same stamp that marks the four dark iron-bound crates at the back of [Hobart](../people/bain-harbour/hobart.md)'s oldest warehouse, which nobody has opened in living memory. Somewhere in her purser's strongbox, wrapped in oilskin, is her bill of lading, still legible, listing four crates landed at Bain before she sailed her last. And carved into a beam of her forecastle by some long-dead sailor are the words of an old sea song, with a last verse that [Wilmot](../people/bain-harbour/wilmot.md) the ropemaker would know at once.

**The Holdfast.** The Saltgrace's stern is broken open into the deepest cave, and her lowest hold is flooded with its water. In it, coiled through the broken decks and up under the floor of the captain's cabin, lives a Disturbance-touched conger eel so vast that its body runs through three decks at once. It strikes out of hatches, holes and stern windows all around anyone who comes into the cabin. Salvagers have long said there was something in the Saltgrace that did not like being disturbed, and Kenwyn calls it the Holdfast, because it has never once let go of the ship.

The creatures of the Bellcaves are strange even by the Disturbance's standards: however strong the adventurer who goes in, what waits in the wreck seems to have grown to match them.

## Leadership Today

[Quenna](../people/bain-harbour/harbour_master_quenna.md) has been Harbour Master for eight years. She went to sea as a deck girl at twelve and was master of her own ship, the Kittiwake, by twenty-seven. The harbour council of captains, shipwrights and merchants chose her from among themselves, and most of them say it was the easiest choice they ever made and the noisiest they have lived with since. She chairs the council from the Harbour Hall and has skippered the town's race boat for fourteen summers.

!!! abstract "Design notes"
    New with this page: the fishing cove origin, Old Bain, the harbour council, the summer race around Veligor, and sea creatures stirred up by the Disturbance. Bain Harbour is on the east coast, as the game's world map has it.

    Bain Harbour's look is a sunlit merchant republic of the sea: canals and bridges cut in from the harbour, pale stone and terracotta, arcaded merchant streets, and the Harbour Hall as a council palace on its square. Fishing is the town's humble origin and trade its pride. Real-world inspirations: the maritime republics of Venice and Genoa, and MapleStory's San Commerci and the Commerci Republic. The canals and the Old Cut are new with this look, and so are the town's districts as described above; the people, their places and the history are unchanged.

    New with "The Lands Around Bain Harbour": the Chalk Downs and Baysight Hill on the royal road from Selsior; the Dune Reach, Marram Cove and the Long Sands on the Southern Coastal Road; Shellbrook Mouth and the Strand, where the Verdant Coast Road comes in; the Clatterstones, the Long Reef, the shelf and the Drop as the sea around the headland; the Old Cut with the Inner Cut, the Pinch, the Outer Cut and the Gullet; the Holm Wood and the Lantern Deep; the Bellcaves and the Saltgrace, Kenwyn, the lanternshades, and four creatures: the Bootbreaker, the Ripsaw, the Deep Lantern and the Holdfast. The bootcrabs answer the crabs "big enough to take a boot"; the sawbills answer the long straight tears in nets, sails and driftwood; the Deep Lantern answers Ysolde's pale lights, Barnaby's eyes, the brushed keel, the grooves in the pilings, the welts in Clemence's casebook and the marks on Ambrose's chart. The lanternshades are creatures of shadow and mist thrown by the Deep Lantern's light, never people. What happened to last autumn's missing Kaldemor cart and its drivers is still open, and so is what is in Hobart's four crates. The Deep Lantern is a creature of the Disturbance, not a sign of the Balrog's intentions. Real-world inspirations: sawfish and swordfish for the sawbills, deep-sea squid (whose sucker rings carry small teeth) and bioluminescent shoals for the Deep Lantern, conger eels in wrecks for the Holdfast, ship canals across headlands and the collapsed sea caves of rocky Atlantic coasts for the Old Cut and the Lantern Deep. Inside the Bellcaves, the monsters' strength follows the player's own, which is why the wreck's creatures "grow to match" whoever goes in.
