# Midbarium

*The Highland Settlement*

Midbarium is a rugged, rural mountain town and the ancestral home of the Savages' culture. It sits high in Veligor's dry uplands, among red-rock peaks, sheer cliffs, deep canyons and steep highland trails, and its people grew strong learning to live with them.

## The Land

The highlands around Midbarium are a hard, beautiful country. Rain is scarce, the sun is fierce, and the wind carries red dust down the canyons for days at a time. Above the town rise wind-carved mesas and bare stone spires; below it, dry riverbeds wind through rocky valleys dotted with caves, hardy scrub and the odd twisted tree. Nights are cold and clear, and the stars over the plateau are famous across the kingdom.

Life here follows the land. There are no grand fields like Kaldemor's. Instead, families farm small stone terraces on the slopes, watered by springs and carefully kept cisterns, and they drive herds of goats and other hardy beasts between the high pastures and the valley floors as the seasons turn. Many Midbarians live not in the town itself but in scattered homesteads and camps across the highlands, coming in to trade, to settle disputes and to celebrate.

## Architecture

Midbarium is built into the mountainside. Its houses are thick-walled and earthen-red, made of stone and sun-dried clay that keep out the heat of the day and hold warmth through the night. Heavy timber beams carry the roofs of the great halls, and terraces, carved stairways and swaying rope-and-plank bridges join the levels of the town across the cliffs.

Out in the highlands, herding families live in sturdy hide tents and stone shelters that can be moved or rebuilt with the seasons. Painted standards and carved totems mark the camps of each family.

## The Four Families

Midbarium is not governed like Selsior. Its people belong to four great **families**, clans bound by blood, oath and shared history, and each family follows a **chieftain**.

### The Kragvar

*The stone-cutters.* The Kragvar built Midbarium. Their ancestors cut the first terraces, raised the great hall and carved the stairways that climb the cliffs, and Kragvar masons still keep every wall and bridge in the town standing. In the Age of Division they were the hardest of the Savage warriors, and in the Balrog War they stood in the front line. Kragvar songs claim that some of their greatest fighters never came home from that war, but stayed beneath the World Tree to keep watch. The family keeps the council fire in the great hall burning, and counts that as its proudest duty.

### The Tolmach

*The herders.* The Tolmach are the wanderers of the highlands. Most of the family still lives in hide tents and moves with its herds between the high pastures and the valley floors, as it has for longer than anyone remembers. They know every old path through the canyons and every spring that runs in a dry year, and that knowledge kept the Savages alive through the long wars. After the founding, it was Tolmach guides who first led traders from Kaldemor and Selsior up into the mountains, and they still bring most of the town's wool, hides and cheese down to market.

### The Uskari

*The hunters.* The Uskari live along the canyon rims, and they are the best trackers, climbers and scouts in Veligor. They once hunted the great beasts of the plateau, and in the old wars they were the eyes of the Savage armies. Since the Age of Disturbance began, the Uskari have taken on a new duty: they patrol the highland trails, drive off the monsters that come up from the canyons, and see travelers safely from one camp to the next. Many young Uskari leave home to become adventurers.

### The Brenna

*The keepers.* The Brenna are Midbarium's healers, storytellers and drummers. They know the highland herbs and how to set a broken bone, and they keep the memory of the families: the long songs that name every chieftain and every Old One back to the Age of Division, recited at every gathering. When the Savages and Magicians were allies, it was Brenna envoys who walked to the forests to talk with them, and the family still has old friends in Fairy Forest. The Brenna host every choosing, and their drums call the families together.

## The Chieftains and the Old One

A chieftain is not simply born to the role. When a chieftain dies, their family gathers around its fire and chooses a new one from among its own people, for strength, wisdom and fairness. A chieftain is expected to lead from the front: to walk the hardest trail first, to judge disputes fairly, to protect the herds and to look after the weakest members of the family.

Above the four chieftains stands one leader for all of Midbarium, whom everyone calls **the Old One**. The name is a title of respect, not a description: an Old One can be any age. The Old One is chosen the same way. When the Old One dies, the four families come together at the great hall, around the fire that is never allowed to go out, and choose the next. By custom, no family may hold the title twice in a row, so each Old One owes the role to all four families, not to their own.

The chieftains meet in council with the Old One to settle matters that touch the whole town. Debates are long and loud, but once the Old One closes the council, every family stands by its decision. Within Wonder Kingdom, the Old One speaks for Midbarium, and the families are as loyal to the kingdom as any lord in the capital.

## Culture and Atmosphere

Midbarium stands for strength, resilience, independence and respect for the ancestors. Its people admire anyone who overcomes hardship, and they guard the knowledge handed down by earlier generations: the old paths through the canyons, where water can be found in a dry year, and the stories of the four families.

Hospitality is sacred in the highlands, where a traveler turned away might not survive the night. Anyone who comes to a Midbarian fire in peace is given water, food and a place to sleep, no questions asked. Feasts, storytelling, contests of strength and long nights of drumming and song mark the families' gatherings.

For all its rugged looks, Midbarium is not hostile, primitive or grim, and it holds nothing against outsiders. Today it is a peaceful, welcoming town whose people are proud of both their ancestral traditions and their place in Wonder Kingdom.

## The Lands Around Midbarium

The town itself is safe, as every town in the kingdom is. The highlands around it are not. Since the Age of Disturbance began, the red-rock country has grown harder to walk every year, and the Uskari Trail Watch, stretched thinner each season, holds the trails between the camps. Above the town lie the high ridges and the plateau; below it, the canyons. The Uskari like to say that every monster in the highlands comes up from the canyons sooner or later.

### The Royal Road

The royal road from [Selsior](selsior.md) climbs northwest off the central plain into the canyons. Its lower stretch, the **Canyon Road**, follows a dry riverbed between red walls, past Tolmach cairns and over a rope-and-plank bridge across a dry wash. Its upper stretch is the **South Path**, the old Tolmach caravan path that switchbacks up the last canyon to the town, along ledges a goat can stand on and a trader would rather not. Halfway up it, in a rock cleft, is the **Greywater** spring, which has run for as long as anyone remembers.

Dust boggles, quick little things that whirl up out of the dry ground, pester travelers on both stretches, and woken stumps have taken to the ledges near the top. Corwell of the Trading Post pays to have the road cleared for his caravans, and Ennek, the Tolmach guide, teaches the narrowest ledges of the South Path to anyone willing to walk them behind him.

### The Northern Woodland Trail

The old trail to [Fairy Forest](fairy-forest.md) leaves the town along the **North Ridge**, a high red ridge with an Uskari watch post and a view of the far-off woods. It drops down into the **Edgewood**, where the red rock gives way to the first trees, and on through the **Young Woods** to the forest's edge. The Brenna envoys walked it in the days when the Savages and the Magicians were allies, and the Brenna chieftain's spring letters to the forest still travel along it. Woken stumps hold the ridge, and the woods have creatures of their own.

### The High Ridges

From the North Ridge a path climbs onto the **High Ridges**: **Windworn Hill**, the **Long Ridge** and the **Broken Ground**, bare rock and wind, where old twisted trees cling to the ledges and the nearest Tolmach high pastures lie. The trees have woken. Stumps of dead wood and living trunk now walk the ridges, swing at anything that passes and root where they please, and they have driven the herds off the nearer pastures. The oldest of them, a great split tree called **Old Gnarl**, has rooted itself on **Howling Peak**.

This is where the Old One's quiet request begins: walk the patrols, and tell him the truth about them. Skarra, captain of the Trail Watch, has red marks on the ridges she wants checked; Sennu of the Tolmach wants a safe route back to a high pasture; Yarrek wants to know what his three goats met when they strayed down a ridge gully; and Tavi, chieftain of the Uskari, walks the worst stretch personally and will take company.

At the far end of the Broken Ground, the **far canyon bridge** swings across the Far Canyon to the far slopes. Vosk, the master of bridges, keeps it sound.

### The Far Slopes

Across the bridge lie the **far slopes** beyond the canyons, where rimwort grows and the Uskari once hunted the great horned beasts of the plateau. Old dry-season fires have scorched much of it. Past the **Far Anchor**, the ledge where the bridge's ropes are tied into the rock, the land runs out over the **Scorched Slopes**, the **Boar Runs**, the **Ironback Flats** and the **Hot Rocks** to the **Ash Mesa**.

The animals here have gone wild in the way an old Brenna verse describes: boars and flint-hided hogs that charge anyone on sight, and on the cliff below the Far Anchor, **cliff gnawers**, big sharp-toothed canyon rodents that climb sheer rock, nest in chewed rope and leave bites that fester. They are what has been gnawing the bridge's ropes by night. Above them all, a great horned plateau beast the Uskari call **Grimhorn**, huge, ash-coated and with one horn chipped, raids the Tolmach herds and returns to the Ash Mesa.

The story of the far slopes belongs to the town: two boys with a secret lookout and a notched stick, a bitten mason, a night watch on the bridge with Vosk and his apprentice Brakka, rimwort for Lessa the bonesetter, and Bekk, the young Trail Watch scout who wants to track the raider and has been forbidden to go alone. The great drum that calls the four families needs a new hide, and only a great horned beast's will do.

### The Withered Reach

Beyond the far slopes lies the **Withered Reach**, where the Disturbance lies thicker than anywhere else in the highlands. Dry storms break over it, bringing wind and lightning but never rain; the grass is dead and the trees are swollen and grey. Through the **Dry Hills** and the **Weathered Rocks** the way leads to the **Old War Camp**, the ruin of a Savage camp from the Age of Division that once held the highlands' western edge, where the Trail Watch now keeps a post. In the **War Camp Ruins** the camp's carved war masks have been stirred into moving, and hounds of bone and red dust prowl the broken walls. Beyond them, stone hulks, rock that walks, hold the **Red Ravine**.

The springs of the Reach are dying one by one, and the Greywater on the South Path, which ran through the driest year in living memory, has gone dry. In a hollow beneath the Red Ravine stands the reason: a vast dead tree, the **Rootdrinker**, which has driven its roots down through the rock into the deep water that feeds the highland springs and is drinking them dry.

Ennek, Sennu and old Mabbu of the Tolmach are at the heart of this story, with Brakka's eye for stone and Zirra's need for staves. So is Imre the stargazer, who has noticed that the stars low toward the island's heart sometimes shimmer as if seen through heat, and whose dates, set beside the red marks on Skarra's map, fall just before the worst stirrings of the year. Nobody can say why. The story ends in the great hall, at a gathering called by a drum with a new hide, where Ahvi the song-keeper sings an old verse about a season when the beasts grew wild before the Burning, and the four families say aloud in council what each of them has been too proud to say alone.

### The Dustfall Cave

Below the Uskari Rim, on the Trail Watch's canyon trail, a deep cave opens in the canyon wall. Ketta of the Uskari sheltered in it from a dust storm on her way home from adventuring, and the Uskari now call it the **Dustfall Cave**. It is reached from the rim by the Trail Watch's rope-way, and it goes down a very long way: through the **Dustfall Mouth**, down the **Cliff Gallery**, along the **Cold Draught**, where a wind blows up from below and never stops, and into the frost-rimed **Chill Caves**. Cave drakes have filled it and are breeding there.

At the bottom, in a lake in the dark called the **Sunken Pool**, lives the great drake **Hollowmaw**, grey-blue, frost-rimed and larger than any beast Ketta met in three years on the roads of Veligor. It is what she saw, and what saw her. Hunters of every strength go down into the cave again and again, because the drakes never stop coming up.

## Leadership Today

The Old One is [Hask](../people/midbarium/old_one_hask.md), born an Uskari. He led the Uskari as their chieftain for nine years, and six years ago, after Old One Varra of the Brenna died in her sleep, the four families gathered in the great hall and chose him through a long night of argument, drumming and song. He gave up his chieftainship that night, and the Uskari chose a new chieftain before dawn. Varra had been Old One for twenty-five years, and the Old One before her was a Tolmach, so no family has held the title twice in a row.

!!! abstract "Design notes"
    The geography and look are inspired by MapleStory's Perion. Midbarium is rural and mountainous, ruled by the chieftains of four families and the Old One, with a dry highland and desert feel. It should draw on highland, canyon and steppe cultures, not on Middle Eastern or Arabian desert cultures. Still open: how a family decides between rivals when it chooses a chieftain.

    The lands around Midbarium draw on Perion's ridges, burnt lands, excavation ruins and drake caves. In the game, the roads hold levels 0 to 20; the High Ridges are the 20-30 band with Old Gnarl as its boss, the Far Slopes 30-50 with Grimhorn, and the Withered Reach 50-70 with the Rootdrinker; the Dustfall Cave is Midbarium's theme dungeon. Kept open on purpose: what makes Imre's stars shimmer; whether Grimhorn is the beast that killed Bekk's father; how Ketta got her scar; what Ahvi's verse means; and whether the bone hounds and moving masks of the war camp are anything more than the Disturbance working on what the old camp left behind.
