# Kaldemor

*The Town of Joyful Craftsmanship*

Kaldemor is a cheerful pastoral town set among fertile fields, rolling hills and green countryside, "the greenest town this side of the old forest", as its mayor likes to say. Its heart is **joyful craftsmanship**: its people love making things with their own hands, growing their own food and living well together. Just under three hundred people live there, not counting the snails.

## History

### One Seed

Every child in Kaldemor knows how the town began. Long ago, a traveller crossing the eastern meadows planted a single seed and slept beside it for the night. In the morning, the seed had become a tree taller than a tower.

People came from all over to see it, and some of them never left. They built their homes around the great tree, and then in it: the **great treehouse** behind the town square is the oldest building in Kaldemor. Nobody remembers the traveller's name, but the town still lives by the saying the story left behind: *every great town starts with one seed.*

Whether the seed came from the World Tree, as the Magicians of Fairy Forest suspect, nobody can say. But the soil around Kaldemor has been strangely generous ever since.

### Mushroom Houses and Windmills

That generous soil grows mushrooms big enough to live in, and Kaldemor's settlers did exactly that. The town's famous **mushroom houses** are real mushrooms, hollowed out, fitted with doors and windows, and painted in bright colors. They grow a little every spring, and their owners simply add another room.

The early settlers were mostly Commons: farmers, carpenters, potters and bakers. They planted the fields and orchards, raised a windmill farm in the park, and built workshops wherever there was room. When the Royal Road was laid from Selsior to the east, Kaldemor became the last stop before the eastern countryside, and its markets grew busy with travellers.

### The Clock Tower

The town's other landmark is the **clock tower** in the park. It was raised by the whole town together, a gift from every family and every workshop, and its bell has kept Kaldemor's time ever since. The terrace oven in the market was built the same year, by Rosa the baker's grandmother, and it has not gone cold since.

The clock stopped once, not long ago, when a Shroom from the woods carried off its great gear. The whole town held its breath until the gear came back and the bell rang again.

## The Town Today

Kaldemor is led by its **mayor**, chosen by the townspeople. Mayor Tobin has held the post for thirty years and knows every family by name. The town square sits in the middle, with the Market to the east for potions, weapons and bread, and the Park to the west with its clock tower and windmills.

Beyond the town lie the fields, hills and woods where young warriors learn their trade, and lately there has been plenty to learn on. Snails eat the gardens, Shrooms raid the woods, and something huge has settled in a hollow past the woods: a giant snail the townsfolk call **the Snail King**. The town's park ranger and drillmaster keep a close watch on the wilds, and they are always glad of help.

## Architecture and Environment

Kaldemor is a town of colorful wooden houses and cozy cottages, mushroom houses and treehouses, workshops and gardens, barns and welcoming village greens. Beyond it stretch meadows, farmland, orchards, gentle hills and patches of woodland. It is a sunny, colorful place that seems to invite visitors in.

## Culture and Atmosphere

The people of Kaldemor value creativity, practical know-how, friendliness, cooperation and the quiet satisfaction of work done well. Traditional crafts are part of everyday life: woodworking, carpentry, pottery, baking and farming all shape who the town is.

Kaldemor is no poor backwater. It is a prosperous, happy town whose people love the life they have made. Where Selsior stands for the excitement and variety of the great city, Kaldemor stands for the joy of creating, growing and belonging to a close-knit community.

## The Lands Around Kaldemor

Every young warrior in Kaldemor starts in the same two places: the meadow east of the square, thick with snails, and the hills to the west, where pigs root and orange mushrooms bounce along the slopes. From there three roads lead out into the world, and behind the woods past the hills lies a country that has grown far wilder this year than anyone in town likes to say aloud.

### The Royal Road

The Royal Road leaves Kaldemor by the west, over the hills and down into **Kaldemor Woods**, the old woods past the hills where Kaldemor's woodcutters worked for generations. The road runs between tall beeches here, and the woods are full of Shrooms and orange mushrooms that have lately stopped scattering when a traveller shouts. A path off the road leads to the hollow where [Ranger Hale](../people/kaldemor/hale.md) takes the bravest hunters to face the Snail King, and another, a farm track overgrown with hedges, turns south toward the last of the far farms.

Out of the woods the road climbs to **the Crossing**, a green hilltop with a gazebo where the lanes from the outlying farms meet the road, grazed by pigs and ribbon pigs and dotted with old stumps that walk when nobody is watching. Beyond it the road enters **the Greenwood**, a bright wood of tall trees and green mushrooms, and climbs out of it over the rise to the Selsior Approach and the capital's great gate.

It is a road travellers have always loved, and it has grown less kind this year. Pigs and stumps that once kept to the verges now come at carts. A pack of wild boars ran at the Guard's road patrol this spring, and something came out of the trees at Pell's cab at dusk last month and cracked two spokes. Carters now travel in company and keep their lanterns lit by day.

### The Verdant Coast Road

The Verdant Coast Road leaves the far side of the meadow and runs down through Kaldemor's farmland to the sea, green the whole way. First come **the Cider Lanes**, orchard lanes between hedges and apple trees, where snails creep out of the long grass and pigs gorge on the windfalls. Then the land flattens into **the Greenmarsh**, wide water meadows of reeds and slow channels, where marsh slimes wobble along the causeway, mud octopuses haul themselves out of the ditches and reed snakes strike from the edges. The road comes down through the dunes to **Shellbrook Mouth**, where a brook runs out across the sand and the Southern Coastal Road sets off along the south shore toward Goa Harbour, and follows **the Strand**, a long shingle beach full of scuttling crabs, east into [Bain Harbour](bain-harbour.md).

Every autumn Kaldemor's harvest carts go down this road with cider, flour, pottery and woodwork. Last autumn one of them never arrived. It lies tipped over in a reed bed in the Greenmarsh, its load spilled in the water. What turned it over, and what became of the people driving it, nobody has been able to say.

The Eastern Forest Road also leaves the meadow's far edge, running north through the woods to [Fairy Forest](fairy-forest.md).

### Beyond the Woods

The danger this year comes from the west, from the country behind Kaldemor Woods, and the people who keep watch over Kaldemor's fields have each seen a piece of it. The snails come at Wilbur's fields in lines. Elsie's cows will not graze the far pasture. Something gnaws the roots of Clem's youngest trees from below. Wilf's Shrooms carry shiny things away into the deep woods. From the lookout on Kaldemor Hills, Garrick has seen a line of broken treetops running toward the Snail King's hollow from somewhere much farther off. And Meg, who went farther than she has told anyone, felt the ground shudder under her feet on the far side of the woods, as if something far below had turned over in its sleep.

Followed back, these signs lead through three stretches of wild country, each deeper and more dangerous than the last.

#### The Boarwood

The farm track from Kaldemor Woods ends at **the Last Stile**, where the hedges stop and the trees begin. Beyond it lies **the Boarwood**, the woods where Orin felled trees for forty winters, now churned to mud by wild boars. The boars came out of the deeper woods this spring, more of them and fiercer than any boar has a right to be, and they have been raiding the far farms ever since: the geese and hens and pumpkins missing from Mildred's ledger, the trampled trail across Wilbur's fields, the far pasture Elsie's cows refuse. Their tusks hook and wrench, and the curved gouges they leave are the marks Percy the wheelwright has been finding on broken wheels from the Royal Road.

At the heart of the Boarwood stands **Stilebrook Farm**, a farm empty since long before anyone now alive, and its old yard has become the herd's wallow. There lives **Gnarltusk**, the greatest of the boars, a deeply touched beast as tall as a cart, with tusks like ploughshares. It drives the herd before it, and where it charges, fences and young trees go down together.

Wilbur, who wants to know what crossed his fields, Mildred, who wants her losses counted, and Percy and Gareth, who have a wager on what made the gouges, all have a reason to send a hunter into the Boarwood. Orin has a reason of his own: forty years ago he cut his sign into the finest oak he left standing there, and he would like to know whether it still stands. Hale sends the hunters who come back to Stilebrook Farm.

#### The Deepwood

On the far side of the Boarwood the trees close in and grow old. This is **the Deepwood**, the deepest part of the woods past the hills, where Wilf has gathered spores since he was a boy, and the boars ran from it for a reason. The Deepwood is full of mushrooms: horned mushrooms along **the Humming Trail**, where the wind sings through hollow trunks, mushrooms among the flowers of **Windflower Glade**, and great blue-capped mushrooms in **the Bluecap Groves**, where the light comes down blue through the caps. Shrooms swarm everywhere, bolder than ever, and the boldest carry off anything that shines.

The mushrooms have spread from a single place. In **the Bluecap Hollow**, at the woods' darkest point, grows **the Bluecap Mother**, a mushroom the size of a house, Disturbance-touched to the root, whose spores have filled the Deepwood with her brood. Her pale threads run under the woods for miles. They reach the edge of Clem's orchard and strip the roots of his youngest trees from below, and the mushroom houses of Kaldemor nearest the woods lean away from them, which is why Bertie, for the first time in fifty years, cannot hear what they want.

Wilf needs a hunter to walk with him through the Deepwood, and to find where the shiny things are going. Bess wants the cause of the bites from the woods that will not close. Winnie, quietly, wants to help Dottie find her lost lamb Button, and Molly wants to know why a pedlar who stops at the Sleepy Snail every summer is weeks late. In an old woodcutters' hut in **Breezy Hollow**, a quiet corner of the Deepwood, a hunter who looks hard enough will find all of them at once: Mott the pedlar, stranded there since the Shrooms stole his tinware, with a speckled lamb with a bent ear that wandered in out of the mist, and a fat, contented cat who answers to Mistress Whiskers.

The broken line Garrick saw begins at the Deepwood's southern edge. It was the Snail King's own road: the old giant snail Mano lived out its long life quietly in the far woods, until the boars came through and drove it ahead of them all the way to the hollow past Kaldemor Woods.

#### Stonecap Keep

Where the trees thin out on the far side of the Deepwood, in the clearing where Meg felt the ground shake, stands **Stonecap Keep**. It was a fortress once, raised by the Aristocrats in the Age of Division to watch over the southeastern country, and it has stood empty since the founding of the kingdom. Kaldemor's generous soil has been at it ever since. Giant mushrooms have grown up through its courtyards, split its towers and capped its walls, until from a distance the keep looks less like a castle than a castle-shaped mushroom.

This spring the keep woke. Mushrooms now stand along its walls in rows, like sentries. Spores drift down its corridors in choking clouds, and on still evenings a grey spore cloud rises over it and hangs above the woods without moving, the same low cloud Nell has drawn three times in her book. Shrooms come to it from every part of the woods, each carrying something that shines: buttons, spoons, a pedlar's tin cups, once a clock tower's great gear. They carry them up the walls of the old keep road and the **Battlements**, through the **Inner Ward**, to the tower at the keep's crown.

There, grown up through the great hall and out over the top of the tower, sits **the Cap King**, a vast mushroom crowned with everything the Shrooms have ever stolen. It is deeply touched, and it is the oldest danger in the woods. When it rose, its roots heaved under the far country, shaking the ground under Meg's feet and sending the whole woods running. The Shrooms crowded to it, the Bluecap Mother's brood swelled behind them, the boars fled the Deepwood, and Mano fled the boars. Everything that troubled Kaldemor's fields this year ran from the same place.

Many of Kaldemor's people have a part in this last stretch. Hale wants Meg followed and brought home safe, and Meg wants company in the clearing, though she will put it as a dare. Nell has finally shown someone her page of grey cloud. Garrick wants to see the country from the top of the walls, Lottie wants copies of the old marks on the keep's gate for her notebook, Wilf wants to see where the shiny things went, and Mott wants his tinware back. Gareth would like some of the keep's old wall iron for his forge, and Bram has a draught in mind against the spores. In the end Hale wants to know what drove the Snail King out, and the answer is at the top of the tower.

### The Sleeping Stones

Not all of Kaldemor's troubles are out in the woods. Twice this spring Hob's windmill sails shuddered on windless mornings. One summer morning Mabel's jugs crept to the edge of her drying shelf, and one fell. Ewan, who listens to the clock tower the way other people listen to music, has heard it running a fraction fast, every day a little faster, as though something under the Park were pulling at it.

Something is. Under the clock tower, down a stair that Ewan and [Alfie](../people/kaldemor/alfie.md) found when they lifted an old floor stone in its cellar, lie **the Sleeping Stones**: halls of carved stone, deep under the Park, older than Kaldemor itself. Nobody knows who built them. Their guardians are made of stone themselves, moss-grown figures that stood motionless in the dark for longer than anyone can reckon, and that the Disturbance has begun to wake. In the deepest hall, at the end of a long gallery, lies **the Sleeper**, a stone giant so large that its slow turning in its sleep shakes the windmills and pulls at the clock. Every time it stirs, the guardians stand up again.

[Ewan](../people/kaldemor/ewan.md) keeps the stair. He lets down anyone he trusts who has the strength for it, and the guardians always seem to meet a hunter as an equal, however strong the hunter has grown. Hob would like his mornings still again, and Lottie would like copies of the carvings on the halls' walls. Ewan and Alfie would like one more thing, and they will ask it only of someone who can keep a secret: to compare a carving in the deepest hall with the one Alfie found on the old treehouse beam. What a hunter finds when they do, Ewan says, is to stay between the three of them.

## Leadership Today

[Mayor Tobin](../people/kaldemor/tobin.md) has led Kaldemor for thirty years. He was the town's cooper until, at forty-one, the townspeople chose him to follow old Mayor Hettie, mostly because he already knew everyone and nobody could imagine a town meeting without him talking at it. He is a widower; his wife Primrose, a potter, died twelve years ago. Every autumn he settles the price of Kaldemor's harvest with Bain Harbour's Harbour Master.

!!! abstract "Design notes"
    The atmosphere is inspired by the bright, welcoming character of MapleStory's Henesys. The seed story, the treehouse, the mushroom houses, the clock tower, Mayor Tobin and the Snail King come from the game itself. New with this page: the guess that the seed came from the World Tree, and the clock tower being raised by the whole town.

    The Lands Around Kaldemor (new): the Royal Road's Kaldemor stretch (Kaldemor Woods, the Crossing, the Greenwood), the Verdant Coast Road (agreed with Bain Harbour's page), and Kaldemor's three wild bands: the Boarwood with Gnarltusk (levels 20-30), the Deepwood with the Bluecap Mother (30-50) and Stonecap Keep with the Cap King (50-70), plus the theme dungeon, the Sleeping Stones under the Park, with the Sleeper. The chain of displacement (the Cap King's rising drives the Shrooms, the Deepwood's brood drives the boars, the boars drive Mano, Mano and the boars trouble the fields) answers Hale's question of what drove the Snail King out. Real-world touches: English hedgerow farms and stiles, boar wallows, mushroom rings and mycelium, and buried stone halls in the manner of barrows and stone circles.

    Still open: who built the Sleeping Stones and why; what the carving in their deepest hall and the one on the treehouse beam show, who cut them and whether the likeness means anything (the one-seed traveller included); what overturned the harvest cart in the Greenmarsh and what became of its drivers; and why the Cap King rose this spring beyond "the Disturbance".
