# Goa Harbour

*The Wilderness Harbour*

Goa Harbour is a port on the west coast of Veligor, on the far side of the island from [Bain Harbour](bain-harbour.md). It feels like a little of Midbarium and a little of Fairy Forest, with highland ruggedness and woodland magic meeting the sea.

## History

### Where the Mountains Meet the Sea

The coast around Goa Harbour is wild: cliffs that drop straight into the water, forests that run down to the shore, and a single deep inlet cut between them. During the Age of Division, both Savages and Magicians came to that inlet. Savage hunters came down from the hills for the fishing, and Magicians came for the sea-herbs and the strange tides, which they believed were moved by the same magic that flowed through the forests. Being allies, they shared it, and a mixed camp grew up on the shore.

### The Sea Wardens

The camp gave the town its oldest tradition. The tides in the inlet are fast and treacherous, and the first families learned to read them the way Magicians read the forest. They called themselves **wardens of the sea**, and to this day, Goa Harbour's pilots guide every ship through the inlet, and no captain sails in without one.

The name Goa is said to come from an old Savage word for "the gap", the narrow gap between the cliffs where the inlet opens to the sea.

### After the Founding

When the kingdom was founded, Goa Harbour opened to the whole island. Timber from its forests and furs and wool from the highlands went out by sea, and travellers began to come for the wild coast and the strange, half-magical town on it. It never grew as large or as busy as Bain Harbour, and its people prefer it that way.

## Environment and Identity

Goa Harbour is rougher and wilder than Bain Harbour, shaped by the mountains, forests and rocky shores around it. Its houses are built of timber and stone and climb the cliffs above the water, joined by rope bridges and carved stairways much like Midbarium's, while lanterns of the forest's making light its quays at night.

Its people keep proud seafaring traditions, along with the independent spirit of the highlands and a touch of the forests' magic. They are adventurous and welcoming, and the town is unlike any other on the island. It is also a natural home for adventurers: since the Age of Disturbance began, the wild coast and forests around it have filled with monsters, and Goa Harbour's people have never been the sort to wait for help.

## Independence

Goa Harbour is **not Midbarium's port, nor Fairy Forest's**. It has its own people, traditions and history, and its resemblance to those towns is cultural, not political. It is led by its **First Warden**, the most respected of its pilots, chosen by the others.

Bain Harbour and Goa Harbour are not rivals. Both are peaceful, prosperous parts of Wonder Kingdom, and every summer their sailors race each other all the way around Veligor.

## The Lands Around Goa Harbour

Goa Harbour sits where three wild things meet: the sea, the cliffs and the **Saltwood**, the old forest of pine and oak that runs down the west coast to the water's edge. Three roads reach the town from the rest of the kingdom, and all are fair going for anyone who can swing a sword. What lies off them is not. Since the Age of Disturbance began, the forest edge, the cliff faces and the outer rocks of the gap have each filled with their own trouble, and the people of Goa Harbour, who have never been the sort to wait for help, are glad of every hunter who comes.

### The Roads

The royal road and the Western Merchant Road both come out onto **Gull Heath**, the open clifftop above the town, where the grass is short and full of thrift and the wind smells of salt. Gull Heath is where Goa Harbour's own country begins: it ends at the head of the Long Stair, beside the windlass of the rope lift and the iron bar of the Cliff Watch. The hill road also starts from Gull Heath and climbs away into the highlands, where Goa Harbour's wool and pelts come from.

#### The royal road from Selsior

The royal road crosses the capital's city bridge with the Urbanograd road, parts from it, and bears southwest toward the far coast, and for most of its length it climbs. It rises onto the **Gorse Downs**, a long, high country of yellow gorse, sheep-walk and wind, where the road runs between old waymarking stones and the sea first shows as a grey line on the horizon. Wild pigs root in the gorse, horned mushrooms crowd the hollows, and things with too many eyes watch the road from the ditches.

Beyond the downs the road drops into the edge of the Saltwood and becomes the **Saltwood Road**, a sunken track under tall pines, cool and resin-scented, with the sound of the sea somewhere off through the trees. Walking stumps, dark and rotten, and mushrooms of every colour live along it, and the timber carts of Goa Harbour pass in both directions. Halfway through the wood a narrow track turns off toward the sea: this is the way down to the Strandwood and the wild coast, and the town's hunters use it every day. The road comes out of the trees onto Gull Heath.

#### The Western Merchant Road

The Western Merchant Road is older than Urbanograd. It was the Aristocrats' road from their stronghold at Old Grad down to the sea, and the carters of Goa Harbour kept using it long after the stronghold stood empty; they still call it the merchant road, though the merchants on it now are mostly their own. From Gull Heath it runs inland through the **Seaward Woods**, the wind-bent northern reach of the Saltwood, oak and pine full of the smell of salt, where crabs scuttle far further inland than crabs should and noisy troops of tree-dwellers pelt travellers from the branches. Below the woods it crosses the river at **Dusk Ford**, where carters cross only in full daylight now, because something waits in the shallows at dusk, long and low and patient. Past the ford the road climbs toward Old Grad's walls and the Gate Meadows of Urbanograd.

#### The Gullwalk and the south coast

The Southern Coastal Road leaves the town to the south along the **Gullwalk**, a path along the clifftop with the sea breaking far below and the seabirds wheeling level with the walker's shoulder. Savage gulls and cliff crabs hold the turf, and the path is not as easy as it was. A little way along, a steep way leads down the cliff face itself to the Saltface. At the Gullwalk's foot the cliffs fall back onto the Long Sands, and the road runs on along the south shore to [Bain Harbour](bain-harbour.md).

### The Strandwood

The Strandwood is the strip of land where the Saltwood comes right down to the wild coast beyond the town's Forest Shore: pines bent by the wind, scrub and fallen trunks, and below them black rocks and weed that the tide uncovers twice a day. The timber crews cut along its edge, and Caddoc the trapper sets his traps across it at night. The track from the Saltwood Road comes down into it from the forest, and its far end, at the foot of the Forest Jaw, leads on to harder country.

**The threat.** The creatures of the Strandwood come out of the forest. [Torva](../people/goa-harbour/torva_hunter.md), who leads the hunters of the wild coast, has tracked them for years and is sure they come from deeper in the woods every season, as if something there were pushing them out. Boars with weed in their bristles root along the shore, stumps rotted to walking driftwood creep out of the scrub, and thin red-brown foxes hunt the rocks at dusk in packs, cleverer than any fox should be.

**The story.** It begins with Torva, who sends her hunters wherever [Bethoc](../people/goa-harbour/bethoc_innkeeper.md)'s sighting board at the Gap Inn says the trouble is worst, and lately that is always the Strandwood. Clearing the shore for the timber crews brings the player to [Caddoc](../people/goa-harbour/caddoc_trapper.md), whose biggest cage traps have been sprung all season without catching anything: bait gone, doors dropped, no hair, no blood, and the ground swept smooth. Sitting up with him through a night beside his biggest trap, the player sees what does it: a fox far bigger than the rest, grey at the muzzle, that lifts the door with its teeth, takes the bait without touching the wire, and sweeps its own tracks away with a great heavy tail.

Caddoc has spent his life believing that cleverness was the one thing the creatures did not have, and he takes it hard. He wants a trigger even the fox cannot learn, and building it takes the player across the Strandwood for what it needs. On the far rocks at low tide the player guards [Brigid](../people/goa-harbour/brigid_herbs.md), Elspeth's herb gatherer, who now walks the wild coast alone to fill her basket, and Brigid, who notices everything, finds where the fox's swept trail leaves the rocks and goes up into the trees. Caddoc's new trap holds for the first time in a season, and catches only one of the old fox's cubs. The old one is still out there. Torva goes after it with the player, and with her young hunter [Kenna](../people/goa-harbour/kenna_hunter.md), who wants something big enough to be talked about at the Gap Inn, into its earth under the roots of a fallen pine.

**The boss: Old Brushtail.** A huge grey-muzzled fox, touched by the Disturbance and grown sly with it. In its earth it fights with feints and false trails, vanishing into the dust it sweeps up with its tail and striking from where it was not, and when hard pressed it calls its cubs out of the dark. With Old Brushtail gone, Caddoc's traps hold again, and he complains about that too. Torva's question stays unanswered: the fox was only one of the things coming out of the forest, and it did not leave the deep woods by choice.

### The Saltface

The Saltface is the great sea cliff south of the town, reached by the steep way down from the Gullwalk: a sheer wall of dark rock streaked white by seabirds, with narrow ledges, crevices and the frayed stumps of old rope ways. At its foot lies the **Undercliff**, a beach of huge rounded boulders that the sea covers at high tide. Above it, just over the reach of the highest waves, run the **Wrack Ledges**, broad shelves of rock hung with dead weed.

**The threat.** Three winters ago the Cliff Watch saw the first creature climb the cliff face itself instead of coming down the forest paths. The climbers come out of the sea: long, grey, splay-footed sea newts, as big as a dog and bigger, which climb wet rock as easily as a fly climbs a wall. With them have come pale, flat, many-legged sea slaters, quicker than a cat on the rock, which chew anything with oil or tar in it, rope included. Crabs swarm the Undercliff, and gulls gone savage nest on the ledges. The low shelves where the newts used to lie out of the water are drowned now at the top of the spring tides, and the newts have been climbing higher every year, always on the stretches of cliff that no lantern reaches.

**The story.** Everyone who keeps the cliffs has seen part of it, and no one has seen all of it. [Ruan](../people/goa-harbour/ruan_cliffwatch.md), Captain of the Cliff Watch, has the player carry lanterns up to the high landings before dark, then sends them down the Saltface to thin the climbers. [Moira](../people/goa-harbour/moira_watch.md) of the watch has noticed that the climbers keep to the unlit stretches, and shows the player where they gather. [Dougal](../people/goa-harbour/dougal_quaywatch.md), the old night watchman of the quay, sits up on the lowest quay steps with the player through a night to see what has been leaving wet, clawed tracks there, and they watch something long and grey come up out of the water, taste the air and slip back down. Following its wake along the cliff foot leads to the Undercliff, where the newts come ashore.

[Gorm](../people/goa-harbour/gorm_stairs.md), the Master of the Stairs, has fallen behind on his new stair down to the far cove, because his crews will not work the lowest landings after dark. Guarding them below the cracked flight they call the Anvil, the player brings back a broken stone scored with deep scratches in sets of four, and old [Ulric](../people/goa-harbour/ulric_stonecarver.md) the stonecarver, judging it, says plainly what Gorm has not wanted to say: the scratches are claws, and they came from below.

The rope bridges have their own trouble. [Kestra](../people/goa-harbour/kestra_bridges.md), who strings them, has found the ropes of the highest bridge chewed from below again and again, and never seen what does it. The player persuades [Fionn](../people/goa-harbour/fionn_child.md), the stair runner, to tell her what he saw from that bridge one evening in spring, carries [Tarran](../people/goa-harbour/tarran_lanterns.md)'s new lantern up to her before he can change his mind about it, and sits out with her on the highest bridge after dark. The lantern lights the whole cliff face beneath, and the slaters are there in its light, dozens of them, pale and flat on the rock. The lantern does not drive them off; it only shows them. The player goes down into the crevices of the Saltface to clear them out.

With Dougal's measurements, Gorm's stone and Kestra's ropes, Ruan at last takes everything to [Iska](../people/goa-harbour/first_warden_iska.md) at the Wardens' House. The First Warden sends the player down to the Wrack Ledges to find what leads the climbers. Torva goes too, and she knows the creature as soon as she sees it.

**The boss: the Gapclaw.** The first climber, the one that reached the high landing three winters ago and scarred Torva's jaw before she and her hunters drove it back over the edge. It survived the fall and has grown huge, a deeply touched sea newt as long as a fishing boat, lairing in a black hollow behind the Wrack Ledges. It fights on wet, sloping rock, clinging to the walls and roof of its hollow and dropping from above, raking with its four-clawed forefeet, and calling the sea up its tunnel in surges that sweep the floor. When it falls, the climbers do not stop at once, but they come fewer and less boldly, and the Cliff Watch can hold the stairs again. Why the low shelves flooded in the first place is another question, and it belongs to the water.

### The Forest Jaw

The gap that gives Goa Harbour its name is the narrow mouth of the inlet between two cliffs. The town climbs one of them. The other, on the forest side, is the **Forest Jaw**: a tall headland of rock and stunted trees, out of reach from the town except by boat, or on foot the long way round through the Strandwood. Along the shore beyond it lies the **Greystrand**, where the spring tides now reach in among the trees and have left a band of grey, salt-killed pines standing in the weed. Out from the foot of the Jaw, uncovered only at low water, run the **Skerries**, a scatter of rocky islets, reefs and sea stacks reaching toward the open sea.

**The threat.** The things here come in from the deep water outside the gap. Great bull seals, savage and heavy, haul out on the Skerries and give way to no one. Hulking shapes of stone and weed lumber over the reefs at low tide. Salt-dead stumps walk on the Greystrand, and crabs as big as cartwheels hold the rocks under the Jaw. The water itself has grown strange: the strongest run through the gap presses harder against the forest-side cliff every year, and at slack water the sea under the Jaw turns in slow circles where no eddy used to be.

**The story.** This is the longest of Goa Harbour's stories, and it is told by the people who remember the water.

[Brigid](../people/goa-harbour/brigid_herbs.md) has watched the oldest herb beds on the Forest Shore wither since last autumn, the leaves pale, then soft and dark at the root, the beds shrinking from the bottom edge up, and she has noticed the wrack line sitting a little higher on those rocks. Walking the Greystrand with her, the player finds the same thing written across a whole shore of dead trees: the high water reaches further than it used to. With the player beside her, Brigid finally finds the courage to tell [Elspeth](../people/goa-harbour/elspeth_healer.md), and the old healer is gentler about it than Brigid ever feared.

[Agna](../people/goa-harbour/agna_netmender.md), the net-mender of the coves, asks, gruffly, for someone to walk out to the notch she cut on the cove rocks as a girl of ten, at the highest spring tide anyone then remembered, and tell her where the water stands now. The player finds her granddaughter [Eilidh](../people/goa-harbour/eilidh_child.md) already there, with the tide slate she keeps hidden in a crack in the rocks. The water has gone over Agna's notch twice this spring, and Eilidh has written it down and underlined it. Agna hears it in silence, and then tells Eilidh her slate is "not bad".

The slate, the notch and Brigid's dead trees go to [Ailsa](../people/goa-harbour/ailsa_tidebook.md), Keeper of the Tide Book, who has watched the new pages drift from the old for years and has said only "Not yet." She wants tide marks from where the wardens' boats cannot go, and the player sets them for her on the Forest Jaw and out on the Skerries, fighting off what holds them. The marks agree with her pages: higher springs, a later slack, and the run drifting toward the forest cliff. Ailsa suspects the answer is already in the oldest pages of the Tide Book, in a Magician hand she can only half read.

The other half of the answer is in a song. [Cormac](../people/goa-harbour/cormac_singer.md), the old shanty singer at the Gap Inn, has never sung more than the first line of his verse about the gap, "the hour the water forgets", because he has never been sure of its ending. He is sure that Morven of the coves knew another version, and that her grandson [Calder](../people/goa-harbour/calder_fisher.md) heard it. Coaxed by the player, Calder sings his grandmother's ending at last, quietly, over his nets. Cormac weeps, as he always does, and then he and the player carry the whole verse up to the Wardens' House.

It fits the oldest page. What the first camp's Savages kept as a song, its Magicians wrote in the Tide Book, and with the words of the one, Ailsa can finally read the other. Long ago, before the kingdom, the slack in the gap came late for a whole season and the run pressed against the forest cliff; the first wardens sounded the foot of the Jaw, found the rock there hollowed out, and found something great lying in the hollow. When it was driven back out to sea, the verse says, the water remembered its hour.

Old [Varro](../people/goa-harbour/warden_varro.md), who has felt the eddies moving for years and never asked Ailsa about them, goes out with the player to the Forest Jaw at slack water and shows them where the eddy used to turn and where it turns now. Iska hears it all at the Wardens' table and gives the charge herself: at the lowest spring tide of the summer, go down under the Forest Jaw into the hollow the run has scoured there, and find what is lying in it.

**The boss: the Undertow.** In the **Scour**, a vast sea-hollow under the Forest Jaw that the water uncovers only at the lowest tides, lies a deeply touched deep-sea fish longer than any ship that comes through the gap, grey above and pale beneath, with a mouth full of curved teeth. Its bulk has bent the run of the water for years. It fights from the pools and channels across the floor of the Scour, dragging the water with it in rushes that pull at the player's feet, lunging from one pool and rising from another, and as the tide turns the water in the Scour rises and the fight grows harder.

When the Undertow is driven out, the next day's slack comes on its hour again, and Ailsa writes it in the Tide Book. The spring highs do not fall. They stay a hand's breadth above the old pages, and the first camp's page says nothing about that. Ailsa writes "Not all of it" in the margin and leaves it there. Brigid begins moving the healthiest of her herbs to new beds higher up the rocks, choosing each place the way her grandmother taught her, and Cormac sings the gap verse through, all of it, at the Gap Inn.

### The Sounding Caves

At the foot of the town's cliffs, between the Inlet Quay and the far cove, dark cave mouths open at low tide, and the water booms in and out of them. Nobody sensible goes in. [Gwenna](../people/goa-harbour/gwenna_outfitter.md), the outfitter of the Lantern Market, was an adventurer until a bad fall in these caves, and she keeps a list of everyone who has gone in and not come back. [Lachlan](../people/goa-harbour/lachlan_adventurer.md), who grew up in the coves, wants his name on the first true map of them, and he has started: a sheet of oilcloth marking every mouth, the tide each can be entered on, and how far in he has been, which is not very far. The caves are entered from the Fishers' Coves at low water, with Lachlan.

The **Sounding Caves** are a world of their own. Past the **Mouths**, the few dozen yards Lachlan has already mapped, a sheer fall that the cove people call **Gwenna's Drop** leads down into the dark. Beyond it the **Long Echo** runs on under the cliffs, where the sea surges through in rushes that a careful explorer learns to time, and at the end of the light lies the **Still Pool**, where the water should lie flat at slack tide, and does not. Cave crabs, blind bats, jellies that glow on the wet walls and small quick octopuses live in the caves, and the deeper in, the larger and stranger they grow. The caves are a match for whoever goes in: a young hunter and a seasoned one each find creatures worth their blade.

The story is Lachlan's. The first time, [Ronan](../people/goa-harbour/ronan_child.md), the dreamy boy from the coves who thinks Lachlan the greatest hero in Goa Harbour, has gone down to a cave mouth with Lachlan's rope before anyone can stop him, and the player finds him there before Lachlan does. Further in, they find a tide mark scratched on the rock beside the letter A, where Lachlan's grandfather Alasdair stopped as a young man before he came out faster than he went in. Deep in, Lachlan admits what he has told no one: in the deepest cave he ever reached he heard the water moving where the tide should have been still, and turned back for the first time in his life. At the Still Pool they find out why.

**The boss: the Inkmother.** A vast, deeply touched octopus that has made the Still Pool its den, whose slow breathing, pushed through the narrow ways under the cliffs, is the water moving where it should be still. It fights from the pool with long arms that reach across the cavern, clouds of ink that put out the light, and broods of small octopuses that swarm the ledges. With it gone, Lachlan finishes the first true map of the Sounding Caves and writes the player's name on it beside his own. The caves stay dangerous, and new creatures always find their way back in, so hunters go down again and again.

## Leadership Today

[Iska](../people/goa-harbour/first_warden_iska.md) has been First Warden for seven years. She learned the tides from [Varro](../people/goa-harbour/warden_varro.md) from the age of seven, and seven years ago the other wardens chose her to lead them and the town from the Wardens' House. [Nessa](../people/goa-harbour/warden_nessa.md) was the other name spoken that night, and backed Iska herself in the end. Iska has skippered Goa Harbour's race boat for twelve summers.

!!! abstract "Design notes"
    New with this page: the shared Savage and Magician camp, the sea wardens and the First Warden, the meaning of the name "Goa", and the summer race with Bain Harbour. Goa Harbour is on the west coast, as the game's world map has it. New with "The Lands Around Goa Harbour": the Saltwood, Gull Heath, the stretches of the royal road (the Gorse Downs, the Saltwood Road) and of the Western Merchant Road (the Seaward Woods and Dusk Ford, as Urbanograd's page names them), the Gullwalk, the Strandwood, the Saltface, the Forest Jaw and the Sounding Caves, and their creatures and stories. The Forest Jaw story settles why the slack in the gap comes late and the run drifts, but not why the spring highs are higher: that, and what is pushing the creatures out of the deep Saltwood, stay open. What waits at Dusk Ford is left open. The verse ending and the first camp's page are paraphrased, not quoted, so their wording stays free. Real-world inspirations: the sea lochs, skerries and saltings of rocky Atlantic coasts; the sea slater, a real shore creature; and the folk belief that a fox sweeps away its tracks with its tail.
