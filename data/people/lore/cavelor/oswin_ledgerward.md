Oswin is eighty-three, Master of the Ledgers, and the keeper of Cavelor's memory. He is precise, dry and secretly sentimental. He can find any tremor in centuries of ledgers within a minute of being asked, and he cannot find his own spectacles at all, which are usually on his head, or in the soup, or in his grandson's pocket.

### Two boys in the Oath Hall

Oswin and Marrun, now Keeper of the Oath, have been friends since they were small boys learning the Oath Below side by side in the Oath Hall, Oswin two years older and already correcting everyone's pronunciation. They took their first turns on the Deep Watch within two years of each other. Marrun was born to walk the passages. Oswin was born to write things down, and the old Master of the Ledgers, Edrith, took him into the Archive at eighteen. He still took his Warden turns, and still does, though these days Brask quietly gives him the shortest passages and Oswin quietly pretends not to notice.

Oswin has been Master of the Ledgers for thirty-eight years, since Edrith died. Marrun is the only person in Cavelor who calls him by a childhood nickname, and Oswin is the only one who can tell Marrun he is being stubborn and be thanked for it.

### The page

Fifty years ago, Oswin was thirty-three and Edrith's under-archivist. The Great World Tree Expedition had come down the stair only days before, and Cavelor was full of astonished explorers and joyful noise. On the morning the ledgers recorded the change in the seal, it was Oswin's turn at the desk, and it was his hand that wrote the entry.

He knows that page by heart. So does Marrun. Neither of them has ever needed to look at it again. Before that page, the ledgers recorded centuries of small, steady entries: a tremor here, a flicker there, the seal's glow holding. Since that page, Oswin has filled more ledgers than in all the years before, and every year's are thicker than the last.

### His family

Oswin's wife died twenty-four years ago, and his only daughter, Ilse, and her husband Tamm died in the damp fever sixteen years ago, leaving behind their three-year-old son. Oswin raised Kell himself, among the shelves, and the boy grew up eager, talkative and inky, with far too many questions. Kell is now Oswin's apprentice, copying the oldest ledgers so that nothing can be lost to damp or fire. Oswin tells him every day that he is too slow and too chatty. He keeps every drawing Kell ever made in a drawer labelled "Miscellaneous Tremors", and he believes Kell does not know.

### The reports

Every Warden reports what they see, and Oswin reads all of it. The reports of Thera, Gorran's daughter, are his favourites: careful, complete and never exaggerated, the only ones he never has to ask questions about. He suspects she is holding something back, because she has begun to look at him as if deciding something. He is waiting for her to decide.

Halvara of the Root Gardens brings him a monthly note about her strange deepest beds, and he enters each one in the ledgers, just in case it matters. He no longer knows which small things matter and which do not. That frightens him more than any single entry.

### What he wants

Oswin wants the record to be complete when someone finally needs it. He does not know how to mend the seal, and the ledgers do not say. But he believes that if an answer is ever found, it will be found by someone reading carefully, and he means to leave them everything they need.

### Hooks

A quest might help Oswin search the oldest ledgers for a forgotten entry, or carry a copy of the records up to the surface for safekeeping. Someone might also help him find his spectacles, which is a quest in itself.
