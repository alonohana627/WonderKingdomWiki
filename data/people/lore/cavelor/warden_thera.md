Thera is twenty-two, a Warden of the Seal, the elder child of Gorran the smith and the older sister of Doran. She is quiet, sharp-eyed and fiercely proud of her duty, and she takes the Oath Below more seriously than anyone her age in Cavelor, possibly more seriously than some who are three times her age. She hates to be told to rest. People have stopped trying.

### The sensible one

Thera's mother, Maelis, died in the damp fever sixteen years ago, when Thera was six. Her father grieved loudly and kept the forge running, and her little brother cried for weeks, and Thera, as she remembers it, simply decided that someone in the house had to keep things in order. She learned to cook before she was eight, kept the forge accounts by ten, and made sure Doran went to his lessons even when he had other plans. She loves her father's noise and her brother's brightness, but she has never quite believed that either of them would remember to eat without her.

She took her first turn on the Deep Watch at sixteen, the day after her birthday, and has never missed one since. In six years she has become, by Captain Brask's own word, the most dependable Warden he has. Only Wardens with ten years' service walk the very lowest stretch of passages beside the prison, so Thera stands the deepest watch she is allowed, as close to that line as the roster will put her, and she knows exactly how many turns remain before she may cross it.

### Reports to the Archive

Every Warden reports what they see, but Thera reports everything. Every flicker in the seal's glow, every tremor in the stone, every lantern that gutters for no reason goes to Oswin, Master of the Ledgers, written out in her small, careful hand. Many of them turn out to be nothing, and she knows it, and she reports them anyway. Oswin, who has read more Warden reports than anyone alive, says hers are the only ones he never has to ask questions about. She has never told him how much that means to her.

### The notebook

This spring, Thera began keeping a private notebook. In it she writes down small changes that no one else seems to have noticed: things too slight or too strange to put in an official report, or that only make sense when you lay many nights side by side. She has not shown it to anyone. She is not yet sure whom to show it to. Brask would take it seriously but might pull her off the deep passages to protect her. Oswin would add it to the ledgers, and then it would be real. Her father would worry, and Doran would tell everyone.

She is also, though she would deny it, a little hurt that Doran went to the surface and came back so in love with it. She does not want to go up. She does not understand why anyone would want to leave while the seal needs watching. Part of her suspects that is not entirely fair to him.

### What she fears

Thera fears being wrong in either direction: raising an alarm over nothing and wasting everyone's trust, or keeping silent about something that mattered. She does not know what is happening to the seal, and nobody else does either, but she is determined that if there is something to see, she will be the one who saw it first.

### Hooks

A quest might earn Thera's trust enough that she shows someone her notebook, or help her check one of its observations against the ledgers. Someone might also, very carefully, have to make her rest.
