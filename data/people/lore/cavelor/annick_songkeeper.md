Annick is thirty-one, the Singer of the Oath, and the keeper of the old songs that the first guardians brought down into the roots. She is lively, teasing and generous with her voice, and she can turn the gloomiest shift change on the Deep Watch into a sing-along before the outgoing Wardens have finished handing over their lanterns. Cavelor is a serious city with a serious duty. Annick is one of the main reasons it has never been a gloomy one.

### The keeper of the songs

The songs Annick keeps are older than the city. Some came down with the Savage warriors who stayed below, some with the Magicians who understood the seal, some with the Commons diggers and the Aristocrat officers, and over the centuries they have been sung together so often that they have braided into one tradition. There are walking songs for the passages, counting songs for the roster, lullabies, mourning songs and the long chant of the Oath Below itself. Very few are written down. They live in Annick's head and in the heads of the people she has taught.

She learned them from old Singer Aldra, who chose her at nine because she could not stop humming, and who sang with her every day until Aldra died eleven years ago. At sixteen, still Aldra's pupil, Annick sang the Oath songs in the Oath Hall on the day Queen Aveline and Keeper Marrun swore their alliance, and she has never been so frightened or so proud in her life.

Now she teaches the songs to every child in Cavelor. Her favourite pupil, though she tries not to have one, is Rhenna, Ysra the healer's daughter, who is shy and serious and already knows the whole Oath by heart. Annick is trying to teach her to sing it loudly. It is going slowly.

### Corvan

Annick's sweetheart is Corvan, Keeper of the Stores at the top of the Upper Stair. He is cheerful, chatty and an incurable haggler, and the two of them can keep a conversation going through an entire meal without either drawing breath. She teases him about his hidden jar of surface honey, which she is not supposed to know about and which she has found three times. Neither of them is in any hurry to settle anything. They are happy as they are, and Cavelor is small enough that everyone already treats them as a pair.

She takes her own turns on the Deep Watch like every adult in the city, and she is a better Warden than people expect. Even on the lowest passages she sings under her breath, very quietly, because she says the silence down there is too heavy to carry alone.

### The song about the sun

Since Doran came back from the surface last autumn, Annick has been trying to write a new song: a song about the sun. No one in the long tradition ever wrote one, because for centuries no one in Cavelor saw it, and none of the few who have climbed the Stair since the expedition was a singer. Doran gives her more stories than she can use, and every verse she writes from them feels wrong: too bright, or too borrowed, or like a song about a lantern with grand ideas. She has started to suspect that she cannot write it properly without seeing the sun herself, and she has not decided what to do about that.

She worries, too, about the old songs. The Wardens are tired. The children ask harder questions than they used to. Annick believes a song can hold a city together in a bad hour, and she is quietly afraid that one day she will need to find the right one.

### Hooks

A quest might help Annick finish her sun song, perhaps by bringing back something from the surface that Doran's stories cannot. Or someone might help her recover a lost verse of an old guardian song, half-remembered and tucked into the Archive's margins.
