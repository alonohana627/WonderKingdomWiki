Brask is forty-four, and for twelve years he has been Captain of the Deep Watch. It is his job to draw up the turns, so that every adult in Cavelor takes a shift and no passage around the seal is ever left unwalked, not for one hour, not on feast days, not when half the city is down with a cough. He has never once let a gap open in the watch, and he means to hand it on to whoever comes after him in the same condition. He is blunt, steady and tireless, has no patience at all for excuses, and has endless patience for a frightened recruit. He counts everything twice: lanterns, Wardens, footsteps, the days until the next turn of the roster.

### How he came to the watch

Brask was born in the Oath Hall quarter to a family of Wardens, and both his parents walked the Deep Watch until the end of their lives. He took his first turn at sixteen and was, by every account, a dull Warden in the best sense: always early, never distracted, never once caught leaning on a wall. Old Captain Dunmar noticed and began giving him the roster to check. When Dunmar died twelve years ago, Keeper Marrun asked the Wardens who should take the roster on, and nearly every one of them said Brask's name before Marrun had finished the question.

He keeps the roster in a thick book in the guardroom at the head of the Deep Watch, written in a square, careful hand. He knows every Warden's strengths, every family's sicknesses and birthdays, and exactly who will swap a shift and who must be ordered to. Marrun trusts him completely, and Brask would walk into the dark for Marrun without asking why.

### His family and friends

Brask married Halvara, head of the Root Gardens, fourteen years ago, and their son Ottel is eight. Halvara is warm where he is stern, and she is the only person in Cavelor who can make him stop counting for an evening. Ottel collects stones and argues with everyone, and Brask secretly loves that the boy never backs down. His oldest friend is Gorran the smith, who makes the Wardens' blades and lanterns and who laughs at Brask's silences until Brask laughs too.

On the watch, Thera, Gorran's daughter, is the Warden he relies on most: he can give her the hardest stretch of the lowest passages and never think about it again. Varn, the scout of the outer tunnels, reports to him, and Brask values his maps above any amount of talk. Ysra the healer scolds him for walking extra shifts on top of the ones he assigns himself, and he ignores her with great politeness.

### What he hears

This spring Brask doubled the Wardens on the lowest passages. He told the Wardens it was a precaution, and Marrun approved it without a word. What he does not say, to anyone, is what he hears down there on quiet nights. He has written it in the margins of his roster in a private shorthand, and then crossed it out, and then written it again. He will not say it aloud because he is not certain of it, and Brask does not report anything he has not counted twice.

He is afraid, though he would never use the word, that one day the roster will not be enough: that there will not be enough adults in Cavelor to walk every passage the seal needs walked. He has done the sums. He does them again every week.

### Hooks

A quest might help Brask learn what the Wardens hear on the lowest passages at night, or fill a gap in the roster when too many Wardens are hurt or exhausted at once. A brave outsider who can stand a shift without complaint would earn his respect quickly, though he would never say so.
