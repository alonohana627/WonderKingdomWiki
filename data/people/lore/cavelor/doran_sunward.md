Doran is nineteen, the younger child of Gorran the smith and the younger brother of Thera, and since last autumn he has been the most talked-about young Warden in Cavelor. He went up the Upper Stair last summer with one of Keeper Marrun's messengers, and he came home a different person. People started calling him "Sunward" within a week of his return, half as a joke and half because there was no better word for him. He did not mind. He thought it was the best name anyone had ever been given.

### Growing up below

Doran grew up in the noise and heat of his father's forge by the Upper Stair, where the old vents carry the smoke up through the rock. His mother, Maelis, died in the damp fever sixteen years ago, when he was three, and he remembers her only as a voice singing in the next room. Gorran raised both children alone, loudly and lovingly, and Thera, three years older, decided early that someone in the family had to be sensible. Doran decided just as early that it would not be him.

He learned the Oath Below before he could read, like every child in Cavelor, and took his first turn on the Deep Watch at sixteen. He was a good Warden, quick and sharp-eared, but even then Brask had to remind him more than once that a lantern is for watching the passage, not for making shadow puppets on the wall. His best friend has always been Kell, Oswin's grandson, and the two of them spent their childhood arguing about the Balrog stories in the Archive's margins and daring each other to climb a little higher up the Stair than they were allowed.

### The surface

Marrun chose Doran to go up because he was quick on his feet, cheerful on long roads and, Marrun said, "the only one of you who will actually look at everything." Doran was gone nearly two months. He climbed out of the roots into daylight and had to sit down on the grass for an hour because his eyes would not stop watering. He walked across a sea of grass that went on further than any cavern, saw rain for the first time, and stood in the streets of Selsior while the messenger handed Marrun's letters to the crown. He came back with sunburnt ears, a pocket full of seeds that will not grow below, and more stories than anyone could stand to hear twice.

He tells them anyway. He squints at every lantern now and calls it "almost as good as noon". Annick has been pestering him for details for her song about the sun, and he gives her far too many. Little Ottel, Brask's son, refuses to believe the sun is really as big as Doran says, which Doran considers a personal challenge.

### What he carries

Under the brightness, Doran is ashamed of himself. He wants to go back. He wants it badly, every day, while his sister stands the deepest watch she is allowed and the ledgers fill faster than ever. He feels that wanting the sky is a kind of desertion, and he has told no one except Kell. His father would laugh and tell him to go; Thera would not laugh at all. He is not sure which would be worse.

He has an odd rivalry with Varn, the scout of the outer tunnels, who thinks a map is worth more than any number of stories and says so in as few words as possible. Doran thinks Varn is jealous that Marrun did not send him up instead. Varn has never said whether that is true.

### Hooks

Doran would leap at any task that takes him up the Upper Stair again, and a quest might give him one, or make him choose between the surface and a duty below. He also knows the route the messengers take better than any other young Warden, which could matter if a message ever has to travel fast.
