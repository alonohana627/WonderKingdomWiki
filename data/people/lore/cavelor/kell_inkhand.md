Kell is nineteen, Apprentice Archivist, and the grandson of Oswin, Master of the Ledgers. He is eager, talkative and full of questions, he has ink on every finger and usually a smudge on his nose, and he thinks out loud and apologises for it constantly. "Sorry, sorry, I'm just thinking," is said so often in the Archive that the older scholars have started finishing it for him.

### Raised among the ledgers

Kell's parents, Ilse and Tamm, both died in the damp fever sixteen years ago, when he was three. Ilse was Oswin's only daughter. Kell's grandmother had died years before he was born, so Oswin, already old, dry and precise, took a small, loud boy into his rooms beside the Archive and raised him among the ledgers. Kell learned to read from tremor records and to count from the numbered shelves. He thinks it was a perfectly normal childhood. Everyone else in Cavelor thinks it explains a great deal.

He loves his grandfather enormously, and he spends a fair part of each day finding Oswin's spectacles. He knows that Oswin is secretly sentimental, because Oswin keeps every drawing Kell ever made in a drawer labelled "Miscellaneous Tremors", and Kell has never let on that he knows.

### Copying the records

Kell's work is copying. The great ledgers of the Archive go back to the founding, and the oldest volumes are crumbling at the edges, spotted with damp and brittle with age. If one were lost to damp or fire, a piece of Cavelor's memory would be gone forever. So Kell copies them, page by page, into fresh books that are stored in a different hall from the originals. It is slow work, and he has years of it ahead of him, and he loves it.

What he loves most are the margins. Over the centuries, the guardians who kept the ledgers wrote all sorts of things beside the tremor counts: notes, prayers, jokes, recipes, complaints about the cold, and above all the stories their families brought down from the surface about the Balrog. A Savage-born Warden wrote that it came out of the deep earth, woken by the noise of war. A Magician hand wrote that it came through a tear in the world. A Commons scribe called it "the Burning" and said it had always been there. An Aristocrat clerk recorded only numbers. Kell has copied every one of them into a book of his own.

### The question nobody answers

Kell wishes, more than anything, that someone would finally tell him which of the stories is true. He has asked his grandfather, who said "each might hold a piece of it" and went back to his ledger. He has asked Keeper Marrun, who listened to the whole question and then said he did not know. He is beginning to suspect that nobody knows, and he cannot decide whether that is terrifying or the most exciting thing he has ever heard.

He also listens, for hours, to old Grisel's stories of her sixty years on the Deep Watch, and he has noticed that she always stops before the part where the seal changed. He has started counting the places where she stops. He has not decided whether to ask her why.

His best friend is Doran, who came back from the surface in love with the sky. Kell is the only person Doran has told that he wants to go back, and Kell has kept that secret faithfully, while privately wishing he could go too, just to see whether the surface libraries hold any of the margins' missing pieces.

### Hooks

A quest might help Kell find a lost or damaged ledger, or carry a question up to the scholars of Selsior or Fairy Forest about the Balrog stories. Someone might also help him get Grisel to tell the end of her story, if she ever will.
