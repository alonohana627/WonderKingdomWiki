Ottel is eight years old, the only child of Brask, Captain of the Deep Watch, and Halvara, head of the Root Gardens. He has his father's stubborn jaw and his mother's habit of being slightly muddy at all times, and he has never in his life seen the sun. He is not convinced that he is missing much.

### A child of the Gardens

Ottel spends most of his days in the Root Gardens, where his mother tends the pale mushroom beds and the root-fruit vines that feed the whole city. He is supposed to be helping. Sometimes he is. More often he is lying on his stomach between the beds, watching the little white crawlers that live in the soil, or digging in the loose rock at the edge of the caverns for stones. Halvara lets him, as long as he waters the seedlings first, and she has learned to check his pockets before washing anything.

He collects interesting stones. He has dozens, sorted into boxes his father counted for him (twice), and he can tell you where every one was found and why it is better than the others. There are banded ones from the old water channels, glittering ones from near the forge, a black one so smooth it looks wet, and one he will not show anyone. That one is his best stone, and he is saving it to give to the sun.

### The trouble with the sun

The trouble began when Doran came back from the surface last autumn and told everyone that the sun was bigger than the whole of Cavelor and brighter than every lantern in the city lit at once. Ottel thought about this very carefully and decided it was not possible. Nothing could be that big and still hang in the air. Nothing could be that bright without burning everyone up. He told Doran so, in front of Annick and three Wardens, and he has not changed his mind since.

Doran, unfortunately, will not give up. Every time they meet, he has a new piece of evidence: the sunburn on his ears, the way surface people squint, the size of the shadows. Ottel answers every one of them. He has decided, privately, that if the sun is real he will give it his best stone, because something that big should have a present, and if it is not real he will keep the stone. Either way he wins.

His closest friend is Rhenna, Ysra the healer's daughter, who is a year older and much more serious. She already knows the whole Oath by heart and practises standing very straight, and Ottel thinks this is very funny and also a little impressive. They play at Wardens in the empty corners of the Gardens, and Rhenna always insists on doing it properly.

### What he does not know

Ottel is fearless in the way of children who have never been told no for long. He does not understand yet why his father has been coming home later and quieter this spring, or why his mother sometimes stands for a long time by the deepest mushroom beds and frowns. He has noticed, though. He notices most things, and he is beginning to collect worries the way he collects stones, sorting them carefully and showing them to no one.

### Hooks

Ottel might wander somewhere he should not while hunting for a new stone, and need to be found. One day, when he is old enough, someone might take him up the Upper Stair so he can see the sun for himself and decide whether to hand over his stone.
