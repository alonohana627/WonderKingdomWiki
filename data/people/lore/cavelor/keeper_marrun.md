Marrun is eighty-one, and he has walked the Deep Watch since he was sixteen: sixty-five years of passages, lanterns and listening, longer than most of Cavelor's people have been alive. He has been Keeper of the Oath for twenty-two years, since old Keeper Ardith died, and he leads Cavelor and the Wardens of the Seal from the Oath Hall. He is grave, patient and slow to speak, but his rare laughs fill the whole hall, and the children of Cavelor sometimes try to make him laugh on purpose, just to hear the echo.

### The watch and the night it changed

Marrun learned the Oath Below before he learned to read, like every child in Cavelor, and he took his first turn on the Deep Watch with a lantern almost too heavy to hold. For nearly thirty years his watch partner was Grisel, and the two of them walked the lowest passages together so often that they could tell each other's footsteps from the far end of a tunnel.

Fifty years ago, when Marrun was thirty-one, Grisel was on shift the night the Great World Tree Expedition first came down the stair. Marrun was asleep after a long watch, and it was Grisel who sent for him. He remembers the explorers' astonished faces and the joy of that first meeting better than almost anything in his life. He remembers, too, the days that followed, when the explorers pushed deeper than the guardians advised, and the morning the ledgers recorded the change in the seal. His oldest friend, Oswin, who keeps the ledgers now, knows that page by heart. So does Marrun. Neither of them has ever needed to look at it again.

For fifty years since, he has watched the seal weaken in the ledgers, entry by entry. Grisel stops her stories before that part. Marrun does not have that choice.

### The Keeper's work

Marrun listens to everyone, from the oldest Warden to the smallest child, before he decides anything. Brask, captain of the Deep Watch, draws up the turns so that no passage is ever left unwalked, and Marrun trusts him completely; lately Brask has doubled the Wardens on the lowest passages, and Marrun approved it without a word, because he hears the same things Brask does. Marrun has never married. He likes to say the Watch was his marriage and Cavelor his children, and nobody in the city has ever thought he was joking.

More and more of his days go to writing to the surface. He sends messengers up the Upper Stair with letters for the crown and for anyone else who will read them, trying to make the kingdom understand how worried Cavelor is. Last year he sent young Doran up with one of those messengers, and Doran came home in love with the sun. Marrun understands that better than Doran thinks.

### The queen's oath

For most of fifty years, the surface listened kindly and did little. The old king who sent the expedition received Cavelor's messengers with real grief, but the warnings were filed more often than answered. Then, fifteen years ago, in the first year of her reign, Queen Aveline came down the Upper Stair herself, the first reigning monarch ever to do so. She walked a full shift of the Deep Watch beside Marrun without complaint, asked more questions than any Warden-in-training, and in the Oath Hall the two of them swore an alliance: the crown would answer every message from below, and Cavelor would send word of every change in the seal.

She has kept that promise. Their letters have passed each month since, she has come down twice more, and four years ago Marrun went up to Selsior for the first and only time, stood under the Great Bell and found the sky far too large. He says she is the first person on the surface who listened to him to the end. He worries that she carries too much, and that he is the one who keeps handing it to her.

### What he fears

Marrun fears the day he has to send word that a letter cannot answer. He does not know how long the seal will hold, or what might mend it, and he will not pretend otherwise.

A quest might carry an urgent message up the Upper Stair, or help Brask learn what the Wardens hear on the lowest passages at night.
