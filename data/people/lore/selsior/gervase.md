Gervase is forty-nine, the kingdom's cartographer, and he cannot stand still. He paces when he thinks, which is always, and he talks while he paces, which is also always, and he is allergic to blank spaces on maps in a way that makes him itch. Show Gervase a map with an empty corner and he will want to know what is there, and then he will want to go and look, and then he will want to draw it.

### The road years

He grew up in the Scholars' Quarter and was drawing maps of his own street at six, with every door, drain and cat marked on them. At twenty-two, twenty-seven years ago, he set out on the royal roads with a quiet young surveyor called Leofric, who was two years younger than him and the only person Gervase had ever met who was more careful than he was restless. For the better part of seven years the two of them walked, measured and argued their way between the towns. Gervase talked and paced and could not bear a blank space on a page. Leofric would walk the same mile three times to be sure of it. Between them they got the roads right.

The survey ended twenty years ago, the year Leofric met the woman who would become queen over a disputed map in the Scholars' Quarter, and married her before the year was out. Gervase likes to say that the road Leofric was defending that afternoon was one they had walked together, and that he would have been less polite about it. He and Leofric are still old colleagues and old friends, and Gervase is one of the few people in the city who still calls the queen's consort by his name and sends him maps with rude notes in the margins.

### The monster map

Gervase works in a narrow house in the Scholars' Quarter with maps on every wall, maps on the ceiling over his worktable and maps laid out on the floor so that visitors must walk on the margins. He draws the kingdom's maps for the Academy, the palace, the Guard and anyone else who will pay, and he has never yet drawn one he thought was finished.

For some years now he has been making a different kind of map: a map of where monsters have been seen. It began as a curiosity, a few marks in the wild places where everyone knew there was trouble. It is a curiosity no longer. His friend Edric, Lieutenant of the Road Patrol, sends him a copy of every patrol report, and comes round in person when he can, to stand in front of the map with a cup of something and tell a terrible joke or two before they both go quiet. Gervase marks every sighting with a dot of ink and the date. Leofric keeps his own map of the same reports on a wall in the palace, and when the two of them compare, the marks agree.

The map is getting crowded. Places that were blank spaces in Gervase's youth, the safe stretches between towns that nobody bothered to draw in detail because nothing ever happened there, are filling up with dots. He has had to start a second sheet for the roads near the towns themselves. He paces in front of it for hours.

### What he wants and fears

Gervase wants to finish a map of the whole kingdom, every road and ford and ridge, with no blank space anywhere, before he is too old to walk. He also wants, for the first time in his life, a map with fewer marks on it than the year before. What he fears is the shape the dots are making. He is not a scholar of the Balrog War and does not pretend to be. But he knows what a map looks like when something is spreading, and he has started, very quietly, to measure how fast.

A quest might begin with Gervase needing someone to walk a stretch of road that is blank on his monster map, to find out whether it is truly quiet or simply unwatched, or with a dot appearing somewhere on his map that should be impossible.
