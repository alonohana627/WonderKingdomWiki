Aylwin is nineteen, a guard of the road patrol, and he blushes so easily that the older riders have made a game of it. He is brave, earnest and very good with a horse, and he has fought monsters on the royal roads without flinching. Ask him whether he writes poetry, though, and he will go red to the ears and change the subject. He does. He has never shown a line of it to anyone.

### Raised in the stables

Aylwin grew up above the Guard stables at the Knights' Court. His parents drowned when a river barge overturned in a spring flood when he was four, and his grandfather Wystan, the stablemaster, took him in. Wystan has kept the Guard's horses for forty years, grumbling at people and gentle with animals, and he raised his grandson much the way he raises a foal: patiently, with few words and a great deal of mucking out. Aylwin learned to ride before he could read, and he learned from Wystan to trust a horse's opinion of a stranger. He trusts it still.

He joined the Guard at seventeen and was put on the road patrol last year, under Lieutenant Edric. He loves it. He loves the dawn rides out of the Court, the long roads, the towns at the end of them and the way the whole patrol comes back in together at dusk. He even loves Edric's jokes, which nobody else admits to.

### The boars

Earlier this spring, on the road to Kaldemor, a pack of wild boars came out of the trees at the patrol, more of them and wilder than any boar has a right to be. Aylwin's horse stood when others shied, and Aylwin stood with it, and between them they held the road while the rest of the patrol turned. He tells the story as if it were funny, mostly about how a boar ate his hat. Edric tells it differently, and so does Wystan, who says that the horses come back from the roads these days jumpy and wild-eyed, and who looked his grandson over very carefully that evening without saying anything at all.

### The princess

Elinor has been coming down to the Guard stables to see the horses since she was small. She and Aylwin grew up nodding to each other across the yard, then talking, then talking for far longer than anyone talks about horses. Somewhere along the way they fell for each other. Neither of them has told anyone, and both of them are sure nobody knows.

Aylwin also knows the other thing. On the first morning a cloaked stranger came to train at dawn with Rowena, Aylwin, saddling up for patrol, knew her by the way she walked across the yard. He has never said a word about it, to her or to anyone else, and he never will. But he worries. He knows what the roads are like now, and he knows Elinor wants to ride them, and he cannot decide whether he is more proud of her or more afraid.

### The poems

The poems are mostly about her. Some are about the roads, the light over the Greenwood at dawn and the sound the patrol makes coming home. He keeps them in a bundle wrapped in oilcloth under a loose board in the hayloft, where he thinks no one will ever look. He has written one he thinks is good, and he has spent most of the spring deciding whether to give it to her.

### What he wants and fears

Aylwin wants to be a guard Wystan can be proud of and a man Elinor deserves, and he thinks, earnestly, that these are probably the same thing. What he fears is the day the patrol meets something worse than boars, and he is not there to stand in front of it, or he is.

A quest might begin with Aylwin's bundle of poems going missing from the hayloft, or with him needing someone to carry a message to the palace that he cannot carry himself.
