Mab is ten, the pie seller's daughter, and she has never in her life walked across the plaza when she could cartwheel across it. She is bold, giggly and entirely fearless, and she tries every trick she sees the street performers do, usually within the hour, usually somewhere she has been told not to. She has skinned both knees so many times that her father Piers says she has knees like pie crust, and she has taken it as a compliment.

### The cart and the Bell

Her father sells pies from a cart by the palace steps, and he claims his recipe is older than the Great Bell. Piers is jolly, round and full of gossip, and he knows who bought what pie and why. He is raising Mab alone. Her mother is not part of her life, and Mab, who does not remember her, has never pressed him about it; Piers talks about the cart instead, and Mab has learned that the cart is how her father says most of the things he means. He hopes she will take it over one day. She helps at it when she has to: she can count change faster than he can, she knows which customers want gravy and which want gossip, and she can sell a cold pie by standing beside it looking hungry.

When she can, which is whenever Piers looks the other way, she practises cartwheels by the Great Bell. The flagstones around its dais are the smoothest in the plaza, and the dais itself makes a perfect audience: tall, still and very grand. Hugh, the Warden of the Great Bell, tells her off every afternoon. Mab has noticed that he has never yet actually stopped her, and that on the day she landed her first handspring he looked away very quickly and polished something.

### The most famous performer in Selsior

Mab wants to be the most famous performer in Selsior, and she has studied the competition. Lambert the juggler is kind to children and has taught her to keep two balls in the air; she is working on three, and he lets her pass the hat after his best shows. She copies Mirabel's spins at dusk, badly, and Mirabel laughs and shows her how to spot a turn. She has watched Rafe's puppet show on Theatre Row so many times she can do the voices, and she sits under Fulke's lute with her chin on her knees. Her father says she would rather run off with the circus. Mab says Selsior is the circus, and she is not wrong.

### Wat and Hodge

Her two best friends are Wat, the errand boy, who knows every alley shortcut in the city, and Hodge, who runs across the rooftops of the old quarters. Between the three of them there is no corner of Selsior they cannot reach: Mab handles the crowds, Wat handles the ground and Hodge handles everything above it. When one of them is in trouble, the other two turn up, usually from an unexpected direction. Mab is the loudest of the three and the one most likely to be caught, and she has never once given up the other two. She knows Hodge practises with a broom handle at dawn; she has offered to teach him a proper flourish, and he has declined, which she thinks is his loss.

### What she wants and fears

Mab wants a stage, a real one, and her name on a playbill twice the size of anyone else's, like Rosamund's. Failing that, she will settle for the plaza on festival day with every eye in it on her. What she fears, though she would never say so, is that her father is right, and that she will be selling pies at the palace steps when she is as old as he is.

A quest might begin with Mab attempting a stunt somewhere very high to impress a crowd, or with her spotting, from the middle of a cartwheel, something on the Great Bell's dais that nobody else in the plaza has noticed.
