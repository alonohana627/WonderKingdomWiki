Cuthbert is forty-eight, the royal cook, and you can hear his kitchen from the far end of the palace. He shouts at the pots. He sings to the bread. He is loud, red-faced and generous, and he runs the palace kitchens like a ship in a storm, with a great deal of noise, a great deal of steam and, somehow, every dish on the table at the moment it was promised.

### Thirty-four years of kitchen

He came to the palace at fourteen as a scullion, thirty-four years ago, in the middle of King Bertram's long reign, and he scrubbed pans for two years before anyone let him near a knife. He learned everything there was to learn about the palace kitchen by being in the way of it. By thirty he was royal cook, eighteen years ago, and two years after that he cooked the coronation feast for Queen Aveline, a feast for half the kingdom in the plaza and the throne hall at once. He did not sleep for three days. Osbert, the Royal Steward, still keeps the menu from that feast in his desk, and Cuthbert, who has found this out, has never once let him forget it.

Osbert has spent years trying to stop Cuthbert shouting at the pots and singing to the bread in the presence of envoys. He has failed. Cuthbert says the bread rises better for a song and the pots know what they did.

### Juliana and Agnes

Cuthbert married Juliana, who kept the palace stillroom and made its preserves, and for a few years the two of them filled the lower palace with the smell of jam and roasting meat. Their daughter Agnes was born in the rooms off the kitchen. Juliana died of a winter fever when Agnes was six, thirteen years ago, and Cuthbert, who had never been quiet in his life, was quiet for most of that winter. Then he picked up his ladle again, put Agnes on a stool by the bread oven, and raised her in the kitchen with every cook, scullion and pot boy in it helping. He still makes Juliana's plum preserve every autumn, from her book, and does not let anyone else touch the jars.

When Agnes asked Osbert for a place in the household at fourteen, rather than the kitchen, Cuthbert was hurt for about a day and proud for the rest of the year. He is proudest of all now that she has the upper halls. He still sends biscuits up with her, which she trades with Peterkin for news, and he pretends not to know.

### Every town's table

Cuthbert cooks for every envoy's feast the palace gives, and he insists on learning each town's dishes properly, not as the capital imagines them. He has argued with Kaldemor's bakers about crust, learned Midbarium's slow-roasted mutton from an envoy's own cook and nearly set the kitchen on fire learning to smoke fish the harbour way. He keeps a great stained book of every recipe he has been taught and who taught it. There are dishes from Fairy Forest that he has made only once, because the herbs did not travel, and there is a page for Cavelor that is still blank, because he has never yet tasted what the guardians below the Tree eat, and nobody in his kitchen knows.

His dream is a great harvest feast with one dish from every town on a single table, the whole kingdom passing plates. He has planned the menu many times over. Every year the roads get harder, the carts come in later and an ingredient or two simply does not arrive, and every year the feast slips a little further off.

He also swears that one of the old keys young Prince Alaric collects used to open a larder that nobody in the palace can now find. He remembers the larder from his scullion days. He cannot remember where it was.

### What he wants and fears

Cuthbert wants his harvest feast, and he wants Agnes to be happy, and he is not entirely sure which comes first. What he fears is a winter when the carts stop coming altogether, and a kitchen with nothing in it to sing to.

A quest might begin with Cuthbert needing one ingredient from a town the carts no longer reach, or with Alaric's key, and an old scullion's memory of a door behind the cold store.
