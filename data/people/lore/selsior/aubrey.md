Aubrey is seventy-six, the oldest Grand Swordmaster in the Royal Guard, and most mornings you will find him asleep. He has a bench against the warm south wall of the Knights' Court practice yard, and he sits on it with his hat over his eyes and his hands folded on his stomach, and he snores. He is wry, slow-moving and still frighteningly quick with a blade. Recruits who think he is asleep learn otherwise the first time they hold a sword wrong in his hearing, because he will say, from under the hat and without moving, "Your wrist is lying to you," and he will be right.

### Fifty-eight years at the Court

He came to the Knights' Court at eighteen, in the year King Bertram was crowned, a carpenter's son from the inner streets with long arms and no patience. He has said many times that he was the worst recruit of his intake and the only one too stubborn to leave. He earned the Swordsman's rank, then the Swordmaster's, and at thirty-five he passed the trial for Grand Swordmaster, the youngest in the Guard then. For the next three decades he was the finest blade at the Court and taught most of the warriors who now hold rank in it.

He was already an old Grand Swordmaster when Queen Aveline made her alliance with Cavelor, and he went down the Upper Stair with the other serving Swordmasters and Grand Swordmasters to swear the new oath in the Oath Hall. He complained about his knees the whole way down and never once about the oath.

He never married. He says the Court was his house and the recruits were noisy enough to count as children. He still keeps his old room off the cloister, and Helewise, the quartermaster, keeps his name on the roll because nobody has ever dared take it off.

### Rhys

Twenty-two years ago a young man called Rhys came to the Court with a borrowed sword and much more confidence than skill. Aubrey watched him swing for about a minute and told him he fought like a man chopping firewood. Rhys spent the next two years learning to stop, and Aubrey has never told him that he was the most promising recruit the old man had seen in twenty years. The spring after Rhys arrived, a young Uskari warrior came down from the highlands to train for a season, and Aubrey, who thought both of them were too fierce for their own good, paired them so they would wear each other down. They did not. They fought eighty evening bouts and scratched the tally on a post, forty-one to thirty-nine, and Aubrey still walks past that post on his way to his bench and taps it.

He gave up teaching nine years ago, a year after Rhys passed the Grand Swordmaster's trial, because he said he had seen what he was waiting for. When Rhys became Captain, Aubrey was the first to salute him and the last to stop calling him "boy". Rhys still listens to him with the patience of a pupil who owes his teacher everything, which Aubrey finds touching and slightly suspicious.

### The test

The old books name one rank beyond the Grand Swordmaster: the Sword Saint, earned by very few. Nobody alive at the Court has met one. Rhys sets that test as the old test, admitting he passes on something older than himself, and the books disagree about what it is. Tobe, one of this spring's recruits, has read six accounts of it, all different, and has started bringing them to Aubrey's bench. Aubrey pretends to be asleep through most of them.

He has spent his retirement looking for the real test. He has read every drill book in the Court's library, most of the sword books in the Academy's great library, and a great many crumbling papers nobody else wanted. Last autumn, in a chest of old Guard drill books that the storeroom was about to throw out, he found a single loose leaf of very old vellum, folded inside a cover where it did not belong. It is in an old hand, half faded, and it describes, he says, a part of the Sword Saint's test. He has shown it to nobody, not even Rhys. He will say only that it is not what anyone expected, and that it is not a fight with another swordsman, and he will not say how he knows the page is real. Rhys suspects that it is.

### What he wants and fears

Aubrey wants to see one Sword Saint before he dies. He would prefer it to be Rhys, and he would settle for anyone holding their wrist right. What he fears is that the page is real, that the test it describes cannot be passed in an age of monsters, and that he will be the one to have to say so.

A quest might begin with Aubrey asking a traveller to find the rest of the book his page was torn from, or with him waking on his bench one morning, very suddenly, because someone he has never seen before is holding a sword in a way he last saw described in old ink.
