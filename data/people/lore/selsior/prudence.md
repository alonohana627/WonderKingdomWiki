Prudence is thirty-six, the alchemist of the Scholars' Quarter, and her eyebrows have grown back twice. The first time was an accident with a copper salt. The second time, she maintains, was a successful experiment that happened to be louder than expected. She is methodical, prickly and proud, with ink on her cuffs, a burn scar across the back of one hand and no patience at all for anyone who says "near enough". In her trade, she says, near enough is how roofs come off.

### The workshop

Her workshop is the ground floor of a tall, thin house in a lane behind the Academy, with a furnace at the back, shelves of stoppered jars to the ceiling, every one of them labelled in her small, upright hand, and a chalk line on the floor that visitors are not permitted to cross. She lives in the two rooms above, which smell, like everything she owns, faintly of sulphur and lavender.

She brews reagents and inks for the Royal Academy: the powders the masters use in their demonstrations, the salts for the students' practical lessons, and the inks that the Academy's copyists and the palace's scribes swear by, a black that never fades and a red that never runs. She learned her craft as an apprentice to an old Academy alchemist, a woman with no eyebrows at all, who took her on at fifteen and left her the furnace and the jars when she died nine years ago. Prudence has kept the workshop alone since. Her own parents, a schoolmaster and his wife, are long dead. She has never married, and says no one would want to live above that furnace, which is true.

### Mirel

Prudence also makes tonics, and she will tell you, with some heat, that they are better than anything sold in the Crown Market. By "anything sold in the Crown Market" she means the potions of Mirel the apothecary, whose stall does a roaring trade with adventurers on their way up the road. Mirel is brisk, cheerful and a born salesman, and Prudence considers all three of these qualities suspicious in a man who handles medicine. She says his tonics are made for the bottle and not the patient. He says hers taste like ink, which, she admits, some of them do.

Their rivalry is old and well known, and it is conducted chiefly through customers, who are told by each what the other has got wrong. They do not, in fact, disagree about very much except who is better. Neither of them would ever say so.

### The moment potion

What Prudence is trying to brew is a potion that heals in a moment. Not a tonic that helps a wound close over days, but one that closes it while you watch. She has read of such a thing in old accounts of the Balrog War, which may be stories, and she has seen enough hurt travellers come up the road this year to believe that the kingdom needs it whether it is a story or not. She has been working on it for three years. She has made a draught that stops bleeding and turns the drinker green for a week, and one that heals a scratch beautifully and blows out the workshop's windows while doing so.

Her neighbours are trying to get her moved. They have sent a letter to the Academy, signed by eleven households, and a second, longer one after the windows. Prudence has answered both, at length, explaining the chemistry.

### What she wants and fears

Prudence wants the moment potion in a stoppered jar with her label on it, and she wants Mirel to have to buy it from her. What she fears is that it cannot be done by anyone, and that she will spend her life near enough.

A quest might begin with Prudence needing a reagent that can only be gathered somewhere monsters have moved into, or with a jar in her workshop that has started, all on its own, to glow.
