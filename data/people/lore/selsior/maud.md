Maud is fifty-two, housekeeper to Dame Orsa, and every dawn she goes into the Crown Market like a general taking a city. She carries two baskets, one on each arm, and a list she does not need, and she moves through the busiest market on Veligor at a pace that clears a path in front of her. "Mind the baskets!" she calls, and the crowd, which has known her for years, minds them. Eggs first, while they are still warm. Bread next, from Margery's stall, before the rest of the city wakes up and wants it. Then fruit, the best in Selsior, which is never at the same stall two days running and which Maud always finds.

### A market child

She was born a few streets from the Crown Market, the daughter of a fruit seller who kept a barrow at its edge, and she spent her childhood under that barrow, sorting bruised apples from good ones and learning to tell a ripe pear by its smell from three stalls away. Her mother taught her that a market is a war fought politely, and that the only way to win it is to arrive first and leave last. Maud swears the Crown Market opens at dawn and closes only when the stalls run out, and she means to be there for both. Both her parents are long dead now, and the barrow went with them, but Maud still stops at the corner where it used to stand, out of habit, and buys something from whoever has it now.

She has kept house for Dame Orsa for twenty-two years, in the rooms above and behind the general store, and she has never married. She says one household that needs running is plenty, and that Dame Orsa's needs running more than most.

### Dame Orsa's house

Dame Orsa is grand, motherly and shrewd. She calls every customer "dear" and remembers every debt, and she runs a shop that stocks everything a traveller forgets, from rope to scrolls home. Maud runs everything else: the kitchen, the linen, the coal, the accounts that are not the shop's, and the steady stream of travellers who come round to the back door to ask Dame Orsa for one more favour. The two of them are very fond of each other and disagree about almost everything, loudly, over breakfast.

Dame Orsa earned her title long ago for service to the crown and has never explained what it was. In twenty-two years Maud has never found out. She has dusted every room in the house and has found no medal, no letter and no portrait, and she has decided that this is suspicious in itself. She has several theories, most of them involving pirates, and she tells a new one to Margery roughly once a month.

### Friends at the market

Margery the baker is Maud's oldest friend in the market and her first stop every morning. Margery learned to bake in Kaldemor and sells out by noon, and Maud is her loudest champion; she tells everyone who will stand still that Margery deserves a bakery big enough to bake through the night, and that someone ought to give her one. Margery's mother, Alys the cheesemonger, lets Maud taste her cheese now, which took about ten years.

And then there is Biscuit, the dog from the gate, who follows his nose to the Crown Market every morning and has learned that the bustling woman with two baskets is a soft touch. Maud keeps rinds and scraps from Dame Orsa's kitchen in her apron pocket for him and tells everyone she is only getting rid of them. Nobody believes her, least of all Biscuit.

### What she wants and fears

Maud wants the market to stay as it is: loud, crowded, full and safe. Lately the carts come in later, the prices from the other towns are higher, and some mornings a stall she has known for years is simply not there. She fears the day the roads close and the stalls run out before noon and do not fill again.

### Hooks

A quest might begin with Maud's dawn list containing something that cannot be found anywhere in the market, which has never happened before. Or it might begin with Maud deciding, after twenty-two years, that she is finally going to find out what Dame Orsa did for the crown.
