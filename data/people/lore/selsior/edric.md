Edric is forty-six, Lieutenant of the Road Patrol, and he has the face of a man who has spent most of his life squinting into weather. He leads the riders of the Royal Guard who keep the royal roads open between the towns, and he has done it long enough to remember when the job was mostly about lost carters and lame horses. It is not about that any more.

### Born to the road

Edric and his younger brother Tristan grew up on the roads before either of them could ride them. Their father was a carter who hauled goods between the capital and the other towns, and the boys spent their childhood on the bench beside him, learning every milestone, ford and good place to water a horse. Their mother kept the house in Riverside, and both parents are dead now. Tristan took to the river, and scouts its banks for the barges. Edric took to the road. They still meet whenever they can to compare notes, and the notes, lately, are always about the same thing: what the wilds are doing, and how fast.

Edric joined the Guard at twenty, twenty-six years ago, and rode his first patrol three years later. He was already a seasoned rider when a young recruit called Rhys came to the Knights' Court with a borrowed sword, and he likes to remind the Captain of it when nobody else is listening. Rhys made him lieutenant seven years ago, in his first year as Captain, and Edric has never wanted any post but this one.

### The patrol

Every patrol Edric leads rides out of the Knights' Court before dawn and comes back, if things go well, before the gates close. Things go well less often than they did. Every season there are more monsters on the roads and bolder ones, creatures that used to scatter at the sound of hooves and now stand their ground. The young riders under him, Aylwin among them, have more courage than experience, and Edric has learned that the best thing he can do for a frightened patrol is make it laugh. He tells the same terrible jokes on every ride. The riders groan at them. That is the point: people who are groaning are not panicking.

He does not joke about the reports. After every patrol he writes down where they met trouble, what it was and how many, and the reports go to the palace, where the queen's consort marks them on his great map of the royal roads, and to his friend Gervase, the cartographer, who is drawing a map of his own of where monsters have been seen. Edric has stood in front of both maps. He does not need them to tell him what he already feels in his bones.

### Friends on the road

His other great friend is Merrick, the caravan master, who prices danger like any other cargo and pays well for fighters because he has to. Edric and Merrick have shared more fires on the road than either can count, and they argue cheerfully about whether the patrol should guard caravans or the caravans should guard themselves. They both know the answer is that there are not enough swords for either.

### What he wants and fears

Edric wants the Court to train faster than the wilds grow worse. He knows it cannot, quite, and that is the tiredness that sleep does not fix. He sees young riders like Aylwin, earnest and brave and fresh from fighting off a pack of boars on the road to Kaldemor, and he is proud of them and afraid for them in the same breath. What he fears most is the day a patrol rides out and he has to write a report that is not about monsters but about one of his own.

A quest might begin with the patrol short a rider on a road that cannot wait, or with a report Edric has not sent to the palace yet because he does not believe what it says.
