Aveline was crowned under the Great Bell sixteen years ago, at twenty-eight, and the Bell has not rung since. She is forty-four now, and she still remembers the sound of it better than anything that was said to her that day. The crowd in the plaza went quiet when it started, and stayed quiet until it stopped, and she understood then that the bell was not ringing for her but for the treaty, and that she had just been handed it to keep.

She has held to that idea ever since. The crown's task, as she likes to put it, is to keep the kingdom together, not to command it. She settles quarrels between towns, keeps the royal roads open, and speaks for Wonder Kingdom when it needs a single voice, but she will not tell Midbarium how to choose its chieftains or Bain Harbour how to run its docks, and she has refused more than one adviser who wanted her to.

### The palace and the people in it

Aveline came to the throne after the long reign of King Bertram, who wore the crown for forty-two years and is still called "the old king" by the palace staff who served him. She runs her court very differently. She listens to everyone to the end, from envoys to kitchen boys, and then decides in a single sentence; Melisende, who advises her whenever two towns fall out over trade, says the sentence is usually the one nobody else thought to say. Osbert, the steward, has kept the household running for longer than she has been queen, and she trusts him with keys she would not give to anyone else.

Her husband, Leofric, was a road surveyor when they met over a disputed map in the Scholars' Quarter, four years before her coronation; she said his road was in the wrong place, and he proved, politely and at length, that it was not. They married twenty years ago. His great map of the royal roads now covers a whole wall of the palace, and every week the two of them stand in front of it and watch the monster marks spread.

Their children are Elinor, nearly grown, and Alaric, who is not. Alaric brings her beetles and rumours, and she takes both seriously. Elinor worries her more. The princess has been coming to breakfast with blistered hands and a look of great innocence, and Aveline, who is very hard to fool, has noticed. She has said nothing yet. She has not decided whether she is angry, proud or simply frightened.

### Allies below and letters from afar

The tie Aveline guards most closely is with Cavelor. It was King Bertram who sent the Great World Tree Expedition fifty years ago, and Aveline has always felt that the crown owes the guardians beneath the Tree a debt it can never fully repay. In the first year of her reign, fifteen years ago, she went down the Upper Stair herself, the first reigning monarch to do so, walked a shift of the Deep Watch beside Marrun, the Keeper of the Oath, and swore an alliance with him in the Oath Hall. The crown promised to answer every message from below; Cavelor promised to send word of every change in the seal. Their letters have come and gone each month since, and she has been down twice more. Marrun is the person outside her family she trusts most, and she fears she is failing him, because she believes every word of his warnings and cannot make the rest of the kingdom believe them half as much.

Her other great correspondent is Rook Tallow of Urbanograd. Nine years ago Rook sent her an indignant letter about Guard patrols that stopped short of the road to Urbanograd, and she answered it herself, three pages long, agreeing with half of it. The patrols were extended the next year, and the letters never stopped. They write roughly every fortnight and argue about nearly everything: guilds, theatre, the Great Bell, whether a kingdom needs a crown at all. They have met only once, on Walkout Day six years ago, when Aveline watched Juna Marrow's play standing in the crowd and Rook found her an upturned crate to sit on. She began writing to Rook long before the Open Table made Rook its Caller, and she likes to say she writes to the person, not the post. She knows Rook reads the funniest parts aloud at the Table, and now writes a few of them on purpose.

### What keeps her awake

The reports from the royal roads keep her up at night. Rhys, whom she made Captain of the Guard eight years ago, has more recruits than ever and less time to train them, and Leofric's map grows more crowded each week. Her fear is not that the towns will turn on one another; she knows they will not. It is that the danger will grow faster than the kingdom's patience, and that one day Marrun will send word that cannot be answered with a letter.

A quest might begin with a sealed message from Cavelor that arrives without its messenger, or with the queen quietly asking someone she trusts to find out who is teaching her daughter the sword.
