Leofric is forty-seven, the queen's consort, and he is happiest with ink on his fingers. The palace staff know to look for him in one of two places: in front of the great map of the royal roads that covers a whole wall of the room beside the queen's study, or bent over a table somewhere with a ruler, a compass and a cold supper he has forgotten to eat. He forgets meals, appointments and occasionally which day it is. He has never once forgotten a road.

### A surveyor's life

He grew up in the Scholars' Quarter, the son of a copyist who made fair copies of other people's maps, and he learned to draw a straight line before he learned to write his name. Both his parents are dead now. At twenty he went out on the royal roads with a young cartographer named Gervase, and the two of them spent the better part of seven years walking, measuring and arguing their way between the towns. Gervase talked and paced and could not bear a blank space on a page; Leofric was quiet and patient and would walk the same mile three times to be sure of it. They made a good pair, and they are still old colleagues and old friends.

It was a road that brought him to the queen. Twenty years ago, in the Scholars' Quarter, a young woman who was not yet queen told him that the road on his map was in the wrong place. Leofric, politely and at considerable length, proved that it was not. She was very hard to fool, and very hard to argue with, and he had done both in one afternoon. They were married before the year was out. Four years later she was crowned under the Great Bell, and Leofric found himself the consort of Wonder Kingdom with no clear idea of what a consort does. He decided, after some thought, that he would keep doing what he was good at.

### The great map

So he made the map. It shows every royal road on Veligor, every town, ford, bridge and milestone, drawn in his small, careful hand, and it took him years. For a long time it was simply the most accurate map in the kingdom. Now it is something else. Each week, the reports come in from Edric, Lieutenant of the Road Patrol, and Leofric marks on the map every place the patrols met monsters: a small red mark for each, with the date beside it in tiny figures.

He does not like how the marks are spreading. They used to cluster in a few wild places. Now they creep along the roads themselves, and each season they reach a little further toward the towns. Every week Queen Aveline comes to stand in front of the map with him, and the two of them look at it for a long time without saying much. Gervase is making his own map of the same thing, across the city in the Scholars' Quarter, and when the two of them compare, the marks agree. Leofric was rather hoping they would not.

### Family

He adores his wife and his children, and is, in the gentlest possible way, a little lost among them. Alaric asks him questions about roads and Leofric answers them at length, with sketches, which Alaric keeps. Elinor is harder. She is bold and restless and stubborn, and Leofric has a feeling there is something she is not telling him, but he is absent-minded about people in a way he never is about maps, and he has not yet worked out what it is.

### What he wants and fears

Leofric wants the roads to be safe enough that the map can go back to being just a map. What he fears is the pattern. He is a surveyor, and he knows a line when he sees one, and the marks on his wall look less and less like scattered trouble and more like something with a direction.

A quest might begin with a stretch of road on the great map that has no recent reports at all, which worries Leofric more than any red mark, or with a survey he needs walked again by someone who can defend themselves.
