Tristan is forty-one, a river scout of the Riverside, and most people who have seen him at work did not notice him at the time. He walks the banks ahead of the barges, a long way ahead, quietly, reading the reeds and the mud and the birds, and when he finds something wrong he warns the barges before they reach it. He is quiet, quick and confident on water and on land, and he would much rather track a beast than fight one. He says a scout who has to fight has already done his job badly.

### The carter's boys

He and his elder brother Edric grew up on their father's cart. Their father hauled goods between the capital and the other towns, and the two boys sat on the bench beside him through most of their childhood, learning milestones, fords and where to water a horse. Their mother kept the house in Riverside, and between journeys the boys came home to it. Both parents are dead now, and the brothers have no other family. Tristan still lives in that house, a narrow place with a door on the lane and a window on the water, and Edric, who sleeps at the Knights' Court more often than not, comes down to it on his rest days.

Edric loved the road. Tristan never did. He found the cart too loud, and he would slip off at the river landings and lie in the reeds while their father watered the horses. An old fowler called Hamund, who netted ducks in the reed beds below the city and has been dead many years, found the boy there one evening and, instead of chasing him off, showed him the track of an otter. Hamund taught him to move without a sound, to read a bank like a page, and to know what had passed by from the shape of the reeds it bent. By the time Edric joined the Guard, Tristan was earning his keep scouting for any barge that would pay him.

### Lucan's scout

For nine years now he has worked for Lucan, the bargemaster, who runs three barges on the river and has never hired anyone to do anything quietly before or since. They are an odd pair. Lucan booms, bets on everything and greets every landing he passes; Tristan says perhaps a dozen words a day, and Lucan listens to every one of them. Lucan trusts him completely, and it is the only thing on the river he does not make a wager on. Tristan, for his part, trusts Lucan to turn a barge when he is told to, which is rarer than it sounds.

River travel has grown busy now that the roads are dangerous, and Tristan's work has grown with it. There are more creatures on the banks than there were, and bolder ones, and the water itself has started to keep secrets. Tristan was on the bank the day one of Lucan's hands came out of the river with a bruise on his leg in the shape of something that had gripped him, and he helped carry the man to Aldith's door.

### The Long Reach

A half-day upstream of the city, below the quarry landing, the river runs deep and slow for a long stretch between high banks. The bargehands call it the Long Reach. Early this spring Tristan found a track there: a broad swathe of reeds pressed flat from the top of the bank down into the deep water, as wide as a cart, with nothing at either end of it. The herons have gone from that reach. Once, watching a loaded barge pass over the deepest pool, he saw the water behind it rise in a slow, smooth hump, as if something underneath had turned to follow, and then settle.

Since then he has asked Lucan to pass that stretch at speed, every time, and he has not said why. He is not sure what he saw, and he will not say what he is not sure of. He also knows that Lucan would either bet on it or tell the whole river by nightfall, and that the bargehands are frightened enough already. He has told Edric. The brothers have always compared notes on how the wilds are changing, road against river, and lately their notes match more than either of them likes.

### What he wants and fears

Tristan wants to know what made that track before anyone has to find out the hard way. What he fears is the day a barge cannot go fast enough, and the day he has to tell Lucan the truth because it is too late to keep it.

A quest might begin with Tristan asking someone steady to watch the Long Reach with him through a night, without a lantern, or with Bennet's new fast barge needing to be tested on exactly the stretch of river where speed matters most.
