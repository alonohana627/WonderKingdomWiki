Rowena is thirty-four, a Swordmaster of the Royal Guard, and she teaches the advanced classes at the Knights' Court. She is cool, precise and sparing with praise. She corrects a stance with two fingers on a shoulder and a single word, and she rarely needs a second one. When she smiles, which is not often, the recruit she smiles at remembers it for a week, and the rest of the yard talks about it for two.

### The locksmith's daughter

She grew up in the city's inner streets, the only child of a locksmith, and she spent her childhood at his bench learning how small, exact movements open things that force cannot. Both her parents are dead now. She says her father taught her everything she knows about the sword without ever holding one: patience, a light touch, and that the right moment is narrower than people think.

She joined the Guard at seventeen, seventeen years ago, and served her first years on the walls and at the palace gates before she was moved to the Court. She was not the strongest recruit in her intake, nor the fastest. She was the most exact. She earned the Swordsman's rank in good time and the Swordmaster's at twenty-four, ten years ago, which was young for it, and Captain Rhys gave her the advanced classes not long after he took command. He trusts her judgment as he trusts his own, and says so, and she has never given him cause to regret it. She has never married. She says she has a yard full of people who need her attention more than any husband would.

### The advanced classes

Her pupils are the Guard's best: Swordsmen working toward the Swordmaster's trial, and the few Swordmasters reaching for more. She drives them hard and quietly. Drill Sergeant Bertrand bellows the recruits into shape in the main yard; Rowena takes what he has made and refines it, in a corner of the Court where her voice never needs to carry. She teaches that a sword is not swung but placed. Rhys says a sword is only as good as the arm behind it, and Rowena adds, when he is out of earshot, that the arm is only as good as the eye.

She arrives at the practice yard before anyone else, and leaves it after most. She has noticed the highland recruit Avice, who comes earlier every week, and she has noticed that Avice has noticed things.

### The stranger at dawn

Last autumn a young woman in a plain grey cloak came to her at dawn and asked, with her hood up and her name unspoken, for private lessons. Rowena agreed without asking for the name. She did not need to. She had stood guard at the palace gates for her first years in the Guard and had watched the princess grow up across the palace yards, running after horses and swordsmen. She knew the walk. She knew the voice. And the cloak, plain as it looked, was finer wool than any recruit could buy, with palace stitching at the hem.

She knows exactly whose daughter Elinor is. She has told no one, not even Rhys. Elinor believes the disguise works, and Rowena has let her believe it, because Elinor wants to earn her sword like anyone else, and Rowena thinks that is exactly how a sword ought to be earned. She teaches the cloaked stranger as hard as she teaches anyone, harder if anything, and she does not go easy on blistered hands. When Elinor comes to the trials, Rowena means to make sure nobody makes them easier for her.

She is aware that she is keeping a secret from the queen, about the queen's own daughter, and that if Elinor is hurt in her yard there will be no explaining it. She has decided she would rather answer for that than send the girl away to learn from someone worse.

### What she wants and fears

Rowena wants to see one pupil of hers go further than she has, and she has begun to suspect which one it will be. What she fears is the morning the queen sends for her and asks a direct question, because Rowena does not lie, and she has not yet decided what she will say.

A quest might begin with Rowena needing a sparring partner for the cloaked stranger who will not hold back and will not ask questions, or with the stranger failing to appear at dawn for the first time since autumn.
