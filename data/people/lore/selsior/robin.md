Robin is nine years old, a mudlark of the Riverside, and he has never met a shiny thing he did not suspect of being a lost crown. When the river runs low he is down on the bank before anyone else, barefoot, with a bucket, a bent spoon for digging and the absolute certainty that today is the day. It never quite is. He has found buttons, buckles, coins from the old king's reign, a silver thimble, three keys to nothing, and more broken crockery than anyone could want. Every single piece, for a moment, was treasure.

### A river family

Robin comes from a family that lives by the river and on it. His grandfather Osmund has poled the ferry across since he was a boy and speaks mostly in nods; Robin is the only person who claims to understand every one of them. His mother Agatha fishes the river with nets that were once Osmund's, and his father Silas sells her catch, and fish from both harbours besides, at a shout in the Crown Market, naming every one as he goes. Robin grew up in the boat, on the bank and under the market stall, and he smells of river most of the time and is not sorry about it.

He is curious about everything, muddy almost always and generous with his finds. His mother is cheerful in any weather and his father is cheerful in any market, and Robin is cheerful in any mud, so the house is a loud and happy one. His parents let him mudlark on two conditions: never alone at the deep end of the bank, and never after dark. The second condition is newer than the first. It came in about the time his grandfather stopped crossing after dark himself.

### The curio dealer

Robin sells the best of his finds to Jasper, the curio dealer in the Crown Market, who sells strange inventions from Urbanograd and buys almost anything with a story attached. Jasper is excitable and talkative and a dreadful salesman, and he pays Robin a little more than the finds are worth, partly because he likes the boy and partly because he cannot resist a story. Robin, in turn, tells him exactly where each thing came out of the mud and spins the rest. They get on very well. Robin thinks Jasper's shop is the best place in the whole city, and he would very much like to see inside the one box Jasper will not open in public.

### The badge

Last spring, at the lowest water of the year, Robin dug an old badge out of the mud below the ferry landing. It is about the size of a large coin, heavy, crusted with river and stamped with a seal. When he had cleaned it he took it straight to Jasper, who turned it over for a long time and admitted, for perhaps the first time in his life, that he had no idea. It is not the crown's seal, nor any town's that Jasper knows, nor any guild's. Osmund looked at it and nodded once, which Robin says meant "put it somewhere safe".

So Robin did. He keeps it under his pillow, and he has not sold it, though Jasper has made him two offers. He is sure it belonged to someone important. On some nights he is sure it is the start of a lost crown. On others, when the river is loud, he is less sure he wants to know.

### What he wants and fears

Robin wants to find something that really matters, and to be the one who knows what it is. What he fears, though he would not say so, is the deep water. His mother's nets have been coming up torn, his grandfather will not cross at night, and Robin has started to watch the river the way they do.

A quest might begin with someone recognising the seal on Robin's badge, or with Robin finding a second one.
