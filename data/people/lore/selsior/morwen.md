Morwen is fifty-four and teaches young mages at the Royal Academy, and the first thing she tells every new class is that magic is mostly patience. The second thing she tells them, usually within the hour, is to stop showing off. She is intense, precise and impatient with anything flashy, and her students are a little afraid of her for about a year, after which the good ones discover that she is the best teacher in the Scholars' Quarter and the rest discover that she remembers everything.

### An old Magician family

Her family has been in Selsior since the Academy was founded by the city's first Magicians, or so the family says; Morwen, who is precise about such things, points out that nobody can prove it and that it hardly matters. What the family did keep, for certain, is a habit of collecting. For generations they gathered whatever survived of the old Magician craft from the Balrog War: notes, fragments of spells, half-copied diagrams, letters that mention a working and then break off. Her grandmother Eluned, who raised her, kept them in a chest at the foot of her bed and read them aloud to Morwen as other grandmothers read stories. Morwen grew up knowing the shape of the seal-craft before she knew her letters, and knowing just as early that most of it was missing.

Eluned died when Morwen was twenty-six, the year Morwen began teaching at the Academy, twenty-eight years ago. The chest is in Morwen's rooms now, and it is a great deal fuller than it was. She has no husband and no children; she has the chest, her classes and the work.

### The work

Morwen studies the old seal-craft, the Magician arts that went into weaving the seal over the Balrog at the foot of the World Tree. Nobody alive has seen that seal made, and the records are scattered, fragmentary and frequently written by people who expected their readers to know the parts they left out. She is trying to put them back together, one piece at a time, in the hope that someone will one day need it. She does not know how the seal was finished, or how it might be mended, and she says so plainly to anyone who asks. She does know more than anyone else in Selsior about how it was begun, and lately she has noticed that more people are asking.

She reads the Magician accounts of the Balrog, the ones that call it something like a wound in the world, and she does not say whether she believes them. What she will say is that a wound is a thing that can reopen, and that the news from the roads, and the rumours of warnings from Cavelor, have made her work feel less like history every year.

### Teacher and friend

Her oldest friend is Bartholomew, the Master of the Academy, who has known her since she was a young teacher with very firm opinions and who still answers her questions with better questions, which she finds infuriating and has come to rely on. They argue over supper about once a week and would each defend the other to the death.

Her present despair is Everard, who came from a harbour town, sleeps through her lectures, spends his evenings on Theatre Row and passes every exam she sets him. She is convinced he could be a great mage if he tried. She is not wrong, and she has not yet understood that this is exactly what frightens him.

Her old regret is Constance, who studied under her for five years and was the most careful student she ever had, and who left in her final year to take up the road. Morwen told her at the door that she would be back for the books one day. Constance comes back to borrow them fairly often. Morwen lends them every time and has never once said she is glad to see her, which Constance, being clever, understands perfectly.

### Hooks

A quest might begin with a fragment missing from Morwen's chest, the one piece of seal-craft she has never shown anyone. Or it might begin with Rosamund of the Gilded Stage arriving at her door, wanting to learn how a Magician really moves when she casts, and Morwen, against every instinct, deciding to teach her.
