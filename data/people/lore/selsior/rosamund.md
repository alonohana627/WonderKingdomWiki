Rosamund is forty, the leading lady of the Gilded Stage and the most famous actress in Selsior, and she knows it, and she would like you to know that she knows it. She can cry on cue, faint on cue and make a full house hold its breath by doing nothing at all for a count of ten. Off stage she is something people do not expect from a woman with her name painted twice the size of anyone else's on the playbills: she laughs at herself, loudly and often, and she is fiercely, unshakeably loyal to the people she loves.

### From the old quarters to the Row

She grew up in the old quarters, the daughter of a glover and a seamstress, both dead now, and she was performing before she could read: on doorsteps, on barrels, on the steps of the Great Bell's dais until the guards moved her along. At seventeen, twenty-three years ago, she walked into the Gilded Stage and asked Lavinia for a part. Lavinia, who was then still playing every great part herself, gave her a line and a half as a maid who brings in a letter. Rosamund made the line and a half so memorable that the audience applauded the letter. Lavinia has never quite forgiven her, and has given her the best parts in the house ever since.

She is vain, and freely admits it; she has a mirror in her dressing room that she talks to. But she works harder than anyone on the Row, she knows every line in every play she is in, and she has never once let a younger actor fall flat on a stage she was standing on.

### Crispin and Marigold

She married Crispin, the house playwright, twelve years ago, after three years of watching him start sentences he never finished and finding, to her surprise, that she wanted to hear the ends of them. She is the only person on Theatre Row who can make him finish one, usually by waiting. She reads every draft he writes, aloud, in every voice, and she tells him which lines land on the third beat and which do not land at all. He says she is the best editor in the city. She says she is simply the person who has to say them.

Their daughter Marigold is ten and has been on stage since she could walk; Rosamund carried her on as a baby in a basket in the second act of The Baker's Wager, and Marigold has refused to leave the stage since. She knows everyone's lines better than they do and is very sure of herself, and Rosamund, who recognises the type, is torn between pride and alarm.

### The Magician and Percival

Crispin's new play is about the Balrog War, and Rosamund is determined to play the play's Magician, who stands for all the Magicians who wove the seal together. Crispin had always imagined it as a quiet part. Rosamund does not do quiet parts, and with every draft it gets less quiet. She wants it because it is the person who ends the war, not the person who waits for someone else to, and because she is forty and has played enough sweethearts for one lifetime.

She will not, under any circumstances, share the stage with Percival in it. They have quarrelled on and off the stage for ten years, ever since Lavinia first cast them opposite each other, and audiences love it, and Lavinia sells tickets on it, and Rosamund is tired of it. Percival wants to play the play's Savage warrior, who stands for the whole front line that held the Balrog at the Tree. Crispin cannot see how to write the Last Stand without both of them in it. Rosamund says that is his problem. Privately she admits that Percival is a very good actor, and that this is the most annoying thing about him.

### What she wants and fears

Rosamund wants the part, and she wants to play it so well that people stop playing the Magician as a legend and start playing her as a person. What she fears is that Lavinia has bet everything on this play, that Crispin cannot find his ending, and that she will not be able to save either of them by being brilliant.

### Hooks

A quest might begin with Rosamund wanting someone to find out how a Magician really works a spell, from someone who would know, so that she can play it properly. Or it might begin with a forged note, apparently from Percival, that very nearly starts their worst quarrel yet.
