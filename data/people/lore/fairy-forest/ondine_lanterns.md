Ondine is Keeper of the Lanterns, and every light on every path, bridge and stair in Fairy Forest is, one way or another, her responsibility. She leads the lantern-makers who make, hang and tend them, and she can tell which of her lanterns is dimming from across the forest. Nobody quite knows how. She says she simply looks, and that anyone else could if they bothered. She is sixty-eight, exacting and proud, and secretly sentimental, a secret she keeps very badly from everyone except herself.

### The workshop

Ondine grew up on the Lantern Paths and came to the lantern workshop at fourteen, as a sweeper of glass dust for old Keeper Lisbet, who made the lanterns before her. Lisbet taught her to cut glass, to mix glow-oil, to seal a lantern against rain and to hang it so it would not swing in a storm, and above all to look at a stretch of dark path and ask what the person walking it would need to see. When Lisbet died thirty-one years ago, the workshop and the title passed to Ondine. She has made the forest's lanterns ever since, and she still sweeps her own glass dust at the end of every day, because she does not trust anyone else to find every splinter.

She was eighteen when the Great World Tree Expedition came home, and she remembers the outer paths as they were then: a few soft lanterns for mushroom-pickers and courting couples, lit late and left to gutter. That is the forest she was taught to light.

### The outer paths

For the last nineteen years, Ondine has made the lanterns along the outer paths a little brighter every autumn. She began quietly, after an edge guard told her that something had come right up to the last lantern and stopped. Now the outer lights are the strongest in the forest, and the edge watch walks among them at night with spears. Ondine keeps a book in her workshop recording every increase, year by year and lantern by lantern. She has never shown it to the Heart Tree. She does not like what the numbers say, and she does not like that nobody has asked her for them.

### Faelan

Her apprentice is Faelan, who has broken more lantern glass than any apprentice she has ever taken, and she has told him so, often. Five years ago, at sixteen, he talked his way into her workshop by describing a lantern he wanted to make one day. She told him the idea was impossible and took him on the same afternoon. She has never told him why: it was the first time in years that anyone had described a light by how it would make a person feel.

He is clumsy and he hums, but he has the best eye for colour the workshop has known, and lately she has started asking his opinion about the glass for the outer paths. She knows he is saving for a lantern of his own design, and suspects it is for Linnet, the young treewright he walks out with. She has said nothing. She has simply started leaving the off-cuts of good green glass on his bench. She also keeps, in a box under her own bench, the prettiest of the coloured panes he has broken, because the colours were right even when his hands were not.

### Vesper

Her daughter Vesper sells the workshop's lanterns, and her own travelling lights, from the shop at the front. Vesper has none of her mother's patience and all of her skill, and the two of them argue about the shop's prices every single day. Ondine thinks Vesper charges too much for travelling lights to people who have no choice but to use the roads after dark. Vesper thinks her mother charges too little for everything else, and that sentiment does not pay for glass. Each is right about half of it, which is why the argument never ends. Ondine is prouder of Vesper's travelling lights than she has ever said aloud.

### What she wants and fears

Ondine wants every path in the forest lit well enough that nobody walking it is afraid. She fears that one autumn she will make the outer lanterns brighter and it will not be enough, and she fears, too, the day she has nothing left to make them brighter with.

A quest might ask a traveller to replace a dimming lantern on a path too far out for the workshop to reach safely, to fetch a rare mineral for a stronger glow-oil, or to carry Ondine's book of lantern brightnesses to someone at the Heart Tree who ought to read it.
