Ailo helps in the Herb Gardens, the terraced beds and glasshouse-hollows below the Heart Tree where Fairy Forest grows its healing plants. Ailo is one of the Children of the Tree, and a Garden Helper by title, though anyone who has watched Ailo at work would say the title undersells it. Ailo is calm, unhurried and deeply content, can work for hours without a word, and seems to enjoy every one of those hours. Weeding, watering, staking beans, carrying compost up the terraces: Ailo does all of it at the same steady pace, and somehow it is always finished by the time it needs to be.

### The gardens

Ailo came to the Herb Gardens to help, and stayed. Hesper, the Mistress of the Herb Gardens, who is blunt and kind and smells faintly of mint, took Ailo on the way she takes on anyone willing to be at work in the beds before breakfast, and has never once had cause to regret it. Hesper says the gardens have never grown so well. The seedlings come up stronger, the slow herbs come up sooner, and plants she had given up on in the glasshouse-hollows have started to flower.

Hesper has asked Ailo why, more than once. Ailo only smiles. Hesper, who is used to being answered rather than smiled at, has decided for now to be grateful instead of curious. Others in the forest have asked too, and they have all received the same smile, and gone away no wiser. Whether there is any secret at all, or whether Ailo simply works carefully and well and takes pleasure in it, nobody can say, and Ailo has never said.

### Merrow

Ailo's closest friend is Merrow, the gardener, who does the digging, weeding and watering that keeps the gardens alive. Merrow is big-hearted and slow-talking, and fiercely protective of his seedling beds, and he found in Ailo the first companion he had ever worked beside without wishing they would talk less. The two of them can spend a whole morning on one terrace and part at noon having said ten words between them, both perfectly content. Merrow has never asked Ailo why the gardens grow so well. He thinks it would be rude. Ailo appreciates that more than Merrow knows.

Ailo knows that Merrow is afraid of the Forest Edge, and that his wife Celandine, the herbalist at the remedy stall, keeps wanting to go there for a plant that grows nowhere else. Ailo has heard Merrow beg her not to go and has heard her answer, and has stayed out of it, as a friend should. But Ailo has started, quietly, to learn everything the gardens' old books say about plants that grow near the Edge.

### The seedling beds

Lately something has been digging up Merrow's seedling beds at night. Nobody knows what. Merrow comes in at dawn to find holes in the rows and seedlings scattered, and his big face goes very still. Ailo has said nothing about it either, but most mornings now Ailo is in the gardens before Merrow, working along the disturbed beds and setting the seedlings back one by one, so that by the time Merrow arrives the damage is half mended. Merrow has noticed. Neither of them has mentioned it.

### What Ailo wants and fears

Ailo wants the gardens to go on growing, and wants Merrow, Celandine and Hesper to go on being exactly who they are. If Ailo fears anything, it is hard to tell, because Ailo is not one for complaining; but those who know Ailo best have seen Ailo pause, now and then, at the gate of the gardens in the evening, and look out toward the outer paths for a long moment before going home.

A quest might ask a traveller to sit up one night with Ailo and see what comes to Merrow's seedling beds, to fetch a plant from near the Forest Edge so that Celandine need not go, or to try, once more, to get Ailo to say why the gardens grow so well, which will almost certainly fail.
