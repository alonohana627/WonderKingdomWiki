Sefa is the Glade Storyteller, one of the Children of the Tree, and every afternoon in the Children's Glade there is a ring of the forest's children sitting in the grass in front of Sefa, waiting to hear what happens next. Sefa is warm, playful and a natural entertainer, with a voice for every character and a pause in exactly the right place. Sefa loves an audience. But anyone who has watched Sefa for long knows that Sefa loves listening even more.

### Trading stories

Sefa does not simply tell stories; Sefa trades them. A child who wants a story must bring one, and almost anything counts: a dream, a rumour from school, what a grandmother said about the winter the river froze, the reason a particular bridge on the Lantern Paths creaks. Sefa listens to every one of them with complete attention, asks questions nobody else would think to ask, and then pays for it with a story of Sefa's own, which is usually better and sometimes, mysteriously, about the very thing the child just described.

Lumi, one of the Children and a student at the Grove School, comes to the glade most days with the news from school, and always asks afterwards whether Sefa's story was true. Sefa never says. Lumi has decided this is the best game in the forest and is keeping score. Sefa is keeping score too, and has not told Lumi what of.

Not long ago Lumi asked, without saying why, what a friend should do when a friend has a dangerous secret. Sefa answered with a story about two foxes and a frozen pond. Lumi went away to think about it and has been thinking about it ever since. Sefa knows exactly what the story meant, and has decided that Lumi will understand it best by working it out alone.

### Aspen

Most afternoons, once the glade has emptied, Sefa walks to the Dewdrop Tea House and meets Aspen, the retired treewright, by the window. The two of them trade stories over a pot of Tansy's tea for as long as the light lasts. Aspen tells about the day news of the expedition came, the bridge he nearly fell off, the forest gathered under the Heart Tree arguing all night, and he improves a different part every time. Sefa notices every improvement and loves them all. Aspen suspects that Sefa is collecting his stories. He is right, and he is flattered, and he would very much like someone to write them down properly before he forgets which parts he made better.

### The great collection

Sefa is gathering the forest's tales into one great collection: the children's stories, the old people's memories, the songs and sayings and half-remembered rhymes, the stories travellers bring in off the roads, and Sefa's own. It is kept on bundles of bark-paper in Sefa's home in the glade, and there are a great many bundles now. Sefa says it is the forest's memory, and that a forest which forgets its stories forgets itself.

Sefa refuses to say which of the stories in it are Sefa's own. Asked directly, Sefa only smiles and offers to tell another one. Some people in the forest find this maddening. Most have decided it does not matter, because the stories are good. A few have tried to catch Sefa out by bringing in a story of their own invention, to see whether it turns up in the collection, and found it there a week later, much improved, with no sign of whose it had been.

### What Sefa wants and fears

Sefa wants every story in the forest gathered before it is lost, and wants an audience for each one. Sefa fears, though the glade would never guess it, that some stories are being forgotten faster than they can be found, as the old people who knew them grow fewer.

A quest might ask a traveller to bring Sefa a story from a far town, to find an old person on the forest's lower paths who knows a tale nobody else remembers, or to work out which of Sefa's stories are true, though Sefa will never say whether the answer is right.
