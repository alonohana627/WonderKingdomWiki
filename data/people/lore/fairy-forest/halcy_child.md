Halcy weaves baskets and glade-flower garlands in the Children's Glade, and sells them to anyone who stops to admire them, which is nearly everyone. Halcy is kind, practical and welcoming, and remembers everyone's name after a single meeting: the name, the face, and usually the name of whatever the person was carrying at the time. Visitors who wander into the glade unsure whether they are welcome tend to leave an hour later with a garland and the feeling that they have been expected all along.

### Among the first

Halcy was among the first of the Children of the Tree to come down and settle in Fairy Forest, in the days when a Child on the Lantern Paths was still a rare sight. Halcy does not talk much about life in the Tree, and when asked, usually answers with a question about the asker's own home instead. Whether that is privacy, politeness or something else, nobody in the forest has quite decided. Old Aspen, who helped coax homes for some of those first arrivals, says Halcy was the one who asked the most questions about how everything worked, and remembered every answer.

What Halcy will talk about, at length, is the forest: which paths flood in spring, which lantern-maker to ask for a lamp that will not frighten a newcomer, where the quietest benches are, and who in the Herb Gardens will give honest advice about a cough. Halcy learned all of it the slow way, and decided early that nobody coming after should have to.

### The guide

So when newer Children come down from the Tree, it is usually Halcy who meets them and helps them find their way. Halcy finds them a place to sleep, introduces them to the right neighbours, explains the forest's customs, and stays close until they are ready to manage alone. Niv, who is shy, gentle and easily overwhelmed by crowds, was one of these, and Halcy still checks on Niv often. Halcy introduced Niv to Oriel, the master treewright, whose tended tree home Niv now lives in, and was glad to see the two of them become friends. Halcy has noticed Niv standing at the edge of the glade looking toward the World Tree, and has decided not to ask why until Niv is ready to say.

### The Eldest's friend

Calyra counts Halcy as a friend. They met long before she was Eldest, when Halcy began coming to sit quietly at the back of her listening lessons at the Grove School, not to learn magic, but to learn how the forest's people learned. Calyra noticed, said nothing for a month, and then asked Halcy's opinion of her teaching. Halcy gave it honestly. They have been friends ever since.

Now Calyra sometimes visits the glade and asks Halcy's advice over a basket of flowers. She says Halcy notices things about the forest that its oldest Magicians stopped seeing long ago. Halcy's advice is usually short and practical, and Calyra usually takes it.

### Trade

Halcy's best customer is Tansy at the Dewdrop Tea House, who buys garlands for every table and baskets to carry her honey cakes, every week without fail. Halcy delivers them personally, partly because Tansy always has a cup of dew tea waiting and partly because the tea house is the best place in the forest to meet newcomers.

### Hooks

Lately Halcy has been weaving more garlands than the market needs, and setting the spares aside without explanation. A quest might ask a traveller to help Halcy settle a newcomer who is struggling, to carry a basket and a quiet message to the Heart Tree, or to gather a flower that grows only in a corner of the forest Halcy can no longer safely reach.
