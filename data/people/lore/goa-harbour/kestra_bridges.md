Kestra is twenty-three and strings the rope bridges that run across the clefts of the cliff above Goa Harbour, from the low spans over the quay to the highest bridge just below the clifftop quarter. She is fearless, light-footed and impatient with anyone who looks down. She can cross any bridge in the town blindfold, and has, more than once, to win a bet. She will not wear a safety line, and she does not intend to start.

### Rope and stone

Kestra grew up in a cliff house above the Inlet Quay. Her mother, Shona, made nets in the coves and taught her every knot she knew before Kestra could write her name. Shona died of a fever when Kestra was fifteen, and the girl, who had already been spending every day clinging to the bridges, simply stayed up there. Gorm, the Master of the Stairs, had taken her onto his crew the year before as a rope-carrier, mostly to stop her climbing things without permission. Within two years she was rigging bridges of her own, and within five she was the best rigger the crew had.

Gorm is proud of her and shouts at her constantly, mostly about the safety line. Kestra shouts back. She thinks a line is something to trip on, and that the way to be safe on a rope bridge is to know the bridge, every plank and every knot, so well that your feet never need to ask. Ruan, the Captain of the Cliff Watch, tests every rope on the cliffs once a month by hanging his weight off it, and he tests hers twice. Kestra finds this insulting and has told him so. He has thanked her and gone on doing it.

### Her day

Most mornings Kestra walks every bridge in the town, end to end, running her hands along the ropes and listening to the planks. She replaces what is worn before it frays and what is frayed before anyone notices. In the afternoons she splices new rope at the top of the Cliff Stairs, and in the evenings she is often to be found at the Lantern Market, at the back of Gunna's workshop, where Tarran is.

Tarran, the lantern-maker's apprentice, is her sweetheart. He is clumsy and full of ideas, and she is neither, and they suit each other better than anyone expected. She is the only person who has never laughed at one of his lanterns that did not light. He is the only person she lets watch her cross a bridge blindfold, because he is too busy worrying to look down.

### The chewed ropes

This spring, on a morning walk, Kestra found the ropes of the highest bridge chewed. Not frayed, not cut: chewed, from below, with the strands bitten through one by one on the seaward side, where nothing should be able to reach. She spliced them that day. Since then she has found fresh damage again and again, always on the highest bridge, always from below, and never once has she seen what does it.

Kestra means to catch it. She has sat out on the highest bridge after dark, which Gorm has forbidden and Ruan does not know about, waiting with a coil of rope and a long knife. Nothing came while she watched. Tarran has promised to make her a lantern bright enough to see the whole cliff face beneath the bridge, and she is waiting for it with more patience than she has ever shown for anything.

She does not know that Fionn, the stair runner, crossed that bridge one evening a few days before she found the first damage, and saw something pale moving fast on the rock below it. If she did, she would want to know exactly what he saw, and she would want to know it now.

### What she fears

Kestra fears very little on a bridge. What frightens her is the idea that someone else, someone who does not know the ropes as she does, will step onto the highest bridge on the night it finally gives.

Kestra also teaches **Rope Slide**, the bridge-builder's quick way down a rope or ladder and safely off its end, to anyone who will help her check every lashing on one of her bridges first.

### Hooks

A quest might sit out on the highest bridge with Kestra after dark, persuade Fionn to tell her what he saw, or carry Tarran's new lantern up to her before he can change his mind about it.
