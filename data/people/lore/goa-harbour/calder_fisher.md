Calder is forty, a cove fisher, and the calm half of the best boat in the Fishers' Coves. He is quiet, patient and endlessly mending something: a net, a rowlock, a child's sandal, a neighbour's gate. While he works he hums the old camp songs, the ones from the days when Savage hunters and Magicians first fished the inlet together, and he hums them so constantly that people in the coves only notice when he stops.

### The songs and the boy

Calder's parents both died in the same hard fever winter when he was five, and he was raised by his grandmother, Morven, in a stone cottage above the far end of the coves. Morven knew every camp song there was and sang them at the fire, at the nets and at the gutting table, and Calder learned them the way other children learn to walk. She died when he was twenty. He still sings her verses, a few of which nobody else in Goa Harbour seems to know, though Cormac at the Lantern Market has tried more than once to coax them out of him over a cup at the Gap Inn.

As a boy Calder spent more time on the Inlet Quay than in the coves, because that was where the boats were built, and that was where he met Tamsin. She was a fierce, skinny girl who wanted to know how everything fitted together; he was the boy who would hold the other end of the plank for as long as she needed. They have been friends ever since. These days she builds and rebuilds Goa Harbour's race boat, the Gap Runner, and Calder is the friend she complains to when the winter rebuild is going badly, which is every winter.

### Sorcha and the children

Calder married Sorcha thirteen years ago. She is loud and he is quiet, she finds the fish and he mends whatever broke in the finding, and between them they bring in the best catch of any boat in the town. People who do not know them wonder how he puts up with the shouting. People who do know them have seen him make her laugh until she has to sit down.

Their daughter Eilidh is ten and bossy and already reads the tide from the cove rocks every evening; their son Ronan is seven and dreamy and always somewhere he is not supposed to be. Calder is the parent Ronan shows his treasures to, because Calder listens. He has seen the smooth stone Ronan keeps hidden in his pocket, once, and promised not to tell. He has kept the promise, though it worries him that the stone is always faintly warm.

### The bigger boat

Calder wants to build a bigger boat one day, a proper deep-water boat with room for a crew, and race it around Veligor himself. He has drawn it a hundred times on the backs of tally sheets. Tamsin has looked at the drawings and told him exactly what is wrong with each, which from Tamsin is high praise, and she has promised to help him build it once the Gap Runner finally beats Bain Harbour. He knows Sorcha has been setting aside a little of every good catch toward it. He has not told her he knows.

### What he fears

Calder is not a worrier by nature, but the sea has turned rougher, and he has started to hum less on the water. His mother-in-law Agna finds the nets torn by something with teeth far bigger than any fish, and he has mended those tears himself and measured them with his hands. He does not say what he thinks they mean.

### Hooks

A quest might help Calder gather timber for his boat, track down someone who remembers the rest of one of Morven's half-forgotten verses, or quietly find out what Ronan's warm stone is without breaking a promise.
