Jory is twenty and works the Inlet Quay, hauling timber, wool and anything else that needs carrying from the landings into the holds of the ships. He is loud, loyal and tireless. He can be heard from one end of the quay to the other, usually talking about the race, and he can carry a bale of highland wool on each shoulder while he does it. Last summer he crewed the Gap Runner for the first time, and nobody on the quay has heard the end of it since.

### A quay boy

Jory was born on the Inlet Quay, the son of a porter who carried cargo there for thirty years until her back gave out. She has gone to live with her sister on a farm far inland, and writes to him in a big, careful hand that he reads aloud to anyone nearby. Jory took over her work at fourteen and found that he was good at it. He knows which captains pay on time, which mates count every bale twice, and how to swing a log off a timber raft and onto a deck without anyone noticing it was ever heavy.

When he is not hauling cargo he works in Tamsin's boatyard, and she works him harder than any captain. He scrapes, sands, carries planks and holds the other end of whatever she is fitting, for as long as she needs, without a word of complaint. Tamsin once told him, by accident, that he was the best thing to happen to the boat in years. He pretended not to hear, so that she would not take it back, and he has thought about it every day since.

### The Gap Runner

Iska picked Jory for the race crew last summer because he had turned up at the yard every evening for two winters, and because, she said, he was the only person on the quay who could row all day and still be cheerful at the end of it. The Gap Runner lost to Bain Harbour, as she had every summer since Tamsin built her. Jory does not care. He talks about the race as though it were the greatest day in the history of Goa Harbour: the send-off, with Mhairi fiddling on the quay; the gap at dawn; the long haul around Veligor; the moment he saw Bain Harbour's sail ahead and knew they could catch it, if only the course had been a little longer. Every time he tells it, the course gets a little shorter and Bain Harbour's lead a little smaller.

Iska is his hero in a way she is nobody else's. He would row around Veligor alone if she asked him to, and he has said so in her hearing, which she pretended not to enjoy. He would never question one of her calls. When Tamsin starts on the subject of a certain tack two summers ago, before Jory's time, he finds something urgent to carry.

### Friends and hopes

Kor, the youngest of the full sea wardens, is Jory's friend from boyhood on the quay. Jory knows Kor wants a place in the race crew more than anything, because Kor asks him about every stroke of last summer's race and then goes very quiet. Jory has decided, privately, that if Iska ever has to choose between them, he will give up his own seat. He has told no one this, least of all Kor.

He wants the Gap Runner to win more than he wants anything for himself. He wants to see Tamsin's face when she does, and to be at the oars when it happens. He fears being left out of this summer's crew, and he fears, much more quietly, the way the cargo ships have started to come in from the gap with their crews pale and silent, and the way the captains now drink their first cup at the Gap Inn without saying a word.

### Hooks

A quest might help Jory haul a heavy timber cargo onto a ship before the tide turns, or take his place on the quay for a week so he can train on the Gap Runner. Another might row with him in a practice run around the headlands, on a day when the water does not behave.
