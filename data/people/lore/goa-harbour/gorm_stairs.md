Gorm is forty-six and the Master of the Stairs, the head of the builders who carve and mend the stairways and rope bridges that climb Goa Harbour's cliffs. He is stubborn, proud and secretly sentimental. He talks about stone the way other people talk about family: this step is sulking, that landing has never forgiven the winter, the old stair above the quay is getting tired and wants looking after. Strangers laugh. People who have lived on the cliffs for a few years stop laughing, because Gorm is usually right.

### Learning the stone

Gorm was born in a cliff house halfway up the Long Stair, the oldest stairway in the town, which climbs from the Inlet Quay to the top of the cliffs. His father was a quarryman who cut stone for the quay walls and died of a fall when Gorm was thirteen. The boy spent the next winter sitting on the steps outside the house, refusing to go anywhere, until Ulric the stonecarver sat down beside him one morning, put a chisel in his hand, and told him that if he was going to sit on a step all day he might as well learn how it was made.

Ulric taught him everything: how to read the grain in a cliff face, where a stair wants to go and where it will fight you, how to cut a step that drains in the rain and holds in the frost. Gorm became Master of the Stairs eleven years ago, when Ulric decided his own hands were better spent on the hardest steps than on arguing with crews. Gorm still goes to Ulric with every difficult piece of rock, and still pretends he is only asking for a second opinion. He has walked past Ulric's little carved animals on a thousand steps and loves every one of them. It has never occurred to him to read them in order.

### The crews and the rival

Gorm's builders are a small, loud crew of stone-cutters and riggers. The best of them is Kestra, who strings the rope bridges across the clefts of the cliff above the harbour and crosses them blindfold. Gorm is proud of her and shouts at her constantly, mostly because she will not wear a safety line. Lately something has been chewing the ropes on the highest bridge, and Kestra means to catch it. Gorm means to make sure she does not get killed trying, and has not yet worked out how.

His friendly rival is Hamish, who works the rope lift from the quay to the top of the cliffs. Gorm calls lifts lazy. He says a town that stops climbing its own stairs will forget how they were made. Hamish says Gorm just cannot bear anything that goes up the cliff without a step under it. They have had this argument at the Gap Inn for years, and both enjoy it far too much to settle it. Gorm is very fond of Hamish's nephew Fionn, who runs messages on the stairs and knows shortcuts that Gorm himself never cut, and he pretends to be furious about every one of them.

### The far cove stair

Last autumn Gorm promised Iska, the First Warden, a new stair down to the far cove before the summer race, so that the town could stand on the rocks there and watch the boats go out. It was a proud promise, and it is going badly. The rock on the lower part of the cliff is harder and more broken than it looked; a whole flight cracked in the spring frost and had to be cut again; and his crews will no longer work the lowest landings after dark, because of what has started climbing the cliffs at night. The race is weeks away. Gorm is behind, and he would rather cut every step himself than admit it.

### What he fears

Gorm fears that the stairs are being climbed by things they were never built for. He has found fresh scratches on the stone of the high landings, deep and in sets of four, and he has quietly begun cutting the edges of new steps sharper and steeper on the seaward side. He has told Ruan of the Cliff Watch, and no one else.

### Hooks

A quest might guard Gorm's crew on the far cove stair through a night's work, carry a cracked stone up to Ulric for judgement, or finally make Gorm read Ulric's carvings from the top step to the bottom.
