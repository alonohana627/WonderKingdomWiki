Dunmor is forty-four, the lamplighter of the quays, and the husband of Iska, the First Warden. Every evening at dusk he walks the Inlet Quay with his long pole and his tinder box and lights the forest-crafted lanterns one by one, and every morning at first light he walks it again to put them out. He is gentle, unhurried and content, which in Goa Harbour, a town of quick tides and quicker tempers, makes him something of a curiosity. He knows every lantern on the quay by its flicker, and he will tell you which one is sulking.

### The lamplighter's son

Dunmor's father, Tormod, was lamplighter before him, and Dunmor walked the quay at his side from the time he could carry the tinder box. His mother died when he was a boy, and the lanterns became the thing he and his father shared instead of talk. Tormod died twenty-two years ago, and Dunmor, at twenty-two, simply picked up the pole the next evening and went on. Nobody appointed him. Nobody needed to.

The lanterns themselves are made by Gunna, the master lantern-maker of the Lantern Market, by a craft handed down from the Magicians of the first camp. Dunmor does not know how they are made, and he has never asked; he thinks some things are better kept by the people who keep them. He does know what each one needs. He brings Gunna the ones that are failing before anyone else can see that they are, and she has said, in her teasing way, that he understands her lanterns better than her apprentice does. Dunmor has had the kindness never to repeat that to Tarran.

### Iska

Dunmor met Iska on the quay steps, as most people in Goa Harbour meet the First Warden. She was a young warden coming in late from a night pilotage, soaked and furious at a captain who had nearly put his ship on the rocks, and he held up a lantern for her on the slippery bottom step. He held it up for her most nights after that, for a year, before she noticed that he was waiting for her and not the ships. They married twelve years ago.

Iska is quick, sure and hard to rattle; Dunmor is slow and patient and impossible to hurry. He is the one person in the town who can make the First Warden sit down and eat, and he does it every evening, after the lanterns are lit, with a bowl of something hot and no argument at all. He worries about her more than he lets her see. When she takes a ship through the gap on a bad tide, he lights the lanterns on the end of the quay first, and stays by them.

### Arlo and Ysmay

Their son Arlo is nine, and he sneaks down to the quay at dawn to watch old Varro read the water. Dunmor, who is on the quay at dawn every day of his life putting out the lanterns, saw him the very first morning. He has never said a word to the boy, only made sure the lantern on the lowest step is the last one he puts out. Ysmay, his mother-in-law, thinks Dunmor is far too soft. She also says he is the best thing that ever happened to her daughter, though never where Iska can hear.

### What he has noticed

Some nights this spring, the lanterns on the lowest landing of the quay have guttered all at once, though there was no wind, and steadied again a few moments later. Dunmor has not told anyone. He is not sure there is anything to tell. But he has begun to light those lanterns last, and to stand by them a while.

### Hooks

A quest might help Dunmor fetch a failing lantern back to Gunna through a dark night, or watch the lowest landing with him to learn what makes the lanterns gutter.
