Gunna is seventy-seven and the Master Lantern-Maker of Goa Harbour. Her workshop stands at the back of the Lantern Market, low and dim and smelling of beeswax, cut green wood and something sharper that nobody has ever managed to name, and from it come the forest-crafted lanterns that light the town's quays at night. They burn steady in any wind and never seem to need much oil, and they give a light that is warmer and softer than any flame has a right to be. She is calm, mysterious and gently teasing. Ask her a question and she will usually answer with another one, and by the time you have worked out the answer to hers, you will often find you have answered your own.

### An old craft

The craft Gunna practises was handed down from the Magicians of the first camp, who brought it to the shore along with the sea-herbs and the habit of reading the tides. It has passed from one lantern-maker to the next ever since, always to a single pupil at a time, and never written down. Gunna learned it from her great-aunt Seona, who kept the same workshop before her and took the girl in at ten, when her hands were small enough for the finest work. Seona died forty-four years ago, and Gunna has been the master since she was thirty-three.

She makes three kinds of lantern: the great quay lanterns that hang on the landings of the Inlet Quay, the hand lanterns that every fisher and warden carries, and the storm lanterns that go out on ships and come back, sometimes, with the ships. A hand lantern takes her a few days. A quay lantern takes her the best part of a month, and she will not be hurried, not by sea wardens, not by captains, and not by Ruan of the Cliff Watch, who wants one on every high landing and asks her each week how many are ready. She always asks him in return how many nights he thinks a hurried lantern would last. He has never answered.

### The lamplighter and the apprentice

Dunmor, the lamplighter of the quays, brings her his failing lanterns, usually before anyone else could tell they were failing. He does not know how they are made and has never asked, and Gunna likes him all the better for it. She once told him, teasing, that he understood her lanterns better than her apprentice did. She is fairly sure he has had the kindness never to repeat it.

Her apprentice is Tarran: eager, clumsy and full of ideas. Half of his lanterns are brilliant and half of them do not light, and Gunna has never had an apprentice whose good half was so good. He is sure she is keeping the best part of the craft from him. He is right. Gunna has not yet decided whether Tarran is the one to receive her oldest secrets, and it is not a question of skill. He wants to make a lantern bright enough to drive the monsters off the cliff landings. Gunna wants to know, before she teaches him anything more, whether he understands why the Magicians of the first camp never made one.

### What she wants and fears

Gunna is looking for an apprentice worthy of her oldest secrets, and she is aware, as she has been every winter for some years now, that she is not getting any younger. She fears that the craft will end with her, as other crafts of the first camp have ended. She fears, almost as much, that it will be passed on to the wrong hands.

### Hooks

A quest might fetch Gunna the rare forest materials her lanterns need from places the timber crews no longer go, carry a new lantern up to the high landings for Ruan, or face one of her questions and try to answer it well enough to earn a secret of the craft.
