Haldor is forty-eight and the head of the timber crews of Goa Harbour, the cutters who fell trees where the forest runs down to the shore and float the logs round to the Inlet Quay. He is big, careful and soft-spoken, the kind of man who lowers his voice when everyone else raises theirs. Before every tree comes down, he lays a hand on the bark and thanks it. Some of the young cutters grin at this. None of them has ever seen him skip it.

### The day the tree fell

Haldor was born on the Forest Shore and went to work with the crews at fifteen, carrying water and sharpening axes. At twenty-three he was cutting on the crew of Una, the best feller on the shore, when a tree she had judged a hundred times came down the wrong way and killed her. Haldor was close enough to hear the trunk crack and too far away to do anything. Una left a daughter of eleven, Torva, who grew up to lead the hunters of the wild coast. Haldor has never spoken to Torva about that day, and she has never asked him. They work side by side now, his crews cutting and her hunters watching the trees around them, and the silence between them is not unfriendly.

He took two lessons from it. The first was that a tree is never just timber, and should be thanked. The second was that no load of wood is worth a cutter. He has been head of the crews for twelve years, and in all that time he has never lost one. He means to keep it that way.

### The crews

Haldor's crews work in pairs and threes along the Forest Shore, felling, trimming and rolling the logs down to the water, where they are lashed into rafts and towed round the point to the quay at slack tide. The youngest of his cutters is Merrin, who keeps a running tally of who has felled the most and wants a crew of her own. Haldor thinks she will get one and that she is not ready yet, and he has told her both, which she only heard half of. Osric, the woodsman, is the son of Elspeth the healer and the best man in the forest Haldor ever had. Last autumn Osric came out of the deep woods white and shaking and has not gone back since, and he will not work past sunset for any wage. Haldor has never pushed him to say what he saw. He suspects he would rather not know.

### Pulling back

Twice this year Haldor has pulled his crews out of the deeper woods. The first time was at the end of winter, when a cutting party came back to find their camp torn apart and every axe-handle bitten through. The second was this spring, when something large followed a crew through the trees for an afternoon without ever showing itself, and Haldor called them all out before dark and did not send them back. Now the crews cut only along the edge of the forest, within shouting distance of the shore, with Torva's hunters close by.

That has made Tamsin, the boatwright, very unhappy. She wants deep-forest timber for the Gap Runner, the tall, close-grained, slow-grown wood that only grows far in, and she is sure it is the difference between losing to Bain Harbour and winning. They have had the same argument so often that the quay hands can recite both sides. Haldor respects her more than she knows. He still will not send a crew in for a keel.

### What he fears

Haldor fears the forest itself is changing. The edge-wood his crews cut now is poorer every month, and he can feel the cutters getting restless. One day soon one of them will go into the deep woods without asking him, and he will not be there.

### Hooks

A quest might guard a single cutting party into the deep forest for the tree Tamsin wants, find out what bit through the axe-handles, or go after a cutter who has gone in alone.
