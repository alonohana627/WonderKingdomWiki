Tarran is twenty and the apprentice of Gunna, the Master Lantern-Maker of Goa Harbour, in her low, dim workshop at the back of the Lantern Market. He is eager, clumsy and full of ideas. Half of his lanterns are brilliant and half of them do not light, and he cannot always tell beforehand which half a new one will fall into. His fingers are scarred from hot wax and cut glass, his eyebrows have been singed off twice, and he is the happiest person in the workshop by a long way.

### The apprentice

Tarran grew up on the Lantern Market, under the stalls and between the carts, and spent most of his childhood standing at Gunna's door, staring at whatever was on her bench until she sent him away. At fourteen he stood there for a whole week without being sent away, and on the eighth day she handed him a broom. He has been her apprentice for six years. The craft passes from one lantern-maker to a single pupil at a time, and it is never written down, so Tarran carries all of it in his head, along with a great many ideas of his own that Gunna has not asked for.

Gunna is calm, mysterious and gently teasing, and she answers almost every question with another question. Tarran has learned an enormous amount from her, and he is sure she is keeping the best part of the craft from him. He is right. He can make a hand lantern now that burns steady in a gale, and a quay lantern that Dunmor the lamplighter will hang without a second look. But there are steps Gunna still does alone, with the door shut, and there is a sharp smell in the workshop that he has never been able to name or find the source of.

### The bright lantern

Tarran wants to make a lantern bright enough to drive the monsters off the cliff landings. He has heard the iron bar ring at the head of the Long Stair in the night, and seen the hunters coming back to Elspeth's door, and he cannot understand why a craft that can light a quay in any weather has never been turned against the dark things that come up the cliffs. Once, when he asked Gunna, she asked him why he thought nobody had made one before. He said it was because nobody had tried hard enough. She smiled and said nothing more, and he has had the uncomfortable feeling ever since that he gave the wrong answer.

He has tried anyway. His brightest lantern so far lit the whole of the Lantern Market for half a night and then cracked its own glass. Another burned blue, for no reason he could find, and has never been repeated. He has promised Ruan, the Captain of the Cliff Watch, something brighter and sooner than Gunna will give him, and Ruan, who is desperate, has not said no. Gunna knows about the promise. She has not mentioned it, which worries Tarran more than a scolding would.

### Kestra

Kestra, who strings the rope bridges on the cliffs, is Tarran's sweetheart. She is fearless and precise and impatient, and she is the only person who has never laughed at one of his lanterns that did not light. Something has been chewing the ropes on her highest bridge, and she means to catch it, and Tarran has promised to make her a lantern bright enough to see the whole cliff face beneath the bridge. It is the most important lantern he has ever tried to make. He is terrified it will be one of the ones that do not light.

### What he wants and fears

Tarran wants Gunna to trust him with her oldest secrets, and he wants to keep his promises to Ruan and Kestra. He fears that he will have to choose between them. He fears, too, that Gunna has a reason for keeping the bright lantern from him, and that he will find it out the hard way.

### Hooks

A quest might fetch Tarran the materials for his bright lantern without Gunna knowing, carry a finished one up to the high landings to see what happens when it is lit, or try to answer Gunna's question for him before he answers it wrong again.
