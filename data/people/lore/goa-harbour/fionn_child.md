Fionn is ten years old and the fastest stair runner on the cliffs of Goa Harbour. For a copper a trip he will carry a message, a parcel or a forgotten lunch from the Inlet Quay to the highest cliff house and back, and he will usually be back before you have finished counting out the copper. He is quick, cheeky and fearless. He knows every shortcut on the cliffs: the gap behind the smokehouse where you can drop two landings at once, the ledge under the third rope bridge, the goat path that cuts the Long Stair in half if you do not mind holding on with your fingers. Gorm, the Master of the Stairs, pretends to be furious about every one of these. Fionn knows perfectly well that he is not.

### The boy by the windlass

Fionn's father, Duncan, was a cove fisher who drowned in a storm when Fionn was barely one. His mother, Ishbel, died of a wasting cough two winters later. He does not remember either of them clearly, only a blue shawl and a song about a gull. Since he was three he has lived with his uncle Hamish in the little house beside the windlass of the rope lift, at the top of the cliffs, and he has grown up to the creak of the rope and the sound of Hamish talking to whoever is in the basket.

Hamish is chatty, jolly and nosy, and knows everyone's business in Goa Harbour. Fionn adores him and has learned, in self-defence, to keep a secret better than anyone else in town. He brings Hamish all the news he picks up on the stairs, which Hamish loves, and keeps back the one thing Hamish would hate.

### Following the hunters

That one thing is the forest. Whenever he can, Fionn slips away after Torva's hunters as they set out from the Forest Shore, and creeps after them to the edge of the trees. He is sure nobody has noticed. In fact Torva noticed the very first time. She lets him come as far as the last stump and no farther, and she makes sure one of her hunters is always between him and the trees, and none of them has ever said a word to him about it, or to Hamish. Fionn thinks he is the best tracker in the town. He is, in fact, very good, and the hunters have begun to say so to each other when he is out of earshot.

He is saving his coppers in a tin under the floorboards for a bow of his own. He thinks that if he can shoot, Torva will have to take him into the trees. Torva has other ideas, but she has not told him those either.

### The high bridge

Fionn knows Kestra's rope bridges better than anyone but Kestra, and he has crossed the highest of them more times than he is allowed. One evening this spring, coming home late across it after sunset, he saw something pale moving on the cliff face below the bridge: low and flat against the rock, quicker than a cat. It was gone before he could look properly. A few days later, Kestra found the ropes on that bridge chewed. Fionn has not told her what he saw, because he would have to explain why he was on the bridge after dark, and Hamish would find out.

### What he wants and fears

Fionn wants to be a hunter like Torva, and to go into the deep forest and come back with a story big enough to tell at the Gap Inn. He fears very little, which is what worries the grown-ups. The one thing that does frighten him is the thought of Hamish alone at the windlass with nobody to bring him the news.

### Hooks

A quest might need a guide to every shortcut on the cliffs, or someone to persuade Fionn to tell Kestra what he saw on the high bridge. It might also find him one day past the last stump, in trouble, with nobody between him and the trees.
