Eilidh is ten, the elder child of Sorcha and Calder, and by general agreement the person in the Fishers' Coves it is hardest to fool. She is bossy, clever and quick, and she keeps her little brother Ronan in line with a firmness that makes grown fishers step out of her way. She can already gut a fish faster than most adults in the coves, which she will demonstrate to anyone who doubts it, and to several people who do not.

### Cove-born

Eilidh was born in the coves and has spent her whole life between the boats, the nets and the rocks. Her mother is the loudest fisher in Goa Harbour and her father the quietest, and Eilidh has inherited her mother's voice and her father's patience, though she only uses the patience on fish. Her grandmother Agna, the net-mender, taught her knots before letters, and is the only person whose opinion of her work Eilidh truly fears. When Agna says a knot of hers is "not bad", Eilidh walks on air for a day.

Until a few years ago she went out on the Sea Thrift whenever she liked, and she was proud of it. Then the sea turned rougher, and her mother stopped taking the children past the point. Eilidh has asked why at least forty times and has never had a proper answer. She suspects it has something to do with the torn nets her grandmother brings back and does not talk about, and she is right, but nobody will tell her so.

### The tide reader

Eilidh means to be a sea warden. Not a fisher, not a pilot's helper: a warden, one of the people who guide every ship through the gap. Every evening, after the catch is in and the fish are gutted, she climbs out onto the cove rocks and watches the tide come and go, and writes what it does on a slate she keeps in a crack in the rock. Her best friend Arlo, the First Warden's son, often comes too, and they argue about the water until it is too dark to see it. Eilidh is quicker; Arlo is more careful. She knows he gets lessons from old Varro on the quay at dawn, and she is jealous of it, though she would rather eat a raw crab than say so.

Ronan comes along when he can be found. Eilidh complains about him constantly and has never once gone home without him.

### What she has noticed

There is an old mark on the cove rocks, a notch her grandmother cut as a girl to show where the highest spring tide reached. Eilidh has watched it all spring, and the water has gone over it twice. She has written this on her slate and underlined it. She is waiting until she is sure before she shows anyone, because she does not want to be laughed at by the wardens, and she does not want to be told she is only a child.

### What she wants and fears

Eilidh wants to stand at the Wardens' table one day and be taken seriously. She wants to go back out past the point. Most of all she wants nothing bad to happen to Ronan, who wanders off after anything that glows and has, she is fairly sure, a secret in his pocket she has not yet managed to get out of him. She fears that the grown-ups are hiding something about the sea, and that they are hiding it because they are frightened.

### Hooks

A quest might help Eilidh prove what her slate shows by carrying it to someone at the Wardens' House who will listen, or help her find Ronan on an evening he has wandered somewhere he should not have.
