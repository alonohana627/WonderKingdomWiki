Ulric is seventy-nine and the stonecarver of the Cliff Stairs, the man who taught Gorm everything he knows about stone and who still cuts the hardest steps in Goa Harbour himself. He is slow, thoughtful and kind. He speaks as he works, a word at a time, with long pauses in which you can hear the sea, and he has never been known to hurry anything, a step or a sentence. Into every step he cuts he carves a little animal, no bigger than a palm: a crab, a hare, a gull, a seal. Most of the town has walked over hundreds of them without a second look.

### Learning the stone

Ulric was born on the clifftop, the son of a goatherd, and spent his boyhood scrambling the cliffs he would one day cut. At fourteen he was taken on by old Struan, then Master of the Stairs, a short-tempered, soft-hearted man who made him sweep stone dust for a year before he let him hold a chisel. Struan taught him that a step must be cut for the hundredth foot, not the first, and that a stair belongs to the cliff and only borrows the town. Struan also carved small things into his steps now and then, a shell or a knot, and it was from him that Ulric took the habit, and made it his own.

Ulric became Master of the Stairs at forty-one, when Struan's hands gave out, and held the post for twenty-seven years. Thirty-three years ago he found a thirteen-year-old boy sitting on the step outside his dead father's house halfway up the Long Stair, refusing to go anywhere, and sat down beside him with a chisel. That boy was Gorm. Eleven years ago, at sixty-eight, Ulric decided his hands were better spent on the hardest steps than on arguing with crews, and handed the Master's post to Gorm. He has never regretted it, though he still corrects Gorm's mallet grip when he thinks no one is looking.

Ulric never married. He lives alone in a narrow cliff house on one of the upper landings of the Long Stair, with a bench outside the door where he sharpens his chisels in the evening light.

### The story in the stair

The Long Stair is the oldest stairway in the town, and over fifty years Ulric has recut its worn steps one at a time, in his own order and at his own pace. Every step he cut there he carved with care, and the animals on the Long Stair are not chosen at random. Read from the top step down to the quay, they tell the story of the first camp. A wild goat stands on the top step, for the Savage hunters who came down from the hills. A few steps lower an owl watches from a branch, for the Magicians who came through the forest. Then the goat and the owl together; a cormorant diving, for the first fishing; a crab among weed, for the sea-herbs; an otter floating on its back watching the water, for the first wardens learning the tides; a moth at a lantern; a gull over two cliffs, for the gap.

He learned the story from the camp songs, from Struan's tales, and from long evenings at the Gap Inn listening to Cormac and to Morven, Calder's grandmother, when she still sang there. The very last step, at the bottom where the stair meets the quay, he has left blank. The songs end in different ways, and Ulric does not know which ending is true. He will not carve a guess.

Nobody has ever read it. Gorm loves every animal on every step and has never once thought to read them in order. Fionn, who runs the stairs by his own shortcuts, skips half of them, and Ulric tells him he is reading the story with the pages torn out. Ulric has never told anyone what the carvings say. He wants someone to notice. He thinks that if a story has to be explained, it was carved badly.

### The hardest steps

These days Ulric is cutting the flight on Gorm's new far cove stair that cracked in the spring frost, through rock so hard and broken that the crews call it the Anvil. He works by daylight only, slowly, and he has noticed without being told that Gorm has started cutting the seaward edges of new steps sharper and steeper. He has not asked why. He is waiting for Gorm to tell him, as Gorm always does in the end.

### What he wants and fears

Ulric wants someone to read the Long Stair from top to bottom and understand it before his hands give out. He wants to know how the story ends, so he can carve the last step. He fears dying with it unread, and he fears, a little, that the ending he cannot find is one the town will need.

### Hooks

A quest might read Ulric's carvings down the Long Stair and bring the story back to him, or hunt for an old verse that tells him what belongs on the last step. Another might guard him through a long day on the Anvil, where the far cove stair runs closest to the water.
