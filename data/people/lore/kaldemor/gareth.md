The forge in Kaldemor Market is the only place in town that is hotter than Rosa's oven, and the man who works it does not say much. Gareth is forty-eight, broad as a barn door, with a leather apron scorched in a hundred places, forearms marked with old burns and a beard he keeps short so it will not catch. He is quiet and proud of a clean edge. He talks more to his anvil than to people, and the anvil, he says, is the better listener. When he does speak, it is usually to tell you that you are holding your sword wrong.

### The forge

Gareth was not born in Kaldemor. He came from a mining village in the hills toward Selsior, where his father dug ore (his parents are both long dead), and he walked to Kaldemor at sixteen because he had heard there was a smith in the Market who needed a striker. The smith was old Bryn, who had a temper like a quenching tub and the best hands Gareth has ever seen. Bryn made Gareth smelt copper for a whole year before he let him near iron. "Copper forgives," Bryn said. "Iron remembers." Gareth hated that year, and he has made every apprentice since do the same.

Bryn died eighteen years ago and left Gareth the forge, the anvil and a hammer whose handle has been replaced four times and whose head has not. Gareth has worked the forge alone since. He sells swords, shields and armour to the young warriors heading out to the fields, iron tyres and axle-pins to Percy the wheelwright, who is his best customer and the only man in the Market who can match him for gloom, and nails, hinges and brackets to the carpenters.

### Copper first

Gareth teaches smithing to anyone who will listen and many who will not. He thinks every smith should start with copper, and he will not hear otherwise. When a young adventurer asks him to show them the forge, he sends them out to the fields to strike copper ore from the veins that glitter in the east and west, and when they bring it back and smelt their first ingot he looks at it a long time and says, "Copper is soft. But every smith starts there." Then, if they have done it well, he shows them how to make a bronze axe. He also teaches the use of upgrade scrolls, which he does not entirely approve of, because a scroll does in a moment what a smith should do in an afternoon. But a sword can be sharpened with one, and he would rather a young warrior went out with a sharper blade than a purer principle.

### More swords

This year he has been asked for more swords than in the five years before it put together. The young people of Kaldemor, who used to come to him for a hoe or a gate hinge, now come for blades; Quill's recruits come every week, and the warriors who go out to the meadow come back with notched edges and dented shields and go out again sooner. Gareth works later every night. He is proud of the work and does not like the reason for it.

Little Tam, Wilbur's boy, turns up at the forge door with his stick whenever his mother sends him to the Market, and asks for a real sword. Gareth tells him to come back when he can lift the hammer. Tam tries to lift the hammer every time. It is a long way off yet, but Gareth has quietly started a small blade, the length of a boy's arm, and keeps it under the bench.

### Neighbours

Gareth's neighbour in the Market is Hilda, who keeps the town's storage and remembers where every single item sits. She has never lost a thing; he has never broken one on purpose. They nod to each other every morning across the lane, and once a year, at midwinter, he mends the hinges on her storage doors and she will not let him pay for the tea.

### What he wants and fears

Gareth wants a good ore, a clean edge and a forge that is too quiet, because a quiet forge would mean nobody needed swords. He fears that the swords he makes will not be enough. He fears too, a little, that he will die like Bryn, with no one to hand the hammer to, though he would never say so to his anvil.

### Hooks

Gareth has heard from Percy that some of the broken wheels coming off the Royal Road bear marks no beast should make, and he would like a traveller to bring him one of whatever made them, so that he can see what a blade must cut. Another quest might begin when he finally gives Tam the small sword, and asks someone to make sure the boy never uses it.
