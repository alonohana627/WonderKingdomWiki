"Fine view from up here," says Garrick. "Fine view." He says it whether you have climbed up to him or are standing at the bottom of his ladder with your neck craned back, and he means it either way. He is thirty-eight, long-limbed and brown as a hazelnut, with chalk on his fingers, a coil of rope over one shoulder and the calm, slightly distant smile of a man who spends most of his day higher than everybody else. He is easygoing, odd and fond of a good saying. Up, he will tell you, is just a direction.

### The ledge

Garrick lives on a ledge of rock above the fountain in Kaldemor Park, reached by a long rope ladder that he pulls up after him when he does not want visitors and lets down when he does. He has a little shelter of canvas and old planks up there, a stove, a great many ropes, and a view over the Park, the windmills, the clock tower and, on a clear day, the hills to the west. Asked why he lives on a ledge, he says that fewer people ask him what he is doing up there, and that the fountain below makes a pleasant sound at dawn. Both are true. He has never given a third reason, and Kaldemor has stopped expecting one.

Nobody is quite sure where he came from. He walked into Kaldemor nine years ago over the hills, not along the road, with a rope and a pack and nothing else, and when Lia asked him where he had come from he pointed upward and smiled. He has climbed, by his own account, every cliff, tower and tall tree between here and the sea, and a few that were not his to climb. He has no family that anyone knows of, and he does not seem to miss one. If Mildred's ledger lists anything under his name besides "the ledge, Park", nobody has seen it.

### Teaching Rope Up

Garrick teaches one thing, and he teaches it only to people who have earned it. Anyone who climbs his ladder has the hands for climbing, he says, but he will teach Rope Up, the leap that throws a climber onto the rope or ledge above, only to those who have seen the world from above. So he sends them west of the town square to Kaldemor Hills, to climb the long vine on the west side as far as the lookout on its high ledge, and look around, and come back. When they do, he looks at them carefully, says they have the look of someone who has seen the clouds up close, and teaches them. "Bend your knees, look up, and let go." Ropes, he says, are only stairs that forgot their steps.

### The clock tower

Garrick has climbed the outside of the clock tower more often than Ewan the clockkeeper would like, which, as Ewan has pointed out, is more often than never. Once, on a fine morning, he climbed up to the great clock face and waved to Ewan through it, and Ewan dropped his oil can. Ewan has written to the mayor about Garrick four times. Garrick knows, because Tobin read him the third letter aloud, laughing too hard to finish it. Garrick is genuinely fond of Ewan, and genuinely cannot understand the fuss. He never touches anything that swings. He only wanted to see the town from the top.

He sees a great deal from his ledge. He saw Meg walk the rim of the fountain on her hands and fall in, and he applauded, and she has not forgiven him. He sees Archie looking up at him from the Park with a pencil in his hand, drawing something taller.

### What he has seen

From the lookout on Kaldemor Hills, on a clear morning, Garrick can see over the woods to the country beyond. This spring he saw something there that he did not like: a line through the trees, far past the hills, where the treetops had been pushed apart and some of them had fallen, leading toward the hollow where the old snail Mano has settled, and coming from somewhere much farther off. He has told Hale. Hale nodded. Garrick has not told anyone else, because nobody else has asked him what he sees from up there, and he has learned that people rarely want to know.

### What he wants and fears

Garrick wants to climb things, and to be left alone while he does it, except when he wants company, which is more often than he admits. He wants one day to climb the great treehouse to its very top, if Alfie will ever let him. He does not fear heights. He fears, a little, the ground, and the things that are coming along it.

### Hooks

Garrick will teach Rope Up to any traveller who reaches the lookout on Kaldemor Hills by the long vine on its west side. He might also ask a traveller who has learned it to go out with him past the hills and follow the line of broken trees back toward wherever it begins.
