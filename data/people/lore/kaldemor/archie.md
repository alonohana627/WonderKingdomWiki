Archie is going to build a treehouse taller than the great one. He has drawn it eleven times, on shavings, on the backs of Alfie's offcuts and once, unwisely, on the workshop wall. It has seven storeys, a rope lift, a lookout higher than Garrick's ledge and, in the latest drawing, a slide. He is seventeen, eager, careless and full of big ideas, and he starts three things before finishing one. He is also very good at saying sorry, which is just as well, because he gets a great deal of practice.

### Coming to the Workshops

Archie was born in a village down the Royal Road toward Selsior, the second of four children of a carter and his wife, who still live there and keep the cart going. He was the child who took things apart. He took apart the cart, the henhouse and, once, the neighbour's gate, and he was much better at the taking apart than the putting back. When he was fourteen his father drove a load of timber up to Kaldemor's Workshops, and Archie went along, saw the great treehouse for the first time, and refused to get back on the cart.

Alfie, who leads the town's carpenters, was persuaded to take him on as apprentice, partly because the boy would not leave and partly because, when Alfie handed him a plane to get rid of him, he planed a board truer than Alfie expected. That was three years ago. Archie lodges in a little room over a woodshed at the Park end of the Workshops lane, next door to Ewan the clockkeeper's cottage at the foot of the clock tower, so he hears the bell every hour of the night, and Ewan hears him coming home late.

### Alfie

Alfie measures three times and cuts once. Archie measures once, cuts, and then measures to see what happened. Alfie hums when the work is going well and goes silent when it is not, and Archie has learned to dread the silence more than any scolding. He thinks Alfie is the finest carpenter on Veligor and would rather be shouted at by him than praised by anyone else. He has not yet worked out that Alfie is gentler with him than he lets on.

Alfie did not tell Archie what he found in the old treehouse beam this spring. He sent the boy off for nails that morning, and by the time Archie came back the beam was cradled in new oak and Alfie was very quiet. Archie has noticed that Alfie and Ewan now talk in low voices at the foot of the tower. He has assumed, with a sinking heart, that they are talking about him.

### The rung

They are not. But they might be. Last spring Alfie sent Archie to the clock tower to replace a worn tread on the stairs. Archie finished the tread, and then, because he had always wanted to see the bell up close, he climbed the inside ladder, and on the way down he missed his footing and came down hard on a rung, and it cracked. He mended it himself, quickly, with glue and twine and a splint of ash, and it held. It is not a mend Alfie would ever have passed. Ewan, who notices everything in his tower, noticed. Archie knows he noticed, because Ewan has looked at him, once or twice, in a way that makes Archie's ears go hot.

Archie has meant to own up every day since. He is very good at saying sorry for small things. This is not a small thing; it is Ewan's ladder, and Ewan's clock, and the clock has since stopped once, though that was the Shroom and not Archie. He goes pale whenever anyone mentions the ladder, and he climbs the tower stairs, when Alfie sends him, as if they might bite.

### What he wants and fears

Archie wants to build something people will still be climbing in a hundred years, and he wants Alfie to look at a joint of his and hum. What he fears is being sent home on the cart. He is afraid, too, that the rung will fail under Ewan one morning, and that it will be his fault, and that is the fear that keeps him up at night more than the bell does.

### Hooks

A traveller who earns Archie's trust might be asked to help him cut and fit a proper new rung in secret before dawn, and then to stand beside him while he finally tells Ewan. Another quest might begin when Archie, sent up the treehouse for something else, finds the new oak cradle around the old beam and cannot resist taking a look.
