Fen notices things. She notices the beetle on the fountain's rim, the new crack in the bakery step, the squirrel that keeps vanishing behind the big birch in the woods, and the way the mushroom houses lean a little towards the morning sun. Then she asks about them, one question after another, until whoever she has caught either answers or runs out of breath. She is eight, wide-eyed, small for her age, and quite certain that every squirrel in Kaldemor has a secret.

### The mushroom houses

Fen loves the mushroom houses more than anything in town. She was born in one, a small, round, freckled one at the end of the lane that runs from the Park to the Workshops, and she has measured it every spring since she was old enough to hold a string. Now she measures dozens of them. She keeps a knotted cord for each house she has adopted, tied with a scrap of coloured thread so she knows which is which, and every spring she goes round with them, wrapping each cord around its cap and tying a new knot where it ends. She knows which house grew the most last year (the tall yellow one by the Market steps), which grew the least (the one with the green door, which she suspects is shy), and which grew crooked.

She does not entirely know why they grow, and neither does anyone else, which is the best part. Lottie told her the old story of the one seed and the generous soil, and Fen has been digging small holes in odd places ever since, to see whether the soil looks generous.

### Bertie and Rosa

Her great hero is Bertie, the old mushroom house shaper, who has been hollowing out the town's houses and fitting their doors and windows for fifty years. Bertie never measures; he says the mushroom tells him. Fen measures everything; she says the mushroom does not tell her anything yet, but it might if she pays attention. Bertie thinks this is the right answer and lets her carry his tools, which she does with enormous ceremony. She follows him all over town on fitting days, and her dearest wish is that he will let her fit a window one day, a real one, in a real house. He keeps saying "next spring". She has written it down so he cannot forget.

Bertie's granddaughter Daisy paints the houses afterwards, and once let Fen paint a shutter, which is now a colour nobody can name.

Fen also carries flour for Rosa the baker. Twice a week she brings bags up from Hob's windmills to the terrace oven, and Rosa pays her in buns, warm, and sometimes in the heel of the loaf, which Rosa saves only for friends. Fen knows this and is very proud of it.

### Friends and school

Her best friends are Tam, who swings a stick at everything, and Nell, who counts the clouds. Fen is allowed to help Nell count, but only the small clouds. She asks Tam questions about being a warrior that he cannot answer, and she knows perfectly well he makes the answers up, and she likes them better that way. At Lottie's school in the great treehouse, she is the one who asks "but why?" until even Lottie has to go and look something up.

### What she wants and fears

Fen wants to fit a window in a mushroom house, to find out where the squirrel behind the birch goes, and to know why the houses grow. She would like to grow up to be a shaper, like Bertie, and she has already decided she will measure.

She was frightened when the clock tower went quiet this spring. She had never heard the park without its ticking, and she says the silence was spooky, like the whole town holding its breath. She was very glad when the gear came home, but she still listens for the bell every hour, just to be sure.

### Hooks

The squirrel behind the big birch is real, and it really does vanish. Fen is sure there is a hole, or a door, or something stranger, and she is only waiting for a grown-up brave enough to come and look. Another quest could follow her cords: one spring, one of her houses grows far more than it should, all in a single night, and Fen is the only one who has the knots to prove it.
