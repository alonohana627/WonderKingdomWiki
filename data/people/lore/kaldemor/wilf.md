Wilf smells of leaf mould, and he would take that as a compliment. He is forty-six, wiry and weathered, with a nose that has been broken once and a basket that has never been empty, and he gathers mushroom spores and caps for half of Kaldemor. He grumbles about everything: the weather, the price of baskets, the Market, the mayor's jokes, the noise of the windmills and the state of his own knees. Everything, that is, except mushrooms. About mushrooms he will talk with a quiet, glowing reverence for as long as you care to listen, and he knows every one of them in the country round Kaldemor by smell alone.

### The cottage in the damp

Wilf lives at the far end of the farms, in a squat stone cottage that stands in the wettest hollow of the lane, where nobody else would build. That is the point. Behind it, under a sagging roof of old sacking and willow, he grows a few beds of caps in rotted straw and oak dust: brown caps, orange caps and a tray of pale ones he will not name and will not sell. He was born in that cottage. His father gathered before him and taught him the woods; his mother dried and sorted the spores on the kitchen table, and he still uses her sieves. Both are dead now. He has never married. He says he would have to marry somebody who did not mind the smell, and that he has never found anybody that sensible.

His only family is his sister, three years older, who married a farmer and lives in a village a long way south of the hills. They see each other once in a few years and write at midwinter. This spring she was ill, and Wilf went to her, the first time in nine years he had left Kaldemor for more than a night. He stayed a month, until she was on her feet and scolding him, and came home at the start of summer.

### Rosa and Bram

Wilf's spores go into two things above all: Rosa's mushroom bread at the top of the Market steps, and Bram's potions under the striped awning at the far end of the Market. He supplies them both and has done for twenty years. Rosa takes the finest and pays on the nail. Bram takes the most and argues about every bag.

Nine years ago those two stopped speaking, over a spore tincture and a batch of greenish bread, and Wilf has been the only bridge between them ever since. Rosa asks him, in a careless voice, whether Bram is well. Bram asks him, in a careless voice, whether Rosa is still using too much salt. Wilf answers both of them truthfully and briefly, and grumbles about it all the way home, and would not stop carrying the news for anything. He thinks they are a pair of fools. He also thinks they are his two oldest customers, and he would like them both at his funeral, preferably standing next to each other.

### Too bold

While Wilf was away, Rosa had to send a young adventurer out for spores, and Bram ran low and made do. When Wilf came home, he went straight to the woods past the hills with his basket, and came back early, empty-handed and pale.

The Shrooms had always been there. Wilf has spent his whole life slipping past them; they are shy of a man who stands still and smells of the woods, and they used to scatter at a cough. Not any more. They came out of the leaf litter at him in threes and fours, following him between the trees, and they did not scatter when he shouted. One knocked the basket off his arm. He says they were too bold for him to gather alone, and that is as much as he will say. He has heard about the great gear the Shroom took from the clock tower. He does not think it is funny.

### What he wants and fears

Wilf wants the woods back the way they were: quiet, damp and his. He wants someone to walk with him while he gathers, though he will complain the whole time that they are treading on things. He fears that Rosa and Bram will both run short at the same time, and that the woods he has known since he was a boy have turned into something else.

### Hooks

Wilf needs an escort into the woods past the hills to gather spores for Rosa and Bram, and he will teach a patient traveller to tell one mushroom from another by nose. He has also noticed that the Shrooms in the deepest part of the woods are carrying things, shiny things, away toward the same place, and he would very much like to know where.
