Tam is going to be a warrior when he grows up. He will tell you so before he tells you his name, and he will tell you again when you leave, in case you forgot. He is nine years old, bold, loud and only a little afraid of snails, and he carries a stick everywhere he goes, which he swings at anything that looks like an enemy: hedges, washing lines, fence posts, and once, memorably, Mayor Tobin's hat.

He is the son of Wilbur the farmer and Hattie the beekeeper, and he grew up on the biggest farm east of the square, among the pumpkins, turnips and beans, with the hives humming at the edge of the fields. From his father he has a loud voice and a grudge against snails. From his mother he has, so far, only her red cheeks, though she lives in hope of the patience.

### The snail feud

Tam's war against the snails began last autumn. The week before the harvest fair, the snails got into the patch where his father had been growing his prize pumpkin all summer and ate a hole in it big enough for Tam to put his head through, which he did. His father has sworn it will not happen again. Tam has sworn it louder. Every morning he patrols the pumpkin rows with his stick, which he has named Shellbreaker, and every evening he reports to his father how many snails he has defeated. The number is always large and usually includes a few he only frightened.

He says he is not afraid of snails. Much. The blue ones are fine. The red ones are fine from a distance.

### The Park and the Gauntlet

Most afternoons Tam can be found at the Park, hanging on the fence by Quill's Gauntlet and watching the young warriors run the monster yard, the hanging course and the last stand. Quill is the greatest hero Tam knows, and Tam has asked him to be allowed to run the Gauntlet more times than anyone has counted. The answer is always "when you are bigger", which Tam takes to mean "soon". Quill pretends not to notice him watching every run, but Tam has noticed that Quill shouts his footwork lessons a little louder when he is at the fence, and he practises them afterwards in the square with Shellbreaker.

He also watches Digby, who has failed the hanging course eleven times and gets up grinning every time. Digby used to work on Tam's father's farm, and Tam thinks this means they are practically brothers in arms.

### Friends and family

His two best friends are Nell, who counts clouds, and Fen, who counts everything else. Nell will not let him swing Shellbreaker near her tally book. Fen asks him questions about warrior things that he cannot answer, which is annoying, so he makes the answers up.

Mayor Tobin once told Tam that he could even be mayor one day, if he washes behind his ears. Tam is considering it. Being mayor seems to involve a lot of talking, which he is good at, and some washing, which he is not. He hates washing behind his ears more than he hates red snails.

His mother wants him to be careful, and his father wants him to be brave, and Tam wants to be both, which he thinks is exactly what a warrior is. He does not yet know that his mother lies awake some nights over it. Wren has knitted him a cap, too, and he wears it, but only when nobody from the Gauntlet is looking.

### What he wants and fears

Tam wants a real sword from Gareth's forge, a turn on the Gauntlet, and to be the one who finally beats the snails that ate the pumpkin. He has heard grown-ups in the square talking in low voices about something big in a hollow past the woods, and about road warnings Rufus reads out more gently than he used to, and he wants very badly to be old enough to help.

What he fears, though he would never say it, is being left behind: that everyone will go and fight the big thing in the woods while he is still being told he is too small.

### Hooks

One morning Tam will not come home from his pumpkin patrol, and his trail of flattened beans will lead toward the meadow and the red snails, Shellbreaker in hand. Or a traveller who has run the Gauntlet might be asked, very seriously, to teach him one real move, and to swear not to tell his mother.
