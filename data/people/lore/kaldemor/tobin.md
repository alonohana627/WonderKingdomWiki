Tobin has been mayor of Kaldemor for thirty years, and he will tell you, if you stand still long enough, that the great treehouse has outlived three mayors, himself included. Nobody has ever been able to explain to him why that joke does not work, and he has stopped listening to anyone who tries. He is seventy-one, warm, chatty and endlessly proud of his town, and there is not a family in Kaldemor whose name he does not know, along with most of their grandparents.

### From barrels to the square

Before he was mayor, Tobin was a cooper. He made barrels for cider, flour and pickles in a workshop behind the market, and he still taps every barrel he passes to hear whether it is sound. The town chose him at forty-one, after old Mayor Hettie died, mostly because he already knew everyone and nobody could imagine a town meeting without him talking at it. He has stood in front of the treehouse ever since, greeting newcomers, settling squabbles about fences and snails, and telling the story of the seed to anyone who will listen. Every great town starts with one seed, he says, and he believes it.

He was married for many years to Primrose, a potter, who died twelve years ago. They had no children, and Tobin says that is why he has so many: every child in Kaldemor is his to worry about. Tam, who means to be a warrior, has been told he could be mayor one day if he washes. Lia, who meets every newcomer in the square, learned half her job from Tobin and now does it better than he ever did, which he tells her often. Mildred keeps his ledgers and letters, and Tobin knows perfectly well that she has counted the town more carefully than he has; he just prefers to count it by knocking on doors. Ewan, the clockkeeper, has been his friend since they were boys, and when the great gear went missing from the clock tower, Tobin sat up with him every night until it came home.

### The harbour bargain

Much of Kaldemor's good fortune travels by cart to Bain Harbour, and that trade rests on an old partnership. Nineteen years ago, a young captain named Quenna, then twenty-seven and newly master of her own ship, walked up to Kaldemor because she was tired of paying the middlemen in Selsior. She arrived in the square with salt on her boots and argued with Tobin about the price of cider for an entire afternoon. He liked her immediately. Every autumn since, Kaldemor's harvest carts have gone down to the harbour loaded with cider, flour, pottery and woodwork, and come back with salt, dried fish, rope and spices.

Quenna is Harbour Master now, but she still comes to Kaldemor for the harvest fair each autumn to settle the year's prices with him over Rosa's bread. Tobin says she is the only person alive who can out-talk him, and that she is a far shrewder bargainer than she lets on. He has been down to Bain Harbour only twice in all those years, and still talks about the smell of tar.

### What he worries about

Just under three hundred people live in Kaldemor, not counting the snails, and Tobin means to see every one of them safe. His current project is a new fence around the gardens, with ground snail shell mixed into the mortar; he swears it will make the wall tougher, and he is paying young adventurers to bring him shells. Hale's reports from the park and the woods worry him more than he shows, and so does the road to the harbour. Last autumn one of the harvest carts never arrived, and now every cart travels with hired guards.

He does not like to think about the day he stops being mayor. He suspects Mildred thinks about it for him.

A quest might follow the missing harvest cart down the road toward the coast, or help Tobin finish the fence before the snails find another way in.
