Every evening, just as the lanterns come on, Drum sits down on an upturned crate at the edge of Lantern Square, inside a ring of upturned buckets, pans, a washtub and one cracked kettle, and begins to play. The night market sets its pace by the beat. Stall-keepers hang their lamps in time to it, Ladle turns her skewers to it, and by the second hour half the square is walking in step without noticing. Drum says almost nothing and lets the rhythm talk. Drum smiles only when someone in the crowd starts dancing, and then the smile is so sudden and wide that people dance on purpose, just to see it.

### A cooper's yard

Drum is thirty-one and grew up in Goa Harbour, in a cooper's yard above the Inlet Quay, among stacks of buckets, barrels and tubs. Drum's parents still make them there, and send a new bucket inland now and then, by carter, with no note; Drum knows what it means. As a small child Drum was the quiet one in a loud family, and found that a stave struck just so said more than any words, and that the sound of the whole yard at work, hammers and mallets and hoops, was a kind of song if you listened for it.

At seventeen Drum walked inland with a sack of mallets and three nested buckets, and arrived in Urbanograd on a night market evening. Nobody was playing. Drum sat down on the edge of the square and started, and has been there ever since, fourteen years now. Ivo Sable, the street healer, still argues with patients about whether Drum's buckets count as an instrument. Drum has never joined the argument, and has never needed to.

### Partners

Jinx, the juggler of Lantern Square, is Drum's street partner. Jinx talks enough for both of them, which suits Drum perfectly. Together they have worked out an act in which every throw lands on a beat and every drop lands on a rimshot, and nobody watching can tell which drops are deliberate. Jinx counts it a good night when Drum smiles twice. Drum has never said so, but counts it a good night when Jinx is there at all.

Saffi, the fiddler who plays everything twice as fast, is Drum's bandmate. She has been writing a tune for every street in the city, and Drum has found a rhythm for each of the nine she has finished. They rarely discuss music; Saffi starts, Drum follows, and a few bars later Saffi is following Drum. She laughs at her own mistakes before anyone else can, and Drum, unusually, laughs with her.

### The rhythm for the roads

Lately Drum has been working on something new, late at night after the market closes, in an empty corner of the Old Barracks yard. It is a rhythm loud enough, steady enough and strange enough to keep monsters away from the roads. Drum says this is half a joke. It is also half not. The travelers Drum sees stumbling past to Ivo's awning, bitten and shaking, are more every season, and Drum has noticed that on nights the beat is loudest the lanterns facing the wilds tend to survive till morning. It proves nothing. Drum keeps playing anyway, and keeps a tally of which patterns seem to make the stray dogs at the gate fall silent.

### What Drum wants and fears

Drum wants to find out whether rhythm can do more than make people dance, and to play once for the whole square on Walkout Day. Drum fears silence: the night the square is too empty, or too frightened, to need a beat.

A quest might test Drum's rhythm on the road outside the gate, gather new pans and buckets that ring true, or carry word from Drum's family on the quay at Goa Harbour.
