Cobble says a boot should outlive its owner, and means it. There are boots walking about Urbanograd today that Cobble made before their wearers were born, resoled, restitched and handed down, and Cobble can tell you whose feet each pair was cut for. He is seventy-six, grumpy, honest and proud, with a voice like a creaking stair and hands so hard and sure that he can feel a flaw in a hide with his eyes shut. His shop on Workshops Row is small, dark and smells of leather and beeswax, and it has no sign. Anyone who needs to ask where it is, Cobble says, does not yet need his boots.

### The soft-soled boot

Cobble was born in Urbanograd, in a house on the Row, and at fourteen went to work for an old bootmaker named Henrike Tull, a Walker's daughter who had never belonged to any guild and was proud of it. She taught him that a boot is made for one pair of feet and one kind of ground, and nothing else matters. When she died, fifty-two years ago, she left him the shop, the lasts and her bad temper, and he has kept all three in good repair.

Urbanograd has a kind of ground no other town has: slate roofs, wet tiles, plank bridges and chimney ledges. Cobble spent his first years on his own perfecting a boot for it, with a soft, grippy sole sewn in a pattern of his own invention and an upper that bends like a glove and wears like iron. The rooftop runners took to it at once. It is the boot they swear by still.

### Three generations of Kites

Cobble has made boots for three generations of Kites. He made the first pair for Kite's grandmother, who ran messages across the city when he was a young man, and later the first pair for Kite's mother, and a few years ago the first pair for Kite, who holds the record for the run from the Gate Walk to the Great Hall and tells Cobble about it every visit. Cobble pretends not to listen and keeps a note of every record on the back of his shop door. Kite is his most loyal customer, and the only one allowed to criticize the stitching, because Kite has earned it.

Rooftop folk come to him for slippers and repairs too, and so do the actors of the Old Barracks, who need shoes that will not slip on a cart. He is fair in his prices, scathing in his opinions, and never once late with a pair.

### The wrong sort of hands

Cobble wants an apprentice. He is old enough to know that the soft-soled boot will die with him otherwise, and the thought frightens him more than anything on the roads. But he has rejected every one who has asked, for having the wrong sort of hands: too soft, too quick, too impatient, too eager to be finished. He cannot explain what the right sort looks like, only that he will know them.

This year the most persistent candidate is Edgar Penhallow, a guild apprentice visiting from Selsior, who has asked three times and been refused once out loud and twice with a grunt. Edgar's hands are guild hands, neat and careful and used to cutting by pattern, and Cobble said so. Edgar now sweeps Cobble's doorstep every morning. Cobble has not told him to stop, and lately has started leaving a scrap of hide and an awl by the door, as if by accident, to see what the boy does with them.

### What he wants and fears

Cobble wants to hand on the boot before his hands give out. He fears dying with it, and fears almost as much choosing wrong. Good leather comes in from outside the walls less often each season, and the hides that do arrive are poorer; he hoards the best for the runners.

A quest might bring a load of good hides safely through the roads, find Cobble the right sort of hands, or recover a pair of old Kite boots that has gone missing from the shop door.
