Leland is seventy-six, an old well-digger, and he knows more about what lies under Urbanograd's streets than anyone alive. He is quiet, careful and endlessly curious about what lies under things: floors, cobbles, hillsides, other people's questions. He talks to stone as if it might answer, in a low, reasonable voice, the way you might talk to a horse you were not quite sure of. He says it usually does answer, if you are patient, and that the answer is generally "not there".

### Born to the shovel

Leland was born in Urbanograd, in a lean-to against the old fortress wall, the son of Caleb, a well-digger who had come to the city in its early years, when the Walkers were still learning that art does not draw water. Old Grad had been built for a garrison, and its wells had silted up in the generations it stood empty. Caleb spent his life opening the old ones and digging new ones, and Leland went down the shafts with him on a rope from the age of nine. Caleb died when Leland was thirty, and Leland has dug alone ever since.

For more than sixty years he has dug, cleared, lined and capped the wells of the city, and in all that time he has kept a map. It is drawn in chalk on the long wall of his cellar, under his house near the Great Hall: every well he has dug, every shaft he has cleared, every old drain and cellar and buried wall he has met on the way down, each in the right place and at the right depth. When the wall filled up he started on the floor. He never married. He says he spent his courting years at the bottom of holes, and that the holes were better company than he deserved.

### The well that went through

Forty years ago, digging a new well in the yard behind the Great Hall kitchens, Leland's shaft broke through into nothing. He lowered a lantern on a long rope and saw, far below, dark water, the tops of stone pillars and the curve of a vaulted roof, much older than anything he had met before. He sat at the top of that shaft for a whole afternoon, looking down. Then he went to the Open Table of the day, which told him, after a long argument, to cap it. He capped it with a stone slab and a ring bolt, and has been thinking about it ever since. It is the reason his map has a great blank space under the Great Hall, edged in careful dotted chalk.

The other reason is the iron door. Under the Great Hall, behind the kitchens, is an iron door that the Walkers found locked a century ago and never had a key for. Leland has knelt at its keyhole more times than he can count. For years he has carried his questions about it to Hex, the locksmith on Workshops Row, who has made the latches on his well covers for twenty years, and who has looked through the keyhole with Leland's lantern and pronounced the lock "Aristocrat, and very proud of it". They are old friends. Leland is the only person in the city who does not mind Hex's riddles, because he takes as long to answer them as Hex takes to tell them.

### Keeper of the Low Door

When the Open Table at last agrees to open that door, there is really only one person it can name to keep it. It was Hex, kneeling at the lock with his picks, who said aloud that the Table should name Leland its keeper, and nobody argued. Leland is Keeper of the Low Door. He keeps the door, the ropes and the map, and he adds to the map every evening, in chalk, from what the people who go down tell him: the old keep's halls, the flooded cisterns, the long tunnels running out under the hills, and the quarry at the bottom where the stone is warm. He lets anyone with the nerve go down, as often as they like, and he asks every one of them the same question when they come back up: what did the stone feel like?

### What he wants and fears

Leland wants to finish the map. He knows he will not, and he does not mind, so long as someone carries it on after him. What he fears is the warm stone at the bottom. Sixty years of digging have taught him that stone underground is cool to the hand, always, everywhere, and the stone down there has not heard the rule.

### Hooks

A quest could follow Leland's chalk map down into the levels below the door, or help him rig a rope down his old capped well shaft. Another might bring him back a piece of the deepest stone, so that he can put his hand on it and listen.
