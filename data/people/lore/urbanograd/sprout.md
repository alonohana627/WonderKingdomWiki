Sprout is never still. He is eight years old, small, freckled and loud, with hair that sticks up whatever anyone does to it and a giggle that can be heard from the far side of Lantern Square. He lives at the Old Barracks Theatre, among costume baskets, painted flats and folded scenery, and he has run, climbed, hidden and fallen asleep in every corner of it. Every Walkout Day he plays the market urchin who cheers for Juna in the first scene of Juna Marrow's play, and every year, with one line and a cartwheel nobody asked for, he steals the show.

### Growing up backstage

Sprout is the son of Dex, the big booming actor who has played the grandee for fifteen years. His mother, Lark, sang in the troupe's chorus and died of a fever when Sprout was barely a year old. He does not remember her, but he knows her songs, because his father sings them, badly, and because on the anniversary of her death the two of them climb up to the Rooftops to watch the lanterns come on across the city and sing one of them together. Sprout thinks of it as a good night, not a sad one. His father has made sure of that.

Dex raised him backstage, with half the troupe for aunts and uncles. Dina Finch, the director, calls the company her family, and to Sprout that is simply true. Hambone, who keeps the old cart, lets him ride on the tailboard, or rather, does not notice him riding on it, which Sprout counts as the same thing. Thimble, the costume maker, has pins in her mouth at all times and has stitched every one of Sprout's urchin costumes, letting each one out a little more every spring and complaining about how fast he grows. Sprout knows about the crack in the cart's left rear wheel, because he knows everything that happens backstage, and he has kept the secret, which for Sprout is a heroic effort.

### Every part, by accident

Sprout has learned every part in the play without ever meaning to. He has heard the rehearsals so many times that the lines simply stayed in his head: Juna's speeches, the grandee's bluster, the market crowd's jeers, the songs. He sings Piper's new songs and Hambone's old versions of them both, in whichever order annoys both sides most, and he does the grandee's fall off the cart on any raised surface available, including, once, the Great Hall's table during a quiet moment in a debate.

He has decided that Rue, who plays Juna this year, is his favourite Juna ever, and tells everyone so, including the older actresses who have played her before. When Rue froze in the wings at her audition this spring, Sprout told her afterwards that he freezes too sometimes, but only when he is about to do something brilliant. Rue says it was the most useful note she got all year.

### The hat

What Sprout wants most in the world is to play the grandee one day, and the reason is the hat. The grandee's hat is a tall tower of purple felt and feathers that Thimble keeps locked away between shows, and that even Dex is not allowed to take home. Sprout has seen it, been near it and once, for nearly a whole breath, touched it. He has a plan, roughly, involving growing up. Dex pretends to be outraged that his son wants his part, and is secretly the proudest man in Urbanograd.

Sprout fears very little except being told to sit still. He sometimes worries, though, that his father is sad when nobody is looking, and he has started making him laugh on purpose, just in case.

A quest might find Sprout when he has hidden somewhere in the Old Barracks right before his cue, help him get a look at the locked hat, or carry a song of Lark's that someone else in the city remembers back to Dex and Sprout.
