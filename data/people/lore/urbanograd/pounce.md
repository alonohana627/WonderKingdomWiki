Pounce is a big grey cat, broad in the shoulder and broader in the middle, with a torn left ear, a tail like a feather duster and the unhurried walk of somebody who owns everything he can see. From the Rooftops of Urbanograd he can see a great deal, and he is quite certain that all of it is his. By day he is magnificently lazy. By night he is dignified, watchful and very hard to find, and rooftop folk who meet him on a ridge after dark sometimes step aside for him without quite deciding to.

### The uninvited lodger

Nobody knows where Pounce came from. He was simply there one wet autumn, years ago, asleep in the straw of Perch's pigeon loft at the top of the Crooked Tower, the highest roof in the city. Perch tried, briefly, to discourage him. Pounce ignored this, as he ignores most things, and has slept in the loft every day since. The pigeons have accepted him with deep reluctance. They mutter when he arrives and settle, eventually, when he does not move. Perch suspects he has never once tried to catch one, because it would be too much effort, and has come to find his company restful. He is the only lodger Perch has ever had who never asks a question.

Last Walkout Day, Ferro the smith gave Perch's loft a small iron cat, and Pounce has slept beside it every afternoon since, as though he has finally been given a portrait of himself and approves of it.

### His rounds

Pounce has a round, as fixed as a lamplighter's. In the morning he sleeps in the loft. Around midday he crosses to Old Parsnip's rooftop garden next door and sleeps in the warmest trough, which Parsnip has given up on and planted with catmint. In the afternoon he goes back to the loft and the iron cat. At dusk he wakes, stretches, and goes out over the roofs, and where he goes then is his own business. He has been seen on the chimneys above the Night Market, on the ridge of the Great Hall, and once, to general astonishment, riding Rivet's lift.

Rue, who is to play Juna on Walkout Day, rehearses her lines on the Rooftops, usually to Pounce. He is not much help. He yawns at the best speeches and walks away during the climax, and she has decided that this is good training for a real audience. Tick, Knox's son, salutes everyone, including Pounce, and Pounce returns the salute by not leaving, which Tick counts as a great honour.

### The weather cat

Rooftop folk say Pounce always knows when the weather is about to turn. Before a storm he comes in early and curls up in the deepest corner of the loft with his back to the wind; before a long dry spell he sleeps flat out on the slates in the open. Old Parsnip waters by him. Kite plans runs by him. Figgy, down in Lantern Square, has been known to ask Kite whether Pounce has come in yet, because she likes to know when the rain will finish her drawings. Nobody has ever caught Pounce being wrong, though Perch points out that nobody has ever kept a list.

### What he wants

Pounce wants the warm trough, the sunny ledge, the quiet loft and the respect of the pigeons, roughly in that order. He fears nothing he will admit to, though he does not like the evenings when the wind blows from the middle of the island, and on those nights he does not go out at all.

A quest might find Pounce when he goes missing before a storm, coax him down from somewhere he should not be before Rue's rehearsal, or follow him on his night round to find out where he really goes.
