Ferro's forge stands near the far end of Workshops Row, and you can find it by the sculptures. A heron made of old hinges guards the door. A copper fox curls on the roof. A great spiral of fortress chain, gate bolts and cart springs, which Ferro calls nothing at all, rises out of the yard like a frozen whirlwind. Ferro is forty-six, gruff and exacting, broad in the shoulder and scarred on both forearms, and has the patience of iron and roughly the same warmth on first meeting. The warmth comes later, and it is real. Ferro names every blade before it leaves the forge, and is more sentimental about them than anyone would guess.

### Sculptor first

Ferro was born in Urbanograd, the child of a tinker who mended pots door to door and died when Ferro was twenty, leaving a handcart full of scrap metal and no instructions. Ferro looked at the scrap for a long time and then began welding it together into shapes. The odd metals of the city became Ferro's materials: bronze from broken bells, copper from old pipes, salvaged iron from the fortress's own rusted gates, tin, pewter and whatever the carts brought in. For years Ferro made only sculptures: birds, beasts, strange spirals, a fiddler for the corner of Lantern Square that the children still climb on.

Bastian Krell made the bellows that keep Ferro's forge alive, and still mends them, with an opinion thrown in free each time.

### How the knives began

Hatch, the monster hunter, came in one day about sixteen years ago with a bent spear-head and nowhere else to take it. Ferro said it was not that kind of forge. Hatch said a forge was a forge. Ferro mended it, grumbling, and mended it well, and Hatch came back a month later and talked Ferro into forging him a pair of hunting knives. That was long before the other adventurers came asking. Hatch still claims he started Ferro's weapon trade, and says it to anyone in earshot. Ferro still replies that Hatch ruined a perfectly good sculptor. Neither of them has ever wanted the argument to end.

Then the roads grew worse, and the adventurers came. They wanted daggers, throwing stars, claws, knuckle guards, things that are light and quick and do not miss. Ferro makes them now, and makes them very well, and grumbles about every one. But each blade gets a name before it goes, scratched small on the tang where nobody but a very careful owner will ever find it: Small Errand, Last Lantern, Mind the Step. Ferro believes a named blade is more careful in the hand, and will not be argued out of it.

### Shade

Shade, the knife-thrower and road guide, is Ferro's best customer and favorite. Their dealings are short and nearly wordless: Shade holds out a worn knife, Ferro looks at it, and a week later a new one is ready. Ferro has never told Shade the knives' names, and Shade has never told anyone, but Ferro knows Shade has found them, because Shade once returned a chipped knife and asked, quietly, that it be mended and not replaced.

### Walkout Day

Every Walkout Day, Ferro welds one new sculpture and gives it away. Nobody knows in advance what it will be or who will get it: a newcomer, a child, a stall-keeper who had a bad year. Last year it was a small iron cat for Perch's loft, and Pounce has slept beside it every afternoon since. The gift is Ferro's way of remembering that the forge was for making things beautiful first.

### What Ferro wants and fears

Ferro wants a season with no weapon orders at all, and time for one great sculpture for the Gate Walk. Ferro fears hearing that a named blade failed someone on the road.

A quest might fetch an odd metal Ferro needs from outside the walls, help choose who receives this year's Walkout Day sculpture, or return a named knife that has turned up far from its owner.
