Zinnia Calloway has never met a dare she would refuse, and the whole Gate Walk knows it. Nobody calls her Zinnia except, very occasionally, in a tone of warning. She is Zip: eighteen, sunny, sunburnt, with a shock of copper hair she cuts herself with whatever is sharpest, a sword she has not yet earned the right to swing and an arm that still aches when the weather turns. Zip is reckless, cheerful and absolutely determined to be famous, and she has decided that the quickest way to fame is to go and stand next to something terrifying and see what happens.

### A city girl

Zip grew up in Urbanograd, in her grandmother's laundry at the bottom of the Gate Walk, among copper tubs, wet sheets and steam. Her parents died when she was too small to remember them, and her grandmother, Ottoline Calloway, raised her: a big, slow-voiced washerwoman who had walked to every town on Veligor as a girl and told Zip about all of them while wringing out shirts. Ottoline died two winters ago. Zip kept the laundry going for one season, and then, as she puts it, had to admit that she was not a laundry sort of person. She gave the tubs to the woman next door and moved into the room above the shop with a pack, a borrowed sword and Ottoline's old road map.

Growing up at the foot of the Gate Walk, Zip watched every adventurer who came through the gate, and decided early that she would be one of them, and the best one, and that people would sing about her in the Night Market.

### The broken arm

Last spring, Zip went on her first adventure. She talked her way onto a caravan bound for Kaldemor as an extra pair of hands, and two days out, when something large and tusked crashed out of the brush and the carters reached for their spears, Zip ran at it. She did not catch it. She chased it across a stream and up a steep bank and fell off the top of the bank onto a rock, and broke her arm in two places. The caravan's guard, Torvi Brask, carried her back to the wagons over one shoulder, still shouting about the tusks.

That is the true version. Zip has told the story in a good many other versions since, and Tick, the gate messenger, has written three of them down in his notebook, each one featuring a slightly bigger monster. The arm has healed. The story keeps growing.

### Torvi

Torvi Brask, the axe-fighter from the highlands who hires out as a caravan guard, looked at Zip on the bank that day, laughed like a rockslide and said that bravery was wasted on someone who could not fight. Back in the city, Zip followed Torvi around the Gate Walk until Torvi gave in. Now Torvi is teaching her to fight properly: footwork, guard, when to stand and, above all, when to run, which is the lesson Zip finds hardest. They train on the open ground inside the gate most mornings, and the Gate Watch lean on their spears and watch.

### The spark lamp

Margo Venn, the inventor on Workshops Row, has built a lamp that throws sparks, bright and loud enough, she hopes, to frighten off the monsters that make the roads so dangerous. It works beautifully in the workshop, where Bolt, Margo's apprentice, has tested it on a broom, a barrel and himself. Nobody knows whether it works on a real monster. Zip volunteered before Margo had finished asking. Margo is grateful and uneasy in equal measure, and has made Zip promise to bring the lamp back even if she does not bring back the story.

### What Zip wants and fears

Zip wants a song. She wants a tune at the Night Market with her name in the chorus, and to come home through the gate one day to a crowd. What she fears, and has told nobody, is that she will turn out to be ordinary, and that the broken arm was the most interesting thing that will ever happen to her.

A quest might go out with Zip and Margo's spark lamp to find a real monster, keep Zip from accepting a truly stupid dare, or find out what really happened to the tusked beast she chased.
