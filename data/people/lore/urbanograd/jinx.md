Jinx juggles in Lantern Square every evening, and will juggle anything handed over: apples, boots, teacups, a cabbage from Old Parsnip's tubs, and once, memorably, three of Margo Venn's spare parts, one of which turned out to be a spring that went off in mid-air. Jinx is eighteen, restless and cheeky, with quick hands and a grin that never quite switches off. Jinx drops things on purpose to make children laugh, and almost never by accident, though nobody watching can ever tell which is which.

### A runaway from the capital

Jinx was born in Selsior, to a family of respectable clerks who expected their child to become a respectable clerk. Jinx spent most of childhood juggling the household inkpots instead, and at fourteen hitched a ride on a carter's wagon to Urbanograd with a bag of clothes and three wooden balls. That was four years ago. Jinx's parents still live in Selsior, write once a season and hope it is a phase; Jinx writes back cheerfully, and is fairly sure it is not.

The first winter was hard. Jinx slept in a stairwell and juggled for coins in the cold until Tinder, the fire-eater, took pity, shared her supper and showed Jinx which corners of the square stay warm after dark. They have been friends ever since. Tinder is swaggering on stage and nervous off it, and Jinx is the opposite, and both of them find this very funny.

### Life on the square

Jinx's street partner is Drum, who plays a ring of upturned buckets and pans and says almost nothing. Jinx talks enough for both of them, and the two of them have worked out an act in which every throw lands on a beat. Drum smiles only when someone starts dancing, and Jinx counts it a good night when Drum smiles twice.

Jinx's favorite audience is Figgy, the chalk artist's daughter, who sits on the edge of her own drawings and shouts advice. Figgy is Jinx's harshest critic and loudest fan. Jinx sometimes drops a ball onto one of her chalk pictures on purpose so that she can demand the one-copper fee, which Jinx always pays.

Afterwards Jinx passes the hat, counts the takings, and spends most of it at the Night Market within the hour.

### Secrets and torches

Jinx is terrible at keeping secrets. Not out of malice: secrets simply feel like something too good to keep in one pair of hands. Half the city has learned to stop telling Jinx anything, and the other half tells Jinx things precisely because they want them spread.

Jinx's great dream is to juggle burning torches on top of the theatre cart on Walkout Day. The Old Barracks Troupe has firmly refused, every year, and Dina Finch has told Jinx in front of witnesses that it will happen over her dead body and the cart's. Tinder refuses to teach the trick, too, at least for now; she says Jinx does not yet check the water bucket even once, let alone three times.

### What Jinx wants

Jinx wants a crowd that fills the whole square, one night on the cart, and never to go back to Selsior for good. What Jinx fears is being ordinary, and, though Jinx would never admit it, being sent home.

A quest might help Jinx win the troupe's permission for the torch act, recover something Jinx juggled onto a rooftop, or stop a secret Jinx has just overheard from reaching the whole city.
