Ivo Sable runs a little clinic under a patched green awning on the edge of Lantern Square, with a cot, a stool, a cabinet of jars and a kettle that is always warm. He is forty-four, unhurried and very hard to rattle, and his patients agree that the worst part of having a cut stitched by Ivo is the conversation. He talks to every one of them about music, which songs they like, which fiddler is best, whether Drum's buckets count as an instrument, and by the time they have finished arguing, the stitches are in.

### Coming to the square

Ivo grew up on a farm in the orchard country outside Kaldemor, the youngest child of a family of apple growers, and came to Urbanograd at nineteen to be a singer. He was not a good singer. He was, it turned out, very good at patching up the other musicians after they fell off things, and an old street healer called Henna Bright, who kept the awning on the square in those days, noticed. She took him on, taught him to set bones and clean wounds, and scolded him for humming while he worked until she gave up and started humming along. When she died fifteen years ago, she left him the awning, the cabinet and the kettle, and he has kept all three going ever since.

### Everyday work

For most of those years, Ivo's work was the city's own small disasters: rooftop tumbles, burnt fingers from the workshops, sprained ankles from the dancing at the Night Market, and the occasional actor who took a stage fight too seriously. Tinder, the fire-eater, is his most frequent patient by far. He has treated her blistered lips and singed hands more times than he can count, and he likes her far too much to stop scolding her about the water bucket she already checks three times.

But that has been changing. More and more of the people on Ivo's cot are travelers hurt on the roads: bitten, clawed, thrown from frightened horses. The wounds are worse than they used to be, and they come in faster than his supplies do. The bandages, salves and dried herbs he relies on come in from outside the walls, and every season fewer carts bring them.

### Colleagues

Patch, the herbalist at the Gate Walk, sees the road's wounded first, and Ivo sees them second. The two healers send patients back and forth, share whatever supplies they have and trade remedies over tea. Patch scolds her patients and Ivo soothes his, and between them they think they cover most kinds of person.

Crow sells tonics at the Night Market, and Ivo does not doubt they work. What he doubts is that anyone, including Crow, knows which bottle is which. He has asked Crow to label them properly perhaps a hundred times. Crow has ignored him perhaps a hundred times. They have settled into an exasperated friendship in which Ivo sends Crow his patients and Crow sends Ivo the ones who drank the wrong bottle.

### Hopes and fears

Ivo wants a steady supply of clean bandages and a quiet week. He fears, more than he admits, the night someone comes in from the road whom he cannot help.

A quest might escort a cart of medical supplies to the city, gather herbs for his salves in fields too risky for him to visit, or finally sort and label Crow's bottles.
