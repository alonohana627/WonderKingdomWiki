Pip Marrow is nineteen, sharp-tongued and quick with a comeback, and she hates being introduced by her surname. It happens constantly. "This is Pip Marrow," people say, with a meaningful pause, and then they wait for her to say something brilliant, because she is Juna Marrow's great-granddaughter, and Juna Marrow's play is the reason the city exists. Pip usually says something rude instead. It is generally funnier than whatever they were hoping for, which only makes it worse.

### The Marrow line

Juna Marrow, the young playwright whose banned comedy sparked the Long Walk, had one daughter, Hazel, born in Urbanograd in the city's early years. Hazel had a son, Corin, and Corin had Pip. When Pip was three, a winter fever took both her parents within a week, and she was raised by her grandmother in a crooked little house near the Old Barracks.

Hazel Marrow was not a writer and never pretended to be. She was a seamstress with a dry wit and no patience for fuss, and she told Pip, often, that Juna had been a person and not a statue. She died four years ago, at eighty-eight, and Pip still misses her daily. Hazel was the one person in Urbanograd who never expected anything from the Marrow name.

### The weight of a play

Everyone else does. Pip grew up hearing her great-grandmother's lines from the cart every Walkout Day and being asked, from the age of seven, when she would write one of her own. So she did. She has written three plays and burned two of them. The third sits in a drawer, finished, and she has not decided yet whether it is good or merely not embarrassing.

Now she is working on a new comedy, and she will not show it to anyone: not her friends, not the troupe, not even Dina. She writes it late at night in a cheap notebook that she keeps on her person at all times. Even its title is a secret, and the only thing anyone knows about it is that she laughs at it sometimes when she thinks she is alone.

### The people around her

Dina Finch, director of the Old Barracks Troupe, has been Pip's mentor since Pip was small enough to hide in the costume baskets. Since Hazel died, Dina has kept a closer eye on her, teaching her to cut a scene, to time a joke and to survive a bad night, and never once telling her she has to be as good as Juna. Pip has noticed that, and is more grateful than she says.

Hambone, who keeps the old theatre cart, is a good friend. He swears some of the cart's planks come from Juna's own cart, and he lets Pip sit on them during rehearsals, humming the songs, while she pretends not to be moved.

Nix the mask maker has been asked to make a new Juna mask for this year's play and is terrified of getting her face wrong. Since the old pictures of Juna all disagree with one another, Nix has asked Pip to sit as the model. Pip has agreed, and complains about it constantly, and turns up early for every sitting.

### What she wants and fears

Pip wants to write something that is entirely hers, that people love without knowing her surname. She fears that whatever she writes will only ever be judged against a play written a hundred years ago, and that it will lose.

A quest might find a lost page of Juna's that tells Pip something about her great-grandmother as a person, recover Pip's notebook when it goes missing, or help her stage the new comedy in secret.
