Vane is the reason you can cross Urbanograd without touching the ground. The plank bridges that run between the city's rooftops, from chimney to chimney, gable to gable, tower to stacked tower, are Vane's to build and Vane's to mend, and Vane knows every one of them by name. Vane is thirty-seven, lean and weathered, with a coil of rope over one shoulder, a hammer through the belt and a habit, alarming to anyone watching, of testing every new plank by jumping on it as hard as possible. Vane is fearless, practical and calm in a high wind, and has been seen standing on a ridge in a gale, eating an apple, while the bridge beside it bucked like a horse.

### Learning the bridges

Vane was born in Urbanograd, high up, and was carrying nails for the old bridge-builder Corbett before Vane could carry anything else. Corbett had kept the bridges for most of his life and named every one he built: the Long Lean, Grandmother's Step, the Fiddler's Crossing above Lantern Square, the Wobbler, which does not wobble and never has, and the Kiss, which is short. He taught Vane that a bridge is only as good as the worst plank in it and that the wind is never your friend, only an honest enemy. When Corbett died, Vane was twenty-four and already doing the heavy work, and the bridges simply passed to the one who knew them.

Vane has added a good many since. The new ones get names too, chosen with care, because a rooftop runner has to be able to shout "not the Gutter Queen, take Old Copper" across three roofs in a hurry. There are more than two hundred bridges now, and Vane walks some part of them every morning, listening to the planks underfoot. A bad plank sounds different. Vane can hear it from the next roof.

### Rivet

Rivet, who built the lift at the end of Workshops Row, is Vane's partner on every project above the second floor. Rivet tests ropes by hanging off them; Vane tests planks by jumping on them; between them, there is not a roof in the city they do not have an opinion about. They have been arguing about the second lift Rivet wants near the Old Barracks for a year now, about where the platform should land and which bridge it would make redundant, and both are enjoying it. Bastian Krell's covered bridge over Lantern Square is another matter. Vane has told Bastian, more than once, that it would need more timber than reaches the city in a year. Bastian keeps campaigning anyway, and Vane keeps quietly measuring the gap, just in case.

Vane sometimes finds pictures on the undersides of the bridges in the morning, painted overnight: a heron, a laughing moon, a giant asleep. Vane knows perfectly well whose they are, and has said nothing, except to point out that paint does not keep the rain off a plank as well as tar.

### Scrap

Scrap is Vane's daughter, nine years old, who has never used a staircase in her life. She learned to walk on Vane's bridges and to read a plank by its sound before she could read a word, and she tests planks by jumping on them, which is the part of her upbringing Vane most regrets. Vane is trying very hard to raise her well: regular meals, clean ears, bed before the lanterns are all lit and a firm sense of where the edge of a roof is. Scrap agrees with the meals. Her collection of named lost things lives in a biscuit tin under a loose slate on Vane's roof, and Vane has been shown it many times, always under oath.

What frightens Vane, who is frightened of almost nothing, is the road. Scrap wants to be an adventurer like Shade, the knife-thrower who guides travelers into the city, and one day she will want to go out through the gate and see the roads for herself. Vane is grateful that Shade taught her how to fall safely, and dreads the day she wants to learn something more.

A quest might bring good timber safely into the city for a bridge, find out who has been loosening planks on the Long Lean at night, or follow Scrap when she slips out toward the gate.
