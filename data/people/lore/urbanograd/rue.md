Rue will play Juna Marrow on Walkout Day this year, on the cart in the main square, in front of the whole city. She still cannot quite believe it, and some mornings she wakes up certain it was a dream. She is twenty, quiet and observant, the sort of person who notices which board on the stage creaks and who in the audience is about to sneeze. Until the very moment she steps on, she is stricken with stage fright: pale, cold-handed, sure she has forgotten every word. Then she walks into the light, and she is fearless.

### From the farmlands

Rue grew up on a farm in the farmlands around Kaldemor, the third of five children, in a family that grows barley and keeps geese. Her parents and siblings are still there, busy with the harvest, and write to her when the carts are running. Nobody in her family has ever acted, and none of them quite understands why she wanted to, though her mother sends a cake every Walkout Day for luck.

What Rue loved, as a child, was watching. She would sit at the back of the harvest fair and study how the storytellers held a crowd, and then go home and do it all again for the geese. Three years ago, at seventeen, she rode into Urbanograd on a cider cart with a bag of clothes and no plan beyond seeing a real play.

She found work with the Old Barracks Troupe the way Dina Finch once did: pushing the cart, fetching pins for Thimble, sweeping the stage after rehearsals. For two years she never said a line. She learned all of them anyway.

### The audition

This spring Dina held auditions for Juna, and Rue put her name down on a dare from herself. Several older actresses tried for the part. When Rue's turn came, she froze in the wings, so completely that Hambone went to fetch her a cup of water. Then she walked on and owned the stage, and Dina, who had watched it all, gave her the part before the end of the day. Nobody was more surprised than Rue.

Dina is her director now, and gives her notes even at breakfast. Rue writes every one of them down. Dex, who has played the grandee for fifteen years, has been generous with her, teaching her where the laughs fall and how to wait for them; little Sprout has decided she is his favorite Juna ever, and tells everyone so. Pip Marrow, Juna's great-granddaughter, is sitting as the model for the new Juna mask Nix is making, which means Rue will spend Walkout Day wearing a likeness of Pip's face on her own. The two of them have not yet worked out whether that is funny or strange, and suspect it is both.

### Pounce and Crumb

Rue practices her lines on the Rooftops, usually to Pounce, the big grey cat who sleeps in Perch's pigeon loft. Pounce is not much help. He yawns at the best speeches and walks away during the climax, which Rue has decided is good training for a real audience.

Crumb, the baker from the farmlands, is her friend in the city. They met at the Night Market in her first week, when she recognized the shape of his rolls from the harvest fairs back home, and they have looked out for each other ever since. He saves her the burnt buns she prefers, and she listens when he is homesick, because she is too.

### What she wants and fears

Rue wants to get through Walkout Day without freezing for good, and, secretly, to write home that she was good. She fears the day the fright does not lift when she steps on, and the whole square sees it.

A quest might carry a letter or a lucky cake from her family through the dangerous roads in time for Walkout Day, coax Pounce down from somewhere he should not be before a rehearsal, or help Rue find a quiet place to practice that nobody knows about.
