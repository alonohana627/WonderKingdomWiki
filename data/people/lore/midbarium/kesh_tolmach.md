Kesh is nine years old, and there is no traveler in Midbarium he has not asked where they came from and what the road was like. He is chatty, curious and fearless, the son of Harra, who sells the Tolmach's wool and cheese at the Trading Post, and Yarrek, the family's gentle goat herder. Between a mother who can be heard across the whole market and a father who can go a whole morning without a word, Kesh seems to have inherited all of the talking and none of the quiet.

### The kids of the herd

Kesh was born in the Tolmach camps and has never slept more than a few nights under a roof. Like every Tolmach child, he was put to work with the herds as soon as he could walk without falling over a goat. His father has trusted him with the kids of the herd since last year: the young goats, all knees and appetite, who wander off the moment anyone stops watching them. Kesh has named every one of them, as his father taught him, though his names tend to be things like Grumble, Biter and Lady Snort, which Yarrek pretends to find dignified.

He is not yet trusted with the grown goats. He knows exactly why, and he thinks it is unfair. Lately, though, he has stopped asking. Last month three of his father's goats came back from the valley wild-eyed and biting, and Yarrek keeps them penned apart, hoping they will calm. Kesh has watched his father sit beside that pen every evening, talking softly to animals that will not let him near, and it is the first thing in his life that has ever made Kesh go quiet.

### Family

His grandmother Mabbu keeps the home camp now that her knees will not follow the herds, and Kesh spends long afternoons with her, being scolded and fed in the same breath. She remembers every spring that ever ran, out loud, and Kesh is the only one of her grandchildren who asks her to tell it all again. His aunt Sennu is chieftain of the Tolmach. His cousin Ennek is a canyon guide, thirteen years older, and the most interesting person Kesh knows: Ennek has walked every road to the lowlands and met traders from every town in Veligor. Kesh questions him relentlessly. Ennek tells him the funny stories and leaves out the frightening ones, and Kesh has noticed exactly where the gaps are.

At the Trading Post, Kesh runs errands between his mother's stall and Corwell's counter. He has learned that his mother and Corwell argue loudly about prices all day and share a pot of tea when the market closes, and that this is apparently what friendship looks like among traders.

### Korrin and the lookout

Kesh's best friend is Korrin, the youngest son of the Kragvar chieftain. Their families share water on the terraces, and the boys met as small children hiding under the same cistern lid from their respective parents. Korrin climbs and Kesh talks, and between them they get into a remarkable amount of trouble.

Above the terraces they have a secret lookout, a ledge with a little stone wall the two of them built, where they watch for monsters coming up the canyons. So far they have mostly seen eagles. Kesh keeps a count of them with notches on a stick, which he learned from watching Korrin's mother keep her cistern tallies. He also keeps a second, much shorter stick, for things he cannot name. It has two notches, both for something moving on the far canyon bridge after dark.

### What he wants

Kesh wants to see the lowlands, all of them, and then come home and tell everybody about it. He wants to be trusted with the grown goats. And he wants his father's three goats to get better, more than he has ever said aloud.

A quest could ask Kesh what is on his second stick, or help him find out what happened in the valley to make his father's goats wild.
