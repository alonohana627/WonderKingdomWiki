Zirra is the bowyer of the Uskari Rim, and every bow the Uskari hunt and patrol with has passed through her hands. She is sixty-three, precise, sardonic and impossible to flatter. She judges people by how they hold a bow, and she will tell you what your grip says about you whether you asked or not. Compliments slide off her like rain off oiled horn. The only praise she trusts is a straight arrow.

### Big sister

Zirra is the older sister of Hask, the Old One, fifteen years his elder. Their mother died when Hask was six and Zirra twenty-one, and their father, a hunter, was out on the rims more than he was home, so Zirra did much of the work of raising her small, quiet brother. She taught him to string his first bow and told him his stance was a disgrace, which is what she tells everyone. Both their parents are long dead now.

She teases Hask mercilessly in private. She calls him "little brother" in front of nobody, reminds him of every childhood fall and sulk he would like forgotten, and refuses to stand when he enters a room. Let anyone else criticise him, though, and they will find out how sharp a bowyer's tongue can be. She thinks he is the best Old One in living memory and will never say so to his face. Hask, who knows her better than anyone, has never needed her to.

Zirra has never married. She says she has never met a man who held a bow well enough.

### The bowyer's craft

Zirra's workshop sits on the rim, open to the wind, hung with staves curing in the dry air. She makes bows, arrows, climbing ropes and hunting knives, and sells them to anyone with the coin, but the Uskari patrols come first. Since Tavi doubled the patrols, she has been making bows twice as fast as she likes to, and she has not lowered her standards by a hair, which means she sleeps less. Her climbing ropes are the best on the rim, and the one thing she and Vosk the bridge-master agree on is that nobody else's rope is quite good enough.

Her best staves come from the twisted trees that grow low in the canyon valleys, wood that has fought the wind and the drought for a hundred years and bends without breaking. She used to gather it herself each autumn. The valleys where the best stands grow are now marked on Skarra's map with red, and the patrols cannot spare anyone to walk her down there. Her stock of good wood is lower than it has been in her life.

### Pupils

Zirra has taught many young Uskari the bow, and the pupil she is proudest of is Ketta. When Ketta's mother fell from the rim, Zirra watched the twelve-year-old shoot for an afternoon, told her her stance was a disgrace, and spent the next five years correcting it. When Ketta came home last spring from three years of adventuring, the first thing she did was show Zirra her new bow, and the second was to be told what was wrong with it. Zirra has noticed, as Tavi has, that Ketta goes quiet about one thing. Zirra has not asked. She has made Ketta a new bow instead, the best she has made in years, and left it at Ketta's fire without a word.

### What she fears

Zirra fears for her brother, who carries four families and sleeps too little. She fears running out of good wood in the very years the rim needs bows most.

A quest could escort Zirra down to the twisted trees in the canyon valleys to cut new staves, or carry a message from her to Hask that she would rather die than deliver in person.
