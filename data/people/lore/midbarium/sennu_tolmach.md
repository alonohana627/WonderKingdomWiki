Sennu leads the Tolmach from a hide tent like any other herder's, pitched wherever the herds happen to be grazing, and she would not trade it for the finest house in the Kragvar Quarter. She is fifty, weathered and wry, and she has a herder's patience and a herder's habit of counting heads twice. The family chose her as chieftain thirteen years ago, after old Bekku died, because she had walked more of the high paths than anyone her age and because nobody had ever seen her lose her temper with a goat.

### A life on the move

Sennu was born in a camp on the high pastures and spent her childhood following her mother, Mabbu, from spring to spring. Mabbu has walked every herding path in the highlands at least a hundred times, and she taught Sennu where every one of them leads and where water can be found in a dry year. Mabbu's knees no longer let her follow the herds, so she keeps the home camp now and remembers, out loud and at length, every spring that ever ran. Sennu listens to all of it. She knows that one day those memories will be the only map left.

Her sister Harra takes the herds' wool, hides and cheese down to the Trading Post and drives famously hard bargains with lowland traders. The two of them argue about prices and agree about everything else. Sennu's son Ennek leads travelers up the old Tolmach paths, as the family has done since the founding, and she is quietly proud that the traders ask for him by name.

As chieftain, Sennu moves the camps with the seasons as the Tolmach always have, settles the family's disputes over grazing and water, and sits in council with the Old One and the other chieftains. She is not a talker in council. She lets Dorrek thunder and Tavi push, and then she says something dry that makes them both feel slightly foolish.

### This year

This year Sennu has kept the herds closer to town than ever before, because of the monsters in the low valleys. She has lost animals before to cats and storms and bad footing, but never like this: whole flocks scattered in a night, and herders who come back with stories they do not want to tell twice. Keeping the herds near town keeps them alive, but the high pastures closest to Midbarium are starting to suffer for it. The grass is thin, the ground is churned, and Sennu can count, as well as anyone, how many seasons the land can take this before it gives out.

That is where she and Tavi of the Uskari disagree. Tavi wants the herds close, where the trail patrols can guard them. Sennu wants the patrols pushed farther out, into the valleys, so the herds can go back to the pastures they have always used. They need each other and they know it, and they are uneasy allies on the trails because of it: each respects the other, and each thinks the other is asking too much. Sennu has told Hask, the Old One, that the arrangement will hold for one more year. She has not told him what happens after that, because she does not know.

### What she wants

Sennu wants the old paths open again. She wants Ennek to lead traders up the south path without looking over his shoulder, and she wants Mabbu to see the herds on the high pastures one more time. She fears the day a spring that has always run goes dry, because the Tolmach have survived every hard year by knowing where water is, and she does not know what they will do if the land starts forgetting.

She has no idea yet that Ennek has been keeping something from her.

A quest might help Sennu scout a safe route back to a high pasture, or carry a message between her and Tavi when the two of them are too proud to deliver it themselves.
