Hask was born on the Uskari Rim, the younger brother of Zirra the bowyer, and he grew up the way Uskari children do: climbing before he could properly walk, tracking goats along the canyon edges and listening to his elders argue about everything. He was never the loudest of them. He was the one who waited until the arguing was done and then said, quietly, what everyone had been circling around. By the time he was thirty-three, that habit had made him chieftain of the Uskari, chosen around the family fire after old Chieftain Orvek died of a winter fever fifteen years ago.

### The choosing

Hask led the Uskari for nine years. Then Old One Varra of the Brenna died in her sleep at Brenna House, aged eighty-one, after twenty-five years as the voice of all four families. Varra had been patient, funny and very hard to argue with, and the families mourned her for a long time.

Six years ago, the four families gathered in the great hall around the council fire to choose who would follow her. Odda, chieftain of the Brenna, hosted the choosing as her family always does, and Thrun beat the great drum all night. By custom no family may hold the title twice running, so the Brenna could not follow Varra. Dorrek of the Kragvar argued long and hard for an Old One from his own family, and many listened, but by dawn the families had settled on Hask. He was forty-two, one of the youngest Old Ones the Brenna songs name. He gave up the Uskari chieftainship that same night, as custom asks, and the Uskari chose Tavi to lead them before the fire burned down.

### How he leads

Hask is quiet, patient and hard to rattle. He sits through a whole loud council without a word, lets every chieftain shout until they are hoarse, and then speaks once and plainly. Once he closes a council, the families stand by the decision, and that, more than anything, is what makes an Old One.

He leans on a small circle of people. Ulm, keeper of the council fire for forty years, is his most trusted counsel, and Hask often sits with him late at night watching the flames. Odda, who hosted his choosing, is his adviser and his friend; she can tell him in one story what he has been doing wrong for a month. Dorrek is his bluntest critic and, Hask has learned, one of his most loyal; the Kragvar chieftain never once complained about losing the choosing, and Hask respects him for it more than he can say. Tavi, his successor among the Uskari, still walks the worst stretch of trail personally, which Hask understands and worries about in equal measure. Sennu of the Tolmach is the one he watches most closely this year, because her herds are kept nearer town than ever and the high pastures are paying for it.

Zirra teases him mercilessly in private, calls him "little brother" in front of nobody and defends him fiercely to anyone else. Hask has never married. He says the Old One belongs to four families, and that is family enough.

### What troubles him

Hask's private worry is the canyon patrols. Every season they are stretched thinner, and every season the families are a little too proud to say so in council. The Uskari patrol the trails, the Tolmach guard the herds, the Kragvar mend the bridges, and the Brenna mend the people, and every one of them reports that all is well. Hask suspects none of them is telling the whole truth, because he knows he would not either.

He also carries the weight of being young for the post. An Old One can be any age, but Hask feels that he will be in the great hall for a long time, and that the hardest years of the Age of Disturbance may well fall within his.

A quest could begin with Hask quietly asking an outsider to walk the patrols and tell him the truth about them, or with a rope bridge gnawed through on the night before a gathering.
