Odda has led the Brenna for twenty years, and in all that time nobody has seen her raise her voice. She is sixty-eight, warm, shrewd and endlessly calm, and she can settle a quarrel between two chieftains with one well-chosen story. The Brenna are Midbarium's healers, storytellers and drummers, the keepers of the families' memory, and Odda has always believed that a good story, told at the right moment, is a kind of medicine.

### The envoy and the teacher

Odda's friendship with Fairy Forest began forty-seven years ago. At twenty-one she was sent to the forest as a young Brenna envoy, carrying on a tie between the Brenna and the Magicians older than the kingdom itself. In her first week she got lost on the Lantern Paths after dark, and a young teacher from the Grove School named Calyra, then twenty-seven and in her third year of teaching, found her sitting under a lantern trying to look as though she had meant to be there. Calyra walked her home without a word of teasing, and only teased her about it for the next forty-seven years.

Odda stayed three winters in the forest. She taught Calyra Brenna bonesetting and the uses of highland herbs; Calyra taught her forest plant-lore and how to read the oldest Magician script, which Odda still reads slowly and proudly. When Odda came home, they agreed to keep the friendship alive. Every spring since, Odda has written a letter ahead to the forest and then followed it a few weeks later with a bundle of highland herbs, and she has come home each time with forest herbs for the Brenna healers. These days she travels with Uskari escorts on the worst roads, and grumbles that it makes the walk far less peaceful. Calyra is Eldest of the Grove now, and Odda says that suits her perfectly, because Calyra has been telling people what to do since she was twenty-seven.

### The Brenna and the choosing

Odda was chosen chieftain of the Brenna at forty-eight. She married Thrun, the drummer whose great drum calls the families to every gathering, and their daughter Lessa is the best bonesetter in the highlands. Lessa's daughter Neffa is learning the long song of the Old Ones from Odda's cousin Ahvi, and Odda listens to her practise with her eyes closed so the child will not see how proud she is.

The Brenna host every choosing. Six years ago, when Old One Varra died, Odda hosted the one that named Hask. Varra had been Ahvi's sister, and so Odda's cousin as well as her friend, and Odda remembers that night as both the saddest and the finest of her life: Ahvi singing the long song, Thrun drumming until dawn, Dorrek arguing for the Kragvar, and Hask, quiet as ever, accepting a title he had not asked for. Odda is Hask's adviser now and, he says, his friend. She does not tell him what to do. She tells him stories, and he works out the rest.

### What worries her

Lessa has treated more monster bites this year than in her first ten years of healing together, and the far-slope herbs she needs are running short. Ahvi is troubled by an old verse about beasts growing wild before the Burning came, and has asked Odda whether to sing it at the next gathering. Odda has not yet answered. She believes the families deserve the truth, and she knows what a verse like that might do to a frightened hall.

She fears, too, that one spring the road to the forest will be closed, and that she will have to send her letter and stay at home.

A quest might escort Odda's spring bundle of herbs to Fairy Forest, gather the far-slope herbs Lessa needs, or help her decide what to do about Ahvi's verse.
