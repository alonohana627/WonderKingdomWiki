Brakka is nineteen, the elder child of Dorrek, chieftain of the Kragvar, and Gorra, keeper of the cisterns. She is eager, restless and quick with a chisel, and she would rather carve something new than mend something old. This makes her an excellent apprentice mason in every respect but one, because in Midbarium almost everything a mason does is mending.

### Stone and string

Brakka was chiselling patterns into her parents' doorstep before she could spell her name, and at fifteen she was apprenticed to her father's cousin Vosk, master of bridges, who keeps every rope-and-plank bridge in the town sound and has taught three generations of Kragvar to splice a cable. Vosk is careful, dry and suspicious of anything that sways. Brakka is none of those things. For four years they have worked side by side in a state of mutual exasperation that has slowly become mutual respect. She has learned to tie a knot he will trust, which she is told is rare. He has learned that she will finish a long job faster than anyone in the quarter, provided he tells her it is impossible first.

Most of her days are spent replacing worn planks, re-pointing terrace walls and hauling rope, and she does it well and complains about it cheerfully. But every flat stone in the Kragvar Quarter carries one of her drawings.

### The stairway

Brakka dreams of carving a new stairway down the cliff to the valley floor, the first new route down from the town in a hundred years. The last was Durra's Stair, cut by a Kragvar mason of that name when Brakka's great-great-grandparents were young, and every Kragvar child knows the song about it. Since then, the Kragvar have only mended what their ancestors cut.

She was eight when her great-uncle Baruk, then chieftain, died in the rockfall on the old north cliff stair. She remembers her father coming home with his hands torn from digging, and she remembers that the stair was patched afterwards, not rebuilt, because nobody had the heart to cut a new one. Her stairway would run along sounder rock farther south, with landings set into the cliff rather than hung from it, so that no landing could be swept away again. She has never told anyone that this is why she drew it.

Her mother has studied the sketches more carefully than Brakka knows, and asked only where the builders would get their water. Her father calls the whole plan foolish, loudly and often. Brakka has no idea that he keeps a copy of it under his bed.

### Family and friends

Her little brother Korrin, ten years younger, climbs everything and wants to join the Uskari trail watch, which the family finds very funny. Brakka laughs with the rest, but she once climbed up alone and inspected the little stone wall at the boys' secret lookout above the terraces, and found it surprisingly sound. She has said nothing to Korrin, because he would never stop boasting about it. Her grandfather Ulm keeps the council fire in the great hall, and she brings him wood when she remembers, which is less often than she should.

### What troubles her

Twice this season, she and Vosk have climbed out to the far canyon bridge at dawn and found its ropes gnawed half through. Vosk does not know what is doing it, and that frightens Brakka more than the ropes do, because Vosk knows everything about bridges. She has started to wonder whether any new route down the cliff would only be a new way for something to come up. She has not stopped drawing. But she has started drawing gates.

### Hooks

A quest could help Brakka survey the rock for her stairway, which would mean climbing the cliff below the town where few have gone in years, or sit up with her on the far bridge to see what comes for the ropes.
