Ulm has tended the council fire in Midbarium's great hall for forty years, and in all that time he has never once let it burn low. He is seventy-four, gruff and slow-moving, with hands so scarred from the wood and the embers that he can lift a burning log without flinching. He is fiercely proud of his duty, and he talks to the fire more kindly than to most people.

### The fire that never goes out

The fire in the great hall is the heart of Midbarium. The four families choose their Old One around it, the chieftains meet in council beside it, and by custom it is never allowed to go out. The Kragvar, who raised the hall, have always kept it, and they count that as their proudest duty. The keeper sleeps in a small room behind the hearth, and in practice hardly sleeps at all.

Ulm was thirty-four when the old keeper died and the Kragvar asked him to take the fire. He had been a mason until then, and a good one, and some in the family thought he would make a chieftain one day. When the Kragvar chieftain of those years died, the family did look to Ulm first, and he refused them. A chieftain leads from the front, he said, and the keeper of the fire does not leave it. So the family chose his younger brother Baruk instead, and Ulm stayed by the hearth.

Eleven years ago Baruk died in the rockfall on the old north cliff stair, and Ulm's son Dorrek dug for him until his hands bled. Ulm did not go. Somebody had to keep the fire, and he kept it all that night with the whole hall empty and the sound of the digging carrying up the cliff. He has never said what that night cost him. The family chose Dorrek as chieftain the following evening, and Ulm, who had refused the post himself, was prouder than he has ever admitted.

### Family

Ulm's wife Hessa, a Kragvar stone-carver who could cut a face into granite that looked ready to speak, died sixteen years ago, and Ulm rarely speaks of her except to the fire. Dorrek visits him most evenings, and the two of them sit by the fire and say very little, which suits them both. Gorra, Dorrek's wife, sends him a jug of the coldest cistern water every evening, which is the closest thing to fussing he will allow. His granddaughter Brakka brings him wood, when she remembers. His grandson Korrin is the only person allowed to sit on the woodpile while Ulm tends the fire, and the boy listens to the old songs with his mouth open.

### The Old One's counsel

Old One Hask trusts Ulm more than anyone. Many nights, long after a council has shouted itself hoarse and gone home, Hask sits with Ulm and watches the flames, and the two of them talk about whatever the council would not. Ulm tells him plainly what the Kragvar are too proud to say, and Hask listens. Ulm thinks Hask is the best Old One he has served under, and the most tired, and he worries about both.

### The ones who stayed

Ulm knows every Kragvar song about the fighters who stood in the front line of the Balrog War and never came home, but stayed beneath the World Tree to keep watch. When he was young, those songs were simply history. Lately he finds himself wondering aloud, to the fire mostly, whether anyone down there still remembers the Kragvar: whether the songs are true, whether those fighters' children's children still keep watch, and whether they know the names that Midbarium still sings for them. He does not know. Nobody in Midbarium does.

He fears that the families are forgetting how to ask for help, and that the fire will outlast the people who gather around it.

### Hooks

A quest could carry a Kragvar song, and Ulm's question, down toward the World Tree, or sit up one night by the fire while Ulm tells the Old One what the council will not.
