Corwin is fifty-two and has been captain of the Heron for twenty years, running cargo up and down the coast of Veligor without losing a single crate. He is calm, dry and slow to speak, and he never raises his voice, because he has never needed to. Crews who have sailed with him say that a quiet word from Corwin is worse than a shouting from any other captain in the harbour, and that he has never once given an order twice.

### Twenty years without a crate

Corwin went to sea as a cabin boy and worked his way aft over many years, as a deckhand, then a bosun, then a first mate on other men's ships. He bought a share in the Heron at thirty-two, and the rest of her a few years later with money he had saved by never wasting any. The Heron is not the fastest ship in the harbour, a fact Captain Seraphine likes to mention, but she is the most reliable. Merchants who want their goods to arrive on time, dry and complete send them on the Heron, and they pay a little more for it without complaining.

That record is Corwin's pride. He keeps every cargo list he has ever signed in a sea chest in his cabin, and he can tell you what the Heron carried on any voyage in the last twenty years if you give him a minute.

### The council

Corwin sits on the harbour council, and Quenna, the Harbour Master, counts him among the people she relies on most. He is not her friend in the way Mabyn is, but she trusts his judgement because he hates waste of every kind: wasted money, wasted time, wasted words. He also loves a good argument, and the council gives him one most weeks.

His favourite opponent is Captain Annis of the Saltlark. They disagree on almost everything: harbour dues, the order ships should load in, whether a captain ought to cast off under a new moon. Since the deep water turned strange, their arguments have turned to it. Annis wants the council to act on what the sailors are seeing. Corwin wants to know what, exactly, she would have them do, and what it would cost. In truth he takes the sea as seriously as she does, and he has more than once carried his own marks to Ambrose the chartmaker after a bad night. He simply will not agree with Annis in public, because neither of them would know what to do if he did.

### The cost of safety

Corwin now pays adventurers to sail with every voyage, and he grumbles about the cost at every council meeting. His regular hire is Tristram, a harpooner who used to hunt big fish and now stands at the Heron's bow in deep water, still as a post, for hours at a time. Corwin thinks Tristram is worth every coin, and complains about every coin anyway.

### Family

Corwin has no wife or children. His nephew Caspian is the Heron's navigator, the youngest in the harbour, and Corwin is quietly very proud of him, though he would sooner lose a crate than say so. He knows Caspian hopes to navigate the race boat this summer. He has also noticed that the boy goes pale when a storm builds, and he has said nothing about that either. This summer he has taken on Lowen, the fisherman's son and Caspian's friend, as a deckhand, on Caspian's word.

### Hooks

A quest could sail with the Heron as a hired guard, find out why one of her regular cargoes has started arriving short, or settle, once and for all, one of Corwin's arguments with Annis.
