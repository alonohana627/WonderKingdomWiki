Mabyn keeps the door of the Harbour Hall. She opens it at dawn and closes it at dusk, and in between she greets every captain, merchant, fisherman and lost traveller who comes up the steps as if they had come all this way just to see her. She is seventy-four, gentle, chatty and endlessly fond of the old stories, and she knows more tales of Old Bain than anyone alive. She is sure at least half of them are true. She is not always sure which half.

### The galley years

Mabyn went to sea at fifteen as a cook's girl, peeling potatoes in the galley of a coasting trader, and she was a ship's cook for most of her life after. She cooked on half a dozen ships along the coast of Veligor, and sailors still tell each other which of her stews they would sail through a gale for. She was the cook aboard the Sandpiper, a small coasting ship long since broken up, when a twelve-year-old deck girl called Quenna came aboard, loud, hungry and certain of everything. Mabyn fed her, scolded her, bandaged her rope-burned hands and listened to her plans for owning her own ship one day. Quenna is now Harbour Master, and Mabyn is still the person she talks to when she cannot talk to anyone else.

When Mabyn's knees gave up on galley ladders twenty-one years ago, she came ashore and asked the council for the job of keeping the hall door. She has kept it ever since. She never married and has no children of her own, but half the children in Bain Harbour grew up on her stories, and she counts them all.

### The boat-hook

Every morning, before she unbolts the door, Mabyn climbs a little stool and polishes Old Bain's boat-hook, which hangs above the door of the Harbour Hall. It is a long, dark, battered thing of ash and iron, and its shaft is covered in nicks. Mabyn can tell you which nick came from which legendary fight, and she taught every one of them to Jowan, the Harbour Master's son, when he was a small boy running errands through the hall. He can still recite them, and she is very proud of that.

### The tales

According to Mabyn, Old Bain was a fisherwoman who built the first ship big enough to sail all the way around Veligor, and then sailed it, with a crew of her own cousins and a goat for luck. According to Mabyn, she rowed out into a winter storm with that boat-hook to haul a capsized boat home by its stern ring, and the deepest nick on the shaft is where the ring bit. According to Mabyn, the summer race around the island began as a dare, when a crew from another harbour said they could do what Old Bain had done faster. Mabyn believes every word of the first two. She thinks the third is probably true, because it sounds like sailors.

She and Hobart, the warehouseman, have a long and happy disagreement about the four ancient crates at the back of his oldest warehouse. Hobart says they have been there since Old Bain's day. Mabyn says that in that case they are full of Old Bain's spare boat-hooks, and that she will polish those too if he ever opens them.

### The keeper of secrets

Quenna trusts Mabyn with anything at all. When a council meeting has gone badly, the Harbour Master sits on the hall steps after everyone has gone, and Mabyn sits beside her with a pot of tea and listens. What Quenna tells her there, Mabyn has never repeated to a single soul, not even in a story, and for a woman who loves to talk as much as she does, that is the greatest compliment she knows how to pay.

Ottilie, the clerk, works just inside the door, and the two of them are firm friends of opposite kinds: Ottilie tidy and quiet, Mabyn chatty and never quite finished with a sentence. Mabyn brings her a sweet bun every morning and has decided she works too hard.

### What she wants and fears

Mabyn wants the old stories to outlive her. She fears that the new stories the sailors are telling about the deep water will one day turn out to be the true half.

### Hooks

A quest could find the truth behind one of Mabyn's tales of Old Bain, find out whether the deepest nick on the boat-hook really came from a stern ring, or carry a pot of tea and a message from the Harbour Master that she cannot send through the council.
