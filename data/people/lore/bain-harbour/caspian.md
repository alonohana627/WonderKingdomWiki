Caspian is the navigator of the Heron, and at twenty he is the youngest navigator in Bain Harbour. He is confident, clever and a little vain about his star-reckoning: he can put the Heron's position on a chart within a mile on a clear night, and he will tell you so before you ask. He is Captain Corwin's nephew, and he is secretly, badly afraid of storms.

### The night of the storm

Caspian's father, Petroc, was a sailor on a coasting trader, and his mother, Kerra, is Corwin's younger sister. When Caspian was seven, a winter storm came up the coast in a single afternoon, and Petroc's ship did not come home. Caspian remembers standing on the harbour wall with his mother in the rain, watching the lighthouse beam swing out over water so rough it looked white, and waiting for a sail that never came. He has never told anyone that he remembers it so clearly.

His uncle looked after them both from then on, in the quiet way Corwin does everything: money left on the table without comment, a word with a merchant here and there, and a seat at his own table every time the Heron was in. When Caspian was thirteen, Corwin took him aboard as cabin boy. Five years ago his mother married again, a lamp-oil merchant from Selsior, and moved there with him. Caspian chose to stay with the Heron. She writes to him every midwinter, and he writes back at length about stars.

### Reading the sky

Caspian learned the names of the stars in Maren's front room on Merchant Street, sitting on an upturned crate, and outgrew her lessons in about a year. After that he learned from the Heron's old navigator, Jago, who taught him to take a star's height with a quadrant, to keep a dead-reckoning log in a hand neat enough for Ambrose to read, and never to trust a coastline in fog. When Jago died two years ago, Corwin made Caspian navigator in his place. Some of the older captains muttered that it was family favour. Caspian answered them by bringing the Heron home on time, every time, and he still enjoys mentioning it.

He is proud of his skill, and a bit vain about it, and he knows it. He keeps his quadrant polished like a sword and has opinions about every chart in Ambrose's shop. Corwin says nothing about any of this, which Caspian takes, correctly, as approval.

### Storms

The fear is the part nobody sees. On a clear night Caspian is the most confident sailor aboard. When the sky thickens and the swell begins to come wrong, his hands go cold and he cannot stop thinking of the harbour wall in the rain. He does his work anyway, perfectly, and believes no one has noticed. In fact his uncle has noticed him go pale more than once, and has said nothing about it, which is exactly how Corwin shows he cares.

### Lowen

Caspian's closest friend is Lowen, the fisherman's son, three years younger and far less sure of himself. They met on the harbour wall when Lowen was a boy gutting fish for his mother's stall and Caspian was a cabin boy showing off the stars, and Lowen was the only one who actually wanted to learn them. This summer, when Lowen wanted to go to sea against his father's wishes, Caspian put in a word with his uncle, and Corwin took Lowen on as a deckhand for the Heron's next coastal run. Caspian feels responsible for him, and a little frightened by how much.

### What he wants and fears

Caspian wants to be chosen as navigator for this summer's race around Veligor. The Harbour Master picks her own crew, and he means to make the choice obvious. He fears that the race, or some voyage after it, will run into a storm, and that the whole harbour will finally see his hands shake.

### Hooks

A quest could sail with the Heron through a bad night and help Caspian hold his nerve without letting anyone see, find the star sights Jago swore by in a notebook nobody has opened since his death, or help Caspian prove to Quenna that he is the navigator her race boat needs.
