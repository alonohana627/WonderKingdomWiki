Tegan is the sailmaker of Bain Harbour, and she has sewn the sails for half the ships in the harbour. She is forty-one, brisk, practical and a little sharp, and she always has a needle tucked behind her ear, where other people keep a pencil. She does not judge a ship by its lines or its paint or its captain. She judges it by its stitching, and she can tell you from the quay, with one look at a reefed mainsail, whether a ship has been cared for or merely sailed.

### The loft at the yard

Tegan grew up in the sail loft at the edge of the shipyard, a long, high room with a floor wide enough to spread a whole mainsail. Her mother, Kensa, was the sailmaker before her, and Tegan spent her childhood under the canvas, sorting thread, chasing dropped needles between the floorboards and being told to keep her feet off the cloth. Kensa put a palm and needle in her hands at eight and made her sew practice seams on scraps until they were straight enough to please her, which took about three years. Kensa died fifteen years ago, and Tegan, then twenty-six, has kept the loft ever since. She still sews with her mother's sail palm, a leather strap worn to the shape of a hand that was not quite hers.

Her oldest friend is Alwyn, Dunstan's son. They were both children underfoot in the yard, she in the loft and he among the timbers, and between them they fell off most of the things in the shipyard that could be fallen off. Alwyn's mother painted the names on the hulls in gold, and Tegan's mother made the sails that carried them away, and the two children used to sit on the slipway and argue about which mattered more. They still have that argument, about once a month, though now it is about whether a ship is a hull with sails or sails with a hull. Tegan is fond of Alwyn's children, too. Kit has asked her a great many questions about sails. Poppy draws them better than anyone else in the harbour, and Tegan says so, which is rare praise from her.

Tegan never married, and she says she never had the time. She lets the little room above her loft to Brisa of the Harbour Watch, who came to her at sixteen with nowhere else to go and very little money. Tegan has sewn the buttons back on Brisa's coat more times than she can count, and Brisa has never once buttoned it.

### A day in the loft

Tegan's days begin with the light, because sailmaking needs it. She cuts new canvas, sews new sails, and patches the old ones the captains bring in, spread out across the loft floor, and she buys her bolt rope, the rope that edges every sail, from Wilmot the ropemaker, because she says nobody else's rope sits as true along a seam. She made the tall new suit of sails for Captain Seraphine's Bright Morrow when Seraphine rebuilt the ship's rig nine years ago, and she has told Seraphine every summer since that she carries too much of them. She thinks Captain Corwin's sails are the best kept in the harbour and Captain Annis's are the oldest. She patches both without comment, which for Tegan is a compliment.

Since the deep water turned strange, the work has changed. Captains want heavier storm canvas, doubled seams and spare sails folded in the hold, and some of them want to talk about what tore the old ones, which Tegan does not encourage. She sews what she is asked and keeps her opinions for the stitching.

### The secret sails

This summer Dunstan asked Tegan to make the sails for the new race boat, and to make them in secret. He gave her the measurements on a scrap of paper and told her nothing about the hull, not even when she asked. She has patched and recut his race boats' sails for years, but she has never been asked to hide them before, and she is enjoying it very much. She sews the race sails after dark with the loft door barred and tells anyone who asks that she is making a new mainsail for a coaster. She will not tell even Alwyn, and the look on his face when she refuses is, she says, worth the extra lamp oil.

What she has not told Dunstan is that a sailmaker can learn a great deal about a boat from the measurements of its sails. She has done some quiet sums in the margin of that scrap of paper, and she has her own idea of what the new hull looks like. She keeps that to herself as well. It would spoil the secret, and it might be wrong.

### What she wants, and what she fears

Tegan wants to see her sails cross the finish line first, and she wants someday to hand the loft to someone whose seams are straight enough to deserve it. She has not yet found that person. She fears very little that she will admit to, but she does not like the sails that have come back to her this year from deep water with long, straight tears that no wind made.

### Hooks

A quest might help Tegan keep the race sails hidden from a nosy visitor, fetch a special bolt of canvas before the race, or find out whether Brisa, who sleeps right above the loft and hears the needle at night, has worked out what Tegan is sewing.
