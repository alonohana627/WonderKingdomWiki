Tully is a dockworker on the Bain Harbour docks, and he is probably the strongest man in the harbour, though he would never say so and would much rather talk about lunch. He is twenty-six, broad, easygoing and always hungry. He thinks every problem looks smaller after lunch, and he has found, over the years, that he is right about this more often than people who skip it.

### A boy on the docks

Tully has carried crates on the docks since he was a boy. His father, Treve, was a porter there before him, and Tully spent his early years sitting on coiled rope at the head of the pier, watching his father carry things. When Tully was eleven, Treve slipped on a wet gangplank with a barrel on his shoulder and hurt his back, and he never really mended; he died that winter. Tully came down to the docks the next spring, at twelve, asking for work, and Garrow, who had been foreman for two years then, looked at the size of his hands and gave him some. His mother, Lamorna, went to live with her sister in a fishing village up the coast a few years later. She writes to him twice a year, mostly to ask whether he is eating properly, and he writes back to tell her that he is.

Fourteen years on, Tully can lift more than any two other men on the docks, and he carries the things nobody else wants to: the lead-lined tea chests, the iron, the barrels that leak. Garrow shouts at him for eating on the job and then saves him the last of his own bread at the end of the day. Tully knows perfectly well that Garrow gives him the extra shifts first, and Garrow knows that he knows, and neither of them has ever said a word about it. Tully thinks Garrow is the best foreman on Veligor and that Garrow's second pier is a fine idea, and he has told several captains so, loudly, while Garrow pretended not to hear.

### His day

Tully's day starts before the first ship is tied up, with something to eat, and it goes on in the same way. He carries cargo off the ships and into the warehouses, where Hobart counts every crate he brings in and has never once taken Tully's count for it. He carries Finch, the dock office's errand boy, on his shoulders when the boy is too tired to run, and he shares his lunch with him on the warehouse step, which means that Finch eats well and Tully eats twice. He eats bread from Merchant Street, smoked fish from the Fish Market and whatever is left at the Salt and Bread at the end of the night, and he will talk to anyone at all, as long as there is food involved.

This spring Garrow stopped unloading at the far end of the outer pier after dark. Tully does not know why. It means the late cargoes have to be carried a long way round, and he grumbled about it for a week and then stopped, because Garrow would not look him in the eye about it, and Tully has decided that if Garrow does not want to tell him, there is a good reason.

### The boat

Tully is saving every coin he can to buy a small boat of his own. He has been saving for six years, in a tin box with a stone on the lid at the bottom of his sea chest, and he counts it every week on the night before his day off. He has about two thirds of what a small, plain boat from Dunstan's yard would cost, if Dunstan had time to build one, which this summer he does not. Tully wants to fish the bay in the mornings and carry small cargoes along the coast in the afternoons, and never again to carry anything for anyone but himself. He has walked down to the beach below the Waterfront more than once to look at the old boat that lies there under a sailcloth, and he has never quite worked up the nerve to ask whose it is.

He has heard the stories from the deep water like everyone else, and he has heard that Cobb no longer takes his boat past the middle of the bay. Tully says that he only wants a little boat for the bay anyway, and that the bay is a friendly place. He says it a little more often than he used to.

### What he fears

Tully fears very little that can be carried, and is not afraid of hard work or of anybody's temper. What he does fear, privately, is that by the time he has saved enough, there will be nothing left out on the water worth having a boat for.

### Hooks

A quest might find Tully a boat sooner than he hoped, help him carry something too heavy even for him, or have Hobart ask him at last to move the four old iron-bound crates at the back of the oldest warehouse, which nobody has shifted in living memory.
