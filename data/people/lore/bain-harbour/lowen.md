Lowen is seventeen, restless and good-natured, and he blushes to the ears whenever anyone praises him. He is the son of Cobb the fisherman and Gwennol the fishmonger, the older brother of Bree, and the grandson of Wenna, who mends nets on the fish market steps. His family have sold fish in Bain Harbour since it was just a cove. Lowen has just signed on to his first trading voyage, and his father did not want him to.

### The fish market

Lowen grew up behind his mother's stall, gutting fish before he could read and running crates up the steps to his grandmother's sharp corrections. He loves his family and he loves the market, but he has always looked past the harbour wall. The fishing boats come home every evening. The trading ships come home with stories.

His father knows every rock in the bay and has fished it all his life in a little boat built by his own grandfather. Cobb always meant to take Lowen out with him into deeper water once the boy was old enough. Then, twice this year, Cobb's nets came up torn clean through, and he stopped talking about deeper water at all. Lowen took this badly. He thought his father was keeping him ashore out of caution, and that if he waited for Cobb to be satisfied, he would wait forever.

### Signing on

So Lowen went to the docks. His friend Caspian, the young navigator of the Heron, put in a word with his uncle, Captain Corwin, and Corwin, who hates waste and saw a strong pair of arms, took Lowen on as a deckhand for the Heron's next run along the coast. Lowen told his parents the same evening. His mother shouted. His father said very little, which was worse. They have not quarrelled since, but Cobb has not said he is proud of him either, and Lowen has noticed.

Lowen is too eager to please, and on the docks it shows. He volunteers for every job, apologises for things that are not his fault, and says yes to anyone who asks for help. The older sailors find this either touching or useful, depending on the sailor.

### The race

What Lowen really wants is a place on the race crew this summer. He knows the Harbour Master picks her own crew, and he knows that a deckhand on his first voyage is not an obvious choice. He is hoping the voyage on the Heron will change that.

He has a coach, though not one he chose. Thaddeus, the retired captain who lost the race by a boat's length more than thirty years ago, has decided Lowen has the makings of a racing sailor and coaches him from his bench by the water whenever Lowen walks past. Lowen has started taking the long way round. He has also, privately, started doing what Thaddeus tells him, because some of it works.

His little sister Bree wants to sail in the race even more than he does, and reminds him daily that she would be better at it.

### What he fears

Lowen fears that he will freeze when it matters, and that his father was right.

### Hooks

A quest could join the Heron's voyage alongside Lowen, help him prove himself to the race crew, or find out what is tearing his father's nets before Lowen meets it on the open sea.
