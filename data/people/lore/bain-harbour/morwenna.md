Morwenna is a sailor of the Saltlark, and you will usually hear her singing before you see her: a shanty, a lullaby, half a hymn, always under her breath and always slightly out of tune. She is thirty-eight, cheerful and tough, and she has the oldest complaint of every sailor in Bain Harbour. She misses home as soon as she leaves it, and she misses the sea as soon as she is home.

### The lighthouse girl

Morwenna was born in the lighthouse on the headland. Her mother, Ysolde, has kept the light for forty years, and Morwenna grew up climbing its stairs, polishing its great lamp and watching the ships come in from the top of the tower while her mother told her exactly what she thought of every captain aboard them. Her father, Kenver, was a fisherman, and as soon as she was big enough she went out with him in his boat. He taught her to row, to read the weather and to sing at the oars, because, he said, a song keeps time better than counting. He died sixteen years ago, quite suddenly, mending his nets on the fish market steps. Morwenna still sings his songs.

### The Saltlark

After her father died Morwenna worked the Fish Market for a few restless years, and at twenty-six she went to Captain Annis of the Saltlark and asked to sail with her. Annis looked her up and down, asked her three questions about the weather and one about her boots, and took her on. That was twelve years ago. Morwenna has crewed the Saltlark ever since, and she knows the old ship and its older captain better than anyone. She ties her boots left first, double knot, never on deck, because Annis does, and she does not tease Annis about the pinch of salt over the stern, because she has come to suspect it works. Annis is blunt, superstitious and trusts the sea more than people; Morwenna is the person she trusts most of all, and both of them know it.

Morwenna married Tamar, who worked in the ropewalk on the Waterfront, and their son Ossie was born eight years ago. Tamar died of a lung fever the next winter, when Ossie was barely one. Since then, while Morwenna is at sea, Ossie lives at the lighthouse with his grandmother, and when Morwenna is home she lives there too, back in the little room at the bottom of the tower where she slept as a girl. Ossie has decided to be a lighthouse keeper. Morwenna is secretly relieved that he has not decided to be a sailor.

### What she has not told them

On the Saltlark's last voyage, in deep water on a still night, something large brushed along the keel from bow to stern and was gone. The whole crew felt it. Morwenna was at the wheel. Annis turned the ship for home at once and took the mark to Ambrose the chartmaker the next morning.

Morwenna has not told her mother or her son. She tells herself she is protecting Ossie, who would ask questions about it one at a time for a month, and her mother, who is seventy and climbs a hundred and seventeen stairs every night. Really, she does not want to hear it said aloud in the lighthouse, where she has always felt safe. Annis has noticed and has not pressed her. What Morwenna does not know is that her mother, from the top of the tower, has been seeing pale lights moving under the water far out past the headland, and has not told her either.

### Hooks

A quest could carry a message between Morwenna and her mother that neither will say in person, sail with the Saltlark back to the place where the keel was brushed, or find Morwenna a song that will get Ossie to sleep on the nights she is away.
