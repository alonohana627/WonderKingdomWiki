Tristram is a harpooner, and the captains of Bain Harbour hire him to stand at the bow in deep water and watch. He is thirty-nine, patient, still and serious, and he can wait for hours without moving a muscle, his harpoon held loosely in one hand, as if he were part of the ship. He laughs rarely, but when he does he laughs completely, with his head back and his eyes shut, and people who have heard it tend to remember where they were.

### The big fish

Tristram learned the harpoon from his father, who hunted big fish out of the harbour in a narrow boat called the Patience and died in his bed, at a great age, ten years ago. The harpoon Tristram carries was his father's; the shaft has been replaced three times and the head never. From the age of eighteen Tristram hunted the deep water himself, alone in the Patience, for the great fish that come along the coast in late summer. It is slow, quiet work, and he loved it: days of waiting, then a few moments in which everything matters.

Then the deep water changed. The big fish stopped running where they always had, and the things that came up in their place did not behave like fish. Tristram hauled the Patience up on the beach below the Waterfront, where she still lies under a sailcloth, and went to work for the captains instead.

### At the bow

Captain Corwin of the Heron is his regular employer. Corwin is the most careful captain in the harbour and grumbles at the council about what adventurers cost him, but he pays Tristram without complaint and thinks him worth every coin. Tristram likes Corwin because Corwin never asks him to talk. On the Heron he stands at the bow from the moment the ship passes the headland until she is back inside it, and in all the voyages he has made, he has thrown the harpoon at something only twice. He does not describe either time. He says only that he missed once.

On shore he works with Keziah, the sword for hire who sails with Captain Seraphine. They met in her first week in the harbour, when the same captain turned them both down on the same morning, and that afternoon they cleared the rocks below the headland of crabs big enough to take a boot off. She watches everything and he waits for everything, and they have been partners since. She is the only person who can make him laugh on purpose. He has noticed that she goes grey as a sail every time she steps aboard a ship. He has not mentioned it, and he will not.

### Nerissa

Tristram's sweetheart is Nerissa, the pearl diver, who is fearless, quiet and happiest underwater. They suit each other: neither of them says more than they need to, and they can sit together on the harbour wall for an evening without saying anything at all. On her last dive beyond the harbour wall, Nerissa saw something move in the dark below the reef, and she has not gone down since. She will not say why, even to him. Tristram has not pressed her. It frightens him more than anything he has seen from the bow, because he has never known her afraid of the water.

### What he wants

Tristram wants to know what is down there, before anyone else does. He does not want to kill it. He wants to see it clearly, once, and understand it, the way he used to understand the big fish. He fears that by the time anyone understands it, it will be too late to matter.

### Hooks

A quest could stand the bow watch with Tristram, help him get the Patience back in the water for one more voyage of his own, or go down below the reef to find what Nerissa saw.
