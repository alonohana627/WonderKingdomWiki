Brisa is a watchwoman of the Harbour Watch, and she is the fastest runner on the Bain Harbour waterfront. She is nineteen, sharp, fast and impatient, with a quick tongue and quicker feet, and she likes to prove both. If a pickpocket runs on the docks, Brisa catches him. If someone says she cannot, she catches him faster.

### Why she joined

Brisa grew up on the Waterfront, the daughter of a fish-gutter and a carter, and spent her childhood running. She ran errands, ran races, ran away from the Fish Market whenever there was work to do there. Three years ago her parents moved inland to a cousin's farm near Kaldemor, where the work was steadier and the roads were said to be quieter. Brisa was sixteen. She refused to go, found a room above a sailmaker's loft, and stayed. Her parents write at harvest. She writes back at midwinter, briefly.

She joined the Harbour Watch last spring, not because she wanted to keep the peace, which she finds a little dull, but because it was the best way to see everything that happens in town. The Watch walks every pier and every alley, and it hears every story first, or at least second, after Finch the errand runner, who hears everything first and whom Brisa respects for it. Finch is also the only person on the waterfront who comes close to outrunning her. Twice since the night watch on the outer pier began, she has caught him trying to slip out along it in the dark, marched him back to the dock office, and threatened to tell Garrow the foreman. She has not told Garrow. She remembers what it was like to want to see everything.

### Jowan

Jowan joined the Watch at about the same time, and he is her friendly rival in all things. He is earnest, careful and far too proud of his coat; she is quick, careless and has never once worn hers buttoned properly. They compete over who reaches a disturbance first, who makes the catch, and whose report Halloran reads to the end. Brisa always arrives first. Jowan always writes more. She has never told him that his reports are better than hers, and she never will, but she has started reading them when he is not looking.

Halloran, their captain, settles most trouble with a hand on the shoulder and a mug of something hot, which Brisa thinks is far too slow and works far too well. She admires him and argues with him in roughly equal measure. Lately he has been standing a watch on the outer pier at night and will not say why, and it is driving her to distraction.

### The list

Since she joined, Brisa has kept a list of the strange things the sailors report: a light under the water, a hull scraped in water too deep for rocks, a net cut clean through, a seal that would not come near the rocks. She writes them in a sewn notebook she keeps inside her coat, with the date and the name of whoever told her, and there are more than sixty entries now. She does not know what they mean. She thinks someone important ought to read it one day, and she would very much like to be standing there when they do. She has no idea that Delphine at the Salt and Bread has been keeping a ledger of her own, or that Halloran has never thought to ask for hers.

### Hooks

A quest could take Brisa's list to someone who can make sense of it, race her along the waterfront for a wager, or help her find out, at last, what Halloran is watching for on the outer pier.
