    Urbanograd¶

    The Town of Societal Rebels

    Urbanograd is a city unlike any other on Veligor. It was founded 100 years ago, long after the four peoples had united, so its identity has nothing to do with the old wars. It grew up inside the kingdom, built by people who wanted to live their own way: free to express themselves and free of the expectations of ordinary society. Over the century since, it has grown a counterculture all its own.

    The Forming of Urbanograd¶

    A Kingdom That Had Settled Down¶

    A century ago, Wonder Kingdom had known peace for so long that peace had grown comfortable, and comfort had grown habits. In Selsior, the old guilds decided who could practice which trade, who could sell in which square and which songs could be sung in the theatres. Families expected their children to follow the trade they were born into. Even Kaldemor, for all its warmth, had firm ideas about what a good, honest life looked like.

    None of this was cruel. It was simply how things had always been done, and most people were happy with it. But a restless few were not: painters whose work the galleries called vulgar, musicians who played too loud and too fast, tinkerers whose inventions no guild would license, young people who wanted to dress, love and live in ways their elders did not understand. In the capital, they gathered in cellars, rooftops and back-alley taverns, and they began to talk about leaving.

    The Long Walk¶

    The spark was a small thing. When the theatre guild of Selsior banned a play by Juna Marrow, a young playwright whose comedy poked fun at the city's grandees, she performed it anyway, on a cart in the middle of the market square, to the biggest crowd the square had seen in years. The guild had her cart hauled away. That night, she told her friends she was done asking permission, and that she meant to build a town where nobody would ever have to.

    A few weeks later, about three hundred people walked out of Selsior's gates together: artists, musicians, craftspeople without a guild, inventors, wanderers, and plenty who simply wanted something different. Others joined them along the road from Kaldemor, the harbours and the highlands. They called themselves the Walkers, and the day they left is still celebrated in Urbanograd as Walkout Day.

    The Empty City¶

    The Walkers did not build Urbanograd from nothing. They settled in Old Grad, in the west of the island: the ruins of a walled stronghold built by the Aristocrats in the Age of Division and abandoned after the founding of the kingdom, when nobody needed fortresses anymore. For generations it had stood empty: crumbling towers, roofless halls and a maze of narrow streets slowly being taken back by weeds.

    The Walkers saw something else in it. They patched the roofs with whatever they could find, strung lanterns between the towers, painted every bare wall and turned the old barracks into workshops, theatres and homes. They added the name the city still carries: Urbanograd, roughly "the city of Grad", half a joke about the grand old fortress and half a promise of what it would become.

    The first winters were hard. The Walkers knew a great deal about art and very little about farming, and more than one of them went home. Those who stayed learned to trade their crafts, music and inventions for food, and travelers began to come just to see the strange new town.

    The Kingdom's Answer¶

    Not everyone was pleased. Some in Selsior called the Walkers deserters, and a few guild masters asked the crown to send them home. The question went all the way to the royal court.

    The answer came from the founding treaty itself. It promised that every people of Veligor could live together in peace, whatever their culture. The court ruled that the promise did not only protect the four peoples who had signed it, but any people who chose to live differently, so long as they kept the kingdom's peace and laws. Urbanograd was recognized as a town of Wonder Kingdom, with the same rights as any other.

    That ruling, known in Urbanograd as the Open Charter, is the reason the city exists as it does today. Its people have never forgotten that the kingdom chose to welcome them, and that is why their rebellion has always been about how they live, never about overturning the realm that let them.

    A Century of Urbanograd¶

    Over the next hundred years, Old Grad filled up. Each generation built on top of the last, so the city grew upward rather than outward: houses stacked on houses, bridges between rooftops, staircases that lead to three different places at once. New arrivals kept coming from every town on Veligor, bringing their own crafts and ideas and making the city ever more varied.

    Today Urbanograd is a busy, noisy, colorful town, famous for its music, its street art, its workshops and its night markets. Juna Marrow's play is performed in the main square every Walkout Day, on a cart, as it was the first time.

    Cultural Identity¶

    Urbanograd is famous as the home of societal rebels. Its people prize individuality, creativity, unconventional thinking, free expression and the right to live differently. The city draws anyone who loves alternative lifestyles and unusual art, music and fashion.

    The city has no lord or chieftain. Matters that concern everyone are talked out at the Open Table, a long gathering in the old fortress's great hall where anyone who lives in Urbanograd may speak. It is loud, slow and often funny, and its people would not have it any other way.

    But its people are not revolutionaries. They have no wish to unsettle the kingdom, topple its institutions or quarrel with their neighbors. Their rebellion is cultural and personal, not political.

    Relationship with Wonder Kingdom¶

    Urbanograd is a full and proud part of Wonder Kingdom. Its people respect the kingdom and get along well with every other community, and the rest of Veligor accepts the city's unusual character. Its people are understood, not distrusted. Even Selsior's guilds, which once wanted the Walkers brought home, now send their apprentices to Urbanograd to see what new things its workshops have come up with.

    Urbanograd shows that the kingdom's founding promise reaches further than peace between the original four peoples. Wonder Kingdom also welcomes communities that invent entirely new ways of living.

    The Lands Around Urbanograd¶

    Old Grad stands on its rise in the west of Veligor, and the city has only one way out: the old fortress gate at the end of the Gate Walk. Three roads leave from below it. The royal road runs east across the island to Selsior. Stonefang Pass climbs northeast into the red rock toward Midbarium. The Western Merchant Road, the Aristocrats' old road to the sea, runs southwest to Goa Harbour and is described on that town's page. Inside the walls there is nothing to fear. Outside them, the Gate Watch keeps count: Brindle chalks every sighting on the slate by the gate, and the numbers only go one way.

    The Gate Meadows¶

    All three roads begin in the same place: the open meadows below the gate, where Patch learned her herbs as a girl and carts pull up to wait for morning. A city that keeps building upward needs somewhere to keep its timber and stone, so half the meadows are stackyards: piles of beams and fortress stone, half-built scaffolds, rope cranes and sheds, all of it painted by somebody at some point. Creatures have learned to den among the stacks. Adders sun themselves on the warm stone, and the low, shining-eyed things Dagny sees from the gate at night come up out of the old ditches to hunt among the timber. The way into the Outworks opens from the meadows, where the old ditches run off along the foot of the walls.

    The Walkers' Road¶

    The royal road from Selsior is the way the Walkers came, a century ago, with their bundles and their cart, and Urbanograd has called it the Walkers' Road ever since. From the Gate Meadows it runs east past the edge of the Bristlewood to the Halfway Stone, a tall grey marker that the Walkers are said to have slept beside on their first night out of Selsior's sight. Urbanograd's builders keep a stone depot there, with a crane, a scaffold and a carters' shelter, and the crossroads is busy by day and noisy with creatures by night: rats in the stone stacks, things with too many arms in the drainage pools, adders in the verge. Beyond the stone the road runs on toward the central plain and the capital. Since the year after Rook's first letter to the queen, the Royal Guard's road patrols have ridden its whole length, all the way to the Gate Meadows.

    Stonefang Pass¶

    Stonefang Pass is the highland road. Wool carters bring it down from the hills, and Torvi came down it six years ago meaning to stay a week. From the Gate Meadows it bears northeast into the Seepwater Foothills, where springs seep out of the rock and turn the low ground to bog: reed beds, rotten boardwalks, mist in the mornings, and lumpish bog-lurchers that heave themselves out of the pools at anyone who leaves the planks. Beyond the foothills the pass climbs out of the wet into the Western Descent, a road of red rock ledges and switchbacks where dark, walking stumps root along the path and the axe-headed kind wait at the turns. At the top of the descent stand the needle-sharp spires that give the pass its name, and beyond them the pass comes out on Midbarium's western edge, where the highland families' own country begins.

    The Outworks¶

    The Aristocrats did not trust walls alone. Around the foot of Old Grad they dug ditches, raised earth banks and built small outer towers, and when they left, all of it fell into ruin. Urbanograd calls this ring of tumbled stone, brambles and half-buried cellars the Outworks. It is reached from the Gate Meadows and runs right round the city's outward side.

    For a hundred years the Outworks were a place for children's dares. This year they are where the trouble at the walls comes from. Lux finds outer lanterns smashed in the mornings, never on the same night twice. Dagny sees pairs of eyes low in the brush beyond the road. Both are true, and they are two different things. The eyes belong to gloomeyes, lizard-like creatures with great shining eyes that have crept out of the old ditches to hunt along the walls. The lanterns are the work of lampbats, a swarm that roosts in the flooded cellars under the ruin of the outermost tower, which Urbanograd calls the Fallen Tower, and that throws itself at any light on the walls.

    The swarm has a leader. The Gloamwing is a bat the size of a market awning, Disturbance-touched, that hunts every second night and drives the swarm before it. It hates noise, and that is what Drum half-noticed without being able to prove it: on nights the beat in Lantern Square is loudest, the lanterns survive.

    The story of the Outworks begins with Brindle, who needs someone to stand the night watch beside Dagny and find out what the eyes are. It runs through Lux's watch on the outer walls and a crate of new panes carried from Tinsel's furnace before the lanterns go dark, a basket of herbs for Patch from the meadows inside the old ditches, and Drum's rhythm, played for the first time outside the gate, to scatter the swarm. It ends under the Fallen Tower, with the Gloamwing. At the far, eastern end of the Outworks, the ditches give out among trees: the edge of the Bristlewood.

    The Bristlewood¶

    East of the city, along the Walkers' Road from within sight of the city's towers almost to the Halfway Stone, lie the wooded hills of the Bristlewood: old oak and hazel, steep stream valleys and boar wallows. The road's travelers have always heard boar in it. Now the boar come out of it, in herds, and charge the carts.

    Shade knows the Bristlewood's near edge well, and two of the routes Shade no longer trusts are there: the Silent Cutting, a wooded cutting where the birds have stopped singing, and the Tusk Path, a hill path where the tracks have grown too large. Deeper in, the streams spread into the Reedwater Bottoms, a marshy valley floor of reeds and black pools where the boar wallow and reed-lurkers lie in wait. At the edge of the bottoms, two days out from the city by cart, is the stream bank where Zip chased something large and tusked, fell, and broke her arm in two places last spring. At the top of that bank Torvi found tracks bigger than any boar's. Zip has named it Zip's Bank, and has asked everyone else to call it that too.

    The tracks belong to Hackleback, a boar heavier than a loaded wagon, so deeply touched by the Disturbance that its bristles have gone hard as iron. It has made its home in the Wallow, a deep mud hollow at the heart of the bottoms, and it drives the lesser boar out of the trees and toward the road ahead of it.

    This is the hunt that Hatch can no longer make alone. His story is a teacher's: first a test of whether the hero can read tracks at the wood's edge, then Shade's new route past the Tusk Path, then Hatch's own notes, recovered from the camp he had to abandon in the Bristlewood when his knees failed him there. Torvi goes back with the hero to Zip's Bank. Zip and Margo's spark lamp get their trial against real boar, and Zip brings the lamp back, as promised. Patch's rarest root, the one she cannot get anywhere else, is found in the Silent Cutting. Last comes the Wallow, and a hunt Hatch plans but cannot walk. Afterwards, Tick writes the fourth version of Zip's story, the first one that is true.

    The Beacon Downs¶

    Beyond the Bristlewood's far end the trees thin and the land climbs onto high, bare chalk downs that roll away east toward the middle of the island. Along their crest stands a chain of ruined towers: the beacon towers the Aristocrats built to pass fire signals between their strongholds in the Age of Division. Urbanograd calls the whole country the Beacon Downs, and the tallest tower, on the highest hill, the Highbeacon.

    The downs are the wild country Perch's pigeons refuse to cross. At dusk the sky above them belongs to the duskhawks, great hook-beaked hunters that strike anything on the wing, and the grass below belongs to downs howlers, shaggy horned beasts that bay at dusk, and to rams with horns like millstones. The routes the pigeons will not fly at any hour pass over the Highbeacon itself, where Stormcrest nests: a raptor with a wingspan wider than a city street, deeply touched by the Disturbance, that has taken the old tower for its eyrie.

    The Beacon Downs are also the nearest high ground from which to look east toward the middle of the island, and that brings in Starling. The story of the downs begins in Perch's loft, with a message the birds refuse to carry, which the hero walks over the downs on foot and gives to a bird released on the far side. It goes on through the dusk skies and the duskhawks, a new lens carried from Tinsel's furnace to Starling's roof through the evening crowds, and long nights on the downs. On one of them, from the ruins of a lower beacon, Starling and the hero watch the far horizon waver, just as Starling has seen it from the Rooftops, and find that it wavers far beyond the downs, further east than anyone can walk in a night. It is not the downs' doing. But on the nights it comes, the duskhawks are restless and hunt late, and that is why some of Starling's dates match Perch's list and some do not.

    The story ends at the Highbeacon with Stormcrest. Afterwards, the pigeons fly the dusk routes again, though not every route. The hero carries Starling's dates and Perch's list to Rook, who reads them twice, says nothing funny at all, and sends them to Queen Aveline in the next letter. What the flicker is, nobody in Urbanograd can say.

    Low Grad¶

    Old Grad was built as a fortress, and fortresses go down as well as up. Under the Great Hall, behind the kitchens, is an iron door that the Walkers found locked a century ago and never had a key for. Below it lie the old fortress's buried levels: the halls of the old keep, storerooms and vaults, flooded cisterns, the sally tunnels the garrison used to leave the fortress unseen, and the quarry the Aristocrats cut Old Grad's dark stone from. The city calls all of it Low Grad, and until this summer nobody had been down.

    It begins with Velvet's warm stone, and with Tick's notebook. Velvet bought the stone from a frightened wanderer off the deep roads. In his notebook, Tick has written down that same wanderer's story: he sheltered from a storm in a tunnel mouth in the hills, followed it a long way underground, and came out running, with a dark stone in his pocket that would not cool. Brindle hears the story and sees what it might mean: tunnels under the country that may run all the way to the fortress, and a way into the city that no gate guards. The Open Table argues the question for a whole night (Bastian argues both sides, and wins both), and then agrees to open the door. Hex, who learned locks before boxes, opens it, delighted by its Aristocrat lock.

    Low Grad goes down in four levels. The Undercroft holds the old keep's halls and the Aristocrats' storerooms, full of rot, dust and creeping slimes. The Floodwells are flooded halls where lurkers lie in the dark water. The Deep Roads are the sally tunnels, long and narrow, roosted by bats and running out under the hills toward the wanderer's tunnel mouth. Last comes the Warm Quarry, where the dark stone is warm to the touch and creatures of that same stone have stirred. The quarry's master is the Warmstone Colossus, a giant of dark stone and the deepest-touched thing beneath the city.

    The keeper of Low Grad is Leland, an old well-digger who knows more about what lies under the city's streets than anyone alive, and whom the Table has named Keeper of the Low Door. He keeps the door, the ropes and a chalk map of every level that grows a little each day, and he lets anyone with the nerve go down, as often as they like. At the end of the Deep Roads, the Watch has set an iron grate from Ferro's forge across the tunnel. A chip of the warm stone sits on Tinsel's windowsill with the other failed experiments, and it is still warm.

    Leadership Today¶

    Urbanograd still has no lord. Each year the Open Table chooses a Caller to convene it and keep order, and the Caller rules no one. This year's Caller, for the first time, is Rook, a chandler from Workshops Row whose great-grandparent was one of the Walkers. The Table chose Rook this spring because Rook hates making speeches and loves listening to them. Rook has also written to Queen Aveline every fortnight for nine years.

    Design notes

    The atmosphere is inspired by MapleStory's Kerning City. Juna Marrow, the Walkers, Old Grad, the Open Charter and the Open Table are new with this page. Urbanograd lies in the west of the island, as the game's world map has it. Still open: what the city looks like beyond its fortress core.

    The Lands Around Urbanograd are new with this section: the Gate Meadows and their stackyards, the Walkers' Road and the Halfway Stone, Stonefang Pass's Seepwater Foothills and Western Descent (the spires and the far canyon bridge belong to Midbarium's side), the Outworks, the Bristlewood, the Beacon Downs, Low Grad, their creatures and bosses, and Leland. Still open: what the horizon flicker is; why the stone of the Warm Quarry is warm, and whether Velvet's stone is the same; and what the wanderer saw underground. None of them is tied to the Balrog here. The bosses are Disturbance-touched creatures, nothing more. The Walkers' Road follows the Long Walk in spirit; exactly which way the Walkers went is not recorded.
